Australians who look at Paraguay usually come for the same reasons as everyone else: a second residency without a huge investment, a low cost of living and land that's affordable by Australian standards. What's different is the distance. Paraguay sits on the far side of the Pacific, in the middle of South America, and that affects the document chain, the presence rules and how often you'll realistically fly home.

## Getting in and the routes

Entry is easy for now: <Fact k="entry.visa_exemption_us_ca_au" />. You arrive as a visitor and file for residency in Asunción.

The standard path is temporary residency, which lasts <Fact k="temporary.duration" />, followed by permanent residency. For permanent status you document <Fact k="solvency.requirement" />. If you're bringing capital, the Investor Pass leads straight to permanent residency. [The three routes compared](/routes) lays out the options.

## Your document file is built in Australia

Almost all of the slow work happens before you board the plane:

- **Police check.** Usually the AFP National Police Check, of the type issued for immigration. It should be <Fact k="documents.police_certificate_validity" />.
- **Birth and marriage records.** From the state or territory registry that issued them, not the one where you live now.
- **Apostilles.** Australia and Paraguay are both in the Apostille Convention, and Paraguay is <Fact k="apostille.since" />. DFAT issues the apostille.
- **Translations.** Done in Paraguay, by a sworn translator the authorities recognize.

Couriers between Australia and Paraguay are slow, so order in the right sequence. The police check has a shelf life and should be the last thing you order. Our sister site has the deep dive: [apostilles and documents for Australian applicants](https://paraguayresidency.co.uk/guides/documents/apostille-and-documents-for-australian-applicants).

## The distance and the presence rules

There are no direct flights. You'll connect through Santiago, Buenos Aires or São Paulo, and door to door it's a long day or more. That's fine for a move. It matters more for a plan B you hold from Australia.

Temporary residency requires <Fact k="temporary.presence_rule" />. Permanent residency requires <Fact k="permanent.presence_rule" />. If you expect to live mostly in Australia, budget one long trip within each window and put the dates in your calendar. [The presence rules nobody explains](/stories/the-presence-rules-nobody-explains) goes through the details.

## Super, the Age Pension and Medicare

Three Australian systems change when you leave, and none of them are Paraguay's call:

- **Superannuation** generally stays preserved in Australia until you meet a release condition. Moving abroad doesn't unlock it for Australian citizens and permanent residents.
- **The Age Pension** can be paid abroad, but portability rules can reduce it after a period overseas, depending on your working-life residence in Australia. Check with Services Australia before you plan around it.
- **Medicare** generally stops covering you once you move away. Budget for private health insurance in Paraguay. [Healthcare and insurance in Paraguay](/stories/healthcare-and-insurance-in-paraguay) explains how Asunción and the interior differ.

## The Australian tax questions

Australia taxes its residents on worldwide income, and the ATO decides whether you're still resident using its own tests: where you live, your intentions, your ties. A Paraguayan card doesn't answer that by itself. When you stop being resident, capital gains rules can apply to some assets, with choices to make at the time.

Paraguay's side is simpler. Its rule is that <Fact k="tax.foreign_income_treatment" />, and income earned in Paraguay is taxed at <Fact k="tax.territorial_rate" />. Just don't expect a treaty to coordinate the two. Paraguay has <Fact k="tax.double_tax_treaties" />. Settle the Australian side with a registered tax agent before you go.

## Is Paraguay the right plan B from Australia?

It suits Australians who want a low-cost second base in South America, don't need to be close to home and are happy to learn Spanish. It suits less well if you want frequent trips back, English-speaking daily life or a residency that works across Asia.
