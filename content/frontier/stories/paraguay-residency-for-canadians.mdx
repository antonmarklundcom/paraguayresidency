Yes, Canadians can get Paraguay residency, and for most it's the same process an American or a European goes through. Two things are genuinely Canadian: a document folder spread across provinces, and a tax system that watches the day you leave. Get those two right and the rest is ordinary paperwork.

## Getting in and the routes

Entry is simple for now: <Fact k="entry.visa_exemption_us_ca_au" />. You enter as a visitor and file for residency in Asunción.

The standard path is temporary residency first, which lasts <Fact k="temporary.duration" />, and then permanent residency. For permanent status you document <Fact k="solvency.requirement" />. Canadians with capital can also look at the Investor Pass, which leads straight to permanent residency. [The three routes compared](/routes) sets them side by side, and the [Route Finder](/route-finder) points you to one in two minutes.

## The document chain, province by province

A Canadian file usually has a birth certificate from one province, a marriage certificate from another and a national police record. Canada is now part of the Apostille Convention, and Paraguay is <Fact k="apostille.since" />. But the competent authority for an apostille depends on the document and where it was issued. Some records go through a province, others through the federal route.

Three rules of thumb:

- **Map each record to its issuer** before you order a replacement. Where you live now doesn't change who signs your birth certificate.
- **Get the right police record.** A local employment check isn't the same as a national criminal record check. In Canada that usually means a fingerprint-based check through the RCMP. Confirm the exact form with us before you apply. The certificate should be <Fact k="documents.police_certificate_validity" />.
- **Translate in Paraguay.** A bilingual English–French certificate still needs a Spanish translation by a recognized sworn translator.

The full walk-through is in [Canadian records for Paraguay across provincial lines](/stories/canadian-document-chain-for-paraguay).

## Retiring on CPP and OAS

Canadians who retire to Paraguay usually live on CPP, OAS and private pensions. The Paraguay side is straightforward. A pension counts toward permanent residency, and Paraguay's rule is that <Fact k="tax.foreign_income_treatment" />.

The Canadian side needs checking:

- **CPP** is generally paid to beneficiaries living abroad.
- **OAS** can be paid outside Canada only if you meet its residence-history condition. If you don't, it stops after a period abroad.
- **Provincial health coverage** ends once you're away past your province's absence limit. Budget for private insurance in Paraguay from day one. See [healthcare and insurance](/stories/healthcare-and-insurance-in-paraguay).

Confirm your own pensions with Service Canada before you set a moving date.

## The Canadian tax questions

Canada taxes by residence, so the key question is whether you stop being a Canadian resident. The answer turns on the ties you keep: a home, a spouse or dependants in Canada, provincial health coverage, bank accounts and more. A Paraguayan card doesn't settle it.

Three points to raise with a Canadian cross-border tax professional:

- **Departure tax.** When you stop being resident, Canada generally treats you as having sold certain assets at market value on the day you leave.
- **Withholding on Canadian income.** Pensions, RRSP withdrawals and investment income paid to non-residents face Canadian withholding. There's no treaty to reduce it, because Paraguay has <Fact k="tax.double_tax_treaties" />.
- **Paraguayan income.** If you earn locally, Paraguay taxes it at <Fact k="tax.territorial_rate" />.

## Keep the card alive

Snowbirds and part-timers need the presence rules more than anyone. Temporary residency requires <Fact k="temporary.presence_rule" />. Permanent residency requires <Fact k="permanent.presence_rule" />. A winter in Paraguay every year or two covers it comfortably. Moving back to Canada and forgetting about the card doesn't. See [the presence rules nobody explains](/stories/the-presence-rules-nobody-explains).
