A lot of Paraguay marketing jumps straight from "easy residency" to "second passport." Those are two very different things. Residency is an administrative status you can get in months. Citizenship is a court process that comes years later, with requirements a pure plan-B holder usually can't meet.

Here is what naturalization actually takes, what Paraguay's Constitution says about dual nationality, and what the passport is worth once you have it.

## Does Paraguay allow dual citizenship?

On paper, only in limited cases. Paraguay's Constitution says <Fact k="citizenship.dual_nationality" />. Treaties of that kind exist with only a few countries, such as Spain. None exists with the United States, Canada or Australia.

For naturalized citizens the text is stricter: <Fact k="citizenship.naturalized_loss" />. That provision is about acquiring another nationality after you become Paraguayan. It doesn't by itself force you to give up the one you already hold, and Paraguay has no mechanism to strip your US, Canadian or Australian citizenship.

So the practical answer comes from your home country:

- **United States.** US law generally doesn't take away citizenship when you naturalize elsewhere, unless you intend to give it up. Renouncing is a separate, formal act at a US embassy.
- **Canada, Australia and the UK.** All three allow their citizens to hold another nationality.

Treat both points as the starting position, not legal advice. Confirm your own country's current rules with its government or an immigration lawyer before you file anything in Paraguay. And one thing a second passport never changes for Americans: US tax follows citizenship, not residence.

## How naturalization works, step by step

1. **Temporary residency.** It lasts <Fact k="temporary.duration" />. Time on temporary status doesn't count toward citizenship.
2. **Permanent residency.** The clock starts on the date of your permanent residency resolution. Keep that document. You'll cite it years later.
3. **The waiting period.** You need <Fact k="citizenship.years" />. Meanwhile, the presence rule still applies: <Fact k="permanent.presence_rule" />.
4. **The court filing.** Naturalization isn't a DNM process. It's handled by the Supreme Court, and the file needs <Fact k="citizenship.naturalization_requirements" />. Expect a document list much like your residency file: birth certificate, police records from Paraguay and your home country, your resident card, plus a CV and a sworn declaration.
5. **After approval.** You get a Paraguayan cédula as a citizen and can apply for a passport.

We don't publish a processing time for step four. It's a judicial review, not a fixed administrative timeline.

## The catch for plan-B holders

Read the requirements again with a plan B in mind. The Constitution asks for the regular exercise of a profession, trade, science, art or business in Paraguay. The court's document list turns that into paper: an employment certificate with your employer's RUC, a commercial license, a student enrolment or professional credentials.

That is the line most "second passport in a few years" pitches leave out. If your plan is to hold a residency card in reserve and visit once every year or two, you'll struggle to show that you work or do business in Paraguay. Citizenship is designed for people who actually built a life there.

There's a second catch after you succeed. A naturalized citizen who stays away without justification can lose the nationality: <Fact k="citizenship.naturalized_loss" />. A Paraguayan passport isn't a document you can bank and forget, any more than the residency card is.

## What a Paraguayan passport gets you

It's a solid mid-table passport. For a Paraguayan citizen:

- **Mercosur.** Easy entry across South America and residence rights in Argentina, Brazil, Uruguay and the associated countries on simplified terms. This is its real strength.
- **Europe.** Visa-free short stays in the Schengen area.
- **United Kingdom.** Visits with an electronic travel authorization instead of a visa.
- **United States, Canada and Australia.** A visa is required.

For an American, Canadian or Australian, that means the passport rarely opens a door your own passport doesn't already open. Its value is elsewhere: a second nationality if something goes wrong at home, and the right to settle across much of South America.

## Permanent residency vs citizenship

| | Permanent residency | Citizenship |
|---|---|---|
| Who decides | DNM (administrative) | Supreme Court (judicial) |
| What it gives you | The right to live in Paraguay indefinitely | Nationality and a passport |
| Main requirement | <Fact k="solvency.requirement" /> | <Fact k="citizenship.naturalization_requirements" /> |
| How you lose it | <Fact k="permanent.presence_rule" /> | <Fact k="citizenship.naturalized_loss" /> |
| Who needs it | Almost everyone with a plan B | People who actually moved and want full membership |

Most plan-B holders stop at permanent residency, and that's usually the right call. It already gives you a home you can return to, a cédula, a RUC and a bank account. See [cédula, then RUC](/stories/cedula-and-ruc-after-approval) for what that status unlocks.

## If a passport is your long-term goal, start the paper trail now

Naturalization rewards people who kept good records. If citizenship is on your list, even a distant one, do these things from the day you get permanent residency:

- **File the resolution safely.** The permanent residency resolution starts the clock, and the court asks for your resident card and a DNM residence certificate.
- **Keep your entries and exits clean.** Travel within the presence rule, and keep your boarding passes and passport stamps. A gap you can't explain is the ground for losing status later.
- **Build the local record.** Register a RUC, keep it active and file what it requires. A job contract with a Paraguayan employer, a registered business or a university enrolment is what the court file actually asks for.
- **Learn Spanish.** The whole process runs in Spanish, from the petition to any follow-up from the court.

None of this is wasted if you never file. The same records help with banking, a tax residency certificate and any renewal.

## Should you bother?

Naturalize if you've lived in Paraguay for years, you work or run a business there and you want full membership, passport included. Don't plan your whole move around it if you intend to live mostly elsewhere. For that pattern, a well-kept permanent residency does the job, and [the presence rules](/stories/the-presence-rules-nobody-explains) are what you need to master.

If a passport is your main goal, compare honestly. Some countries offer a faster or more predictable path than Paraguay's, and others ask even more of you.
