Most "plan B Paraguay cost" answers quote one number, the filing fee, and stop. That's like pricing a car by its registration. A plan B is something you hold for years, and most of the cost comes after the approval letter: converting to permanent status, renewing the card and, above all, the trips that keep it valid.

Here is the ten-year view. Paraguayan government fees come from our sourced facts. Our own fee and your flights depend on your case, so we don't quote them as figures.

## Stage one: getting the card

This is where people focus, and it's the most predictable part.

- **Documents at home.** Birth certificate, police certificate, apostilles and translations. For a do-it-yourself file, the all-in number is <Fact k="costs.diy_total" />.
- **Temporary residency filing.** The DNM fee is <Fact k="fees.temporary_residency" />.
- **Local certificates.** The Interpol department certificate is <Fact k="fees.interpol_certificate" />, and the domestic police certificate is <Fact k="fees.police_certificate_py" />.
- **The cédula.** The ID card is <Fact k="fees.cedula_first" />. Budget the time: <Fact k="cedula.timeline" />.
- **Our fee, if you use us.** <Fact k="pricing.temporary" />.
- **The trip.** You need to be in Asunción for the appointments. Plan the stay around them, with a buffer.

For what those first months look like day to day, read [an American's first months filing residency](/stories/american-first-90-days).

## Stage two: keeping temporary status alive

Temporary residency lasts <Fact k="temporary.duration" />. While you hold it, the rule is <Fact k="temporary.presence_rule" />. That usually means one extra trip if you don't live in Paraguay. If you'll miss the conversion window, extending instead costs <Fact k="fees.temporary_extension" />.

## Stage three: going permanent

Converting is a second filing, not a formality. The DNM fee is <Fact k="fees.permanent_residency" />. You'll also document <Fact k="solvency.requirement" />, which means current statements, a pension letter or business records, often apostilled again. Our fee for this step is <Fact k="pricing.permanent" />. And it's another trip.

## Stage four: the long, cheap middle

Once you're permanent, the rule is <Fact k="permanent.presence_rule" />. Over the rest of the decade, that's a few trips at minimum. Then the card itself: <Fact k="fees.permanent_card_renewal" />.

Two optional lines belong here too:

- **A RUC and an accountant.** Many residents register a RUC for banking. It can come with filings even in years with no local income, so ask your accountant what yours needs and what a year of upkeep costs. Paraguay's tax rule is that <Fact k="tax.foreign_income_treatment" />, but the paperwork can still exist. See [cédula, then RUC](/stories/cedula-and-ruc-after-approval).
- **A tax residency certificate.** If you want one, it requires <Fact k="tax.residency_certificate" />. That usually means more time in the country, not less.

## Why the fees in old articles are wrong

Paraguay's government fees are <Fact k="fees.basis" />. Every minimum-wage adjustment moves every fee, which is why a fee table from a couple of years ago is out of date. The exchange rate also moves. Today it's <Fact k="fx.reference_rate" />, and the guaraní has strengthened, so dollar conversions in older content look too low.

## The most expensive mistake

Letting the card lapse. If you break the presence rule, the status can be cancelled. Starting over means paying for the whole document chain again: new police certificates, new apostilles, new translations and new filings. One well-timed trip is always cheaper.
