Plan B stories tend to leave out the awkward question: what if your record is not clean? Some people want a second base precisely because of an old mistake. This page explains what Paraguayan law says, how it plays out for North American and Australian applicants, and what a sensible order looks like. It is general guidance, not legal advice, and the decision belongs to the migration office.

## What the law says

The rule sits in the migration law, Ley 6984/2022. For both temporary and permanent residency, <Fact k="residency.criminal_record_refusal" />.

Read it slowly:

- **May refuse.** A power, not a duty. Two people with similar records can get different answers.
- **Prosecuted or convicted.** A pending case counts.
- **Under Paraguayan law.** The offence is measured by what it would carry in Paraguay, not the sentence you got at home.
- **Intentional.** The clause covers intentional offences; negligent ones sit outside it, though the office still sees them.

Repeat offending is its own ground.

## What your file will contain

Every applicant provides a home-country police certificate, which has to be <Fact k="documents.police_certificate_validity" />, plus Paraguayan checks during the process: an Interpol certificate (<Fact k="fees.interpol_certificate" />) and, later, a local police record for the cédula. The myths about what "Interpol" shows are cleared up in [the Interpol certificate explained](/stories/interpol-and-the-police-certificate-myth).

For Americans, Canadians and Australians, the home certificate comes from different agencies with different disclosure rules, and what appears on one may not appear on another. Request the one Paraguay accepts and read it before you plan anything else.

## How cases tend to read

General guidance only.

| Situation | How the law frames it | Sensible move |
|---|---|---|
| Clean certificate | Nothing to weigh | Normal application |
| One old, minor matter | Likely outside the clause | Get the court record, talk to us first |
| Serious intentional offence | Likely inside the clause | Legal advice before fees |
| Case still open | Being prosecuted is enough | Wait for the outcome |
| Several offences | Repeat offending is a ground | Legal advice first |

## Do not hide it

You cannot edit an official certificate, and a residency obtained with false or misleading documents can be cancelled at any time. If citizenship is ever part of your plan, good conduct is a condition; read [citizenship and a second passport](/stories/paraguay-citizenship-and-a-second-passport).

## A sensible order

1. Request your certificate before ordering anything else.
2. If something appears, get the court paperwork.
3. Speak to us or a lawyer before paying Paraguayan fees.
4. If the answer is "not yet", ask what would change it.
