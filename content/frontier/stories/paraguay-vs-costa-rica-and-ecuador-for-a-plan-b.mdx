Costa Rica and Ecuador are the two names that come up most often after Paraguay in plan-B conversations, especially among Americans. All three have residency routes built for people with outside income. They differ in what you have to prove, what it costs to keep the card and what they tax. Here is the plain comparison, with the catch in each.

We only quote figures for Paraguay, from our sourced facts. Costa Rica's and Ecuador's thresholds change and are set by their own governments, so check their official sites for current numbers.

## The comparison at a glance

| | Paraguay | Costa Rica | Ecuador |
|---|---|---|---|
| Entry bar | No investment, no large fixed income threshold | Fixed monthly pension or passive income (pensionado, rentista) | Fixed monthly income (retiree, rentier) or an investment |
| Proof for permanence | <Fact k="solvency.requirement" /> | Renewals with fresh proof of income | Time on temporary status, then conversion |
| Presence rule | <Fact k="permanent.presence_rule" /> | A visit each year to keep status | Strict absence limits in the early temporary years |
| Foreign income | <Fact k="tax.foreign_income_treatment" /> | Territorial for now, reform debated | Worldwide, for tax residents |
| Health system | Private insurance your choice | Mandatory monthly social security (CCSS) | Health insurance required |
| Currency | Guaraní | Colón | US dollar |
| English | Limited outside business circles | Widespread in tourist areas | Limited outside expat towns |

## Where Paraguay wins

**Access.** There's no investment on the standard route and no large fixed income bar. The old deposit is <Fact k="solvency.deposit_abolished" />. For permanent residency you document real income or assets instead of hitting a number.

**Upkeep.** Permanent residency requires <Fact k="permanent.presence_rule" />. For a card you hold in reserve, that's the lightest rule of the three. [The presence rules nobody explains](/stories/the-presence-rules-nobody-explains) has the details.

**Tax design.** Paraguay's rule is that <Fact k="tax.foreign_income_treatment" />. Local income is taxed at <Fact k="tax.territorial_rate" />.

**The catch:** Paraguay is the least polished of the three. Infrastructure outside Asunción is basic, English is limited, and private healthcare thins out quickly beyond the capital.

## Where Costa Rica wins

Costa Rica has the strongest tourism infrastructure, the most English, a large expat community and outstanding nature. Its tax system is also territorial for now, although reform proposals come up regularly.

**The catch:** it's the most expensive of the three, and the card costs more to keep. Pensionado and rentista residents must prove a fixed monthly income, renew periodically with fresh proof and pay into the national social security system (CCSS) every month. The route to citizenship is also longer than Paraguay's.

## Where Ecuador wins

Ecuador runs on the US dollar, so Americans carry no currency risk. Costs are low, especially in the highland cities, and there's an established retiree community.

**The catch:** Ecuador taxes its tax residents on worldwide income, which is a very different design from Paraguay's. Its temporary residency also limits how long you can be out of the country each year in the early years, so it's a poor fit for a card held in reserve. Health insurance is mandatory for residents.

## Healthcare and daily life

Healthcare is where the three feel most different day to day. Costa Rica builds residents into its public system through the CCSS, and it has well-regarded private hospitals on top. Ecuador requires residents to carry health insurance, public or private, and its larger cities have good private clinics. Paraguay leaves the choice to you. Private care in Asunción is solid and inexpensive, and outside the capital it thins out quickly. See [healthcare and insurance in Paraguay](/stories/healthcare-and-insurance-in-paraguay).

Daily life follows the same pattern. Costa Rica is the easiest landing for someone who doesn't speak Spanish. Ecuador and Paraguay both expect Spanish outside a few expat pockets. Paraguay also has the smallest North American expat community of the three, which some people love and others find isolating.

## Which one fits your plan B?

- **A card you rarely use:** Paraguay. The presence rules are the lightest, and there's no income threshold to re-prove.
- **A comfortable, English-friendly retirement:** Costa Rica, if the income threshold and CCSS payments fit your budget.
- **A dollar-based, low-cost move:** Ecuador, if you'll really live there and have checked what worldwide taxation means for you.
- **A second passport:** none of the three is quick. See [Paraguay citizenship and a second passport](/stories/paraguay-citizenship-and-a-second-passport).

Whichever you pick, your home country's tax rules apply on top. Americans stay taxable on worldwide income everywhere. Talk to a tax professional in your own country.
