If you have spent any time in plan-B forums, you have seen someone ask whether Paraguay requires "an Interpol check," usually followed by three contradictory answers. The confusion is real, and it is worth untangling before it costs you a wasted document request.

## Two separate documents share one name

**What you bring from home:** a police or background certificate from your own country of nationality, the same certificate our [stories hub](/stories) walks through for US and Canadian applicants. In several countries, the office that issues it for "use abroad" purposes runs through the national Interpol bureau, which is where the phrase often comes from. That is still your own country's certificate, not a document issued by Interpol as an international body.

**What you get in Paraguay:** during the residency process you also obtain a background certificate from the Interpol department of the Paraguayan national police. It is a real, separate document with a government fee: <Fact k="fees.interpol_certificate" site="frontier" />. Pay only the official amount, and only for the document your file actually needs.

Immigration also screens applicants against international law-enforcement records on its own side. You do not arrange that step and it does not produce a document for you.

## Why this matters for a plan-B applicant specifically

People weighing a second residency tend to research several countries' immigration requirements at once, and terminology gets carried across borders where it does not apply the same way. A certificate that satisfies one country's "police clearance for immigration" category is not automatically the version another country expects, and the word "Interpol" attached to either does not settle the question.
