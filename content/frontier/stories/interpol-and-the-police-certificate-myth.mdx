If you have spent any time in plan-B forums, you have seen someone ask whether Paraguay requires "an Interpol check," usually followed by three contradictory answers. The confusion is real, and it is worth untangling before it costs you a wasted document request.

## Two separate things share one name

**What immigration does internally:** Paraguay screens applicants against international law-enforcement records as one part of reviewing a residency file. This happens on the authority's side. You do not arrange it, pay for it, or receive a certificate for it.

**What you actually provide:** a police or background certificate from your own country of nationality — the same certificate our [stories hub](/stories) walks through for US and Canadian applicants specifically. In several countries, the office that issues that certificate for "use abroad" purposes is administered through the national Interpol bureau, which is almost certainly where the phrase came from. That administrative detail does not change what you are sending: your own country's police certificate, not a document issued by Interpol as an international body.

## Why this matters for a plan-B applicant specifically

People weighing a second residency tend to research several countries' immigration requirements at once, and terminology gets carried across borders where it does not apply the same way. A certificate that satisfies one country's "police clearance for immigration" category is not automatically the version another country expects, and the word "Interpol" attached to either does not settle the question.
