"Where should I live?" is the question that follows "can I get residency?" The honest answer is that it depends on what you are optimising for, and that your first base should be chosen by your paperwork rather than by a brochure. This page describes the main options in plain terms. It is not a ranking, and prices change, so treat any figure as a starting point.

## Start where the paperwork is

The migration office, the ID office, the banks that handle new residents and the main hospitals are in and around Asunción. That is why most Plan B residents spend their first weeks there, even if they intend to live elsewhere. The steps are in [an American's first months](/stories/american-first-90-days), and the same order applies to Canadians and Australians.

## The main options

| Place | What it is like | Best for | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|---|
| Asunción and suburbs | Capital, most services, airport | First base, families, remote workers | Busier and pricier |
| Encarnación | River city on the Argentine border | People who want water and a slower pace | Further from the capital |
| Ciudad del Este | Trading city on the Brazilian border | Business and cross-border life | Busy, commercial |
| Smaller towns | Quiet, cheaper | Retirees, land buyers | Thin services, slower internet |

## What to check before you choose

- **Healthcare:** where the hospitals are, and what your insurance covers. See [healthcare and insurance](/stories/healthcare-and-insurance-in-paraguay).
- **Internet and power:** test both if you work remotely.
- **Distance to Asunción:** every renewal and many procedures pull you back.
- **Land:** if you want acreage, read [land and farms as a foreigner](/stories/land-and-farms-as-a-foreigner) first, because border areas carry restrictions.

## A sensible way to choose

1. Live in Asunción through your filing and cédula.
2. Rent short-term in a second place for a month.
3. Decide with a full season of experience.
4. Sign long leases or buy only after that.
