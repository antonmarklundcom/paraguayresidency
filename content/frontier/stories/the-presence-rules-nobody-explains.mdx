If there is one detail that separates a genuinely useful Paraguay plan-B residency from
a disappointing one, it is the presence requirement — and it is also the detail most
"easiest residency in the world" content either skips entirely or states incorrectly.
We would rather lose a click to a more exciting headline than let a client find this out
the hard way after filing.

## Why this gets misreported

Paraguay's residency programs are genuinely more accessible than many alternatives, and
that accessibility is real. But "accessible to obtain" and "requires no ongoing
attention" are different claims, and content optimized for a dramatic headline tends to
blur them together. The truth sits in the middle: getting the card is genuinely easier
than in most comparable countries, and keeping it valid still requires understanding a
real rule, not none at all.

## What the rule actually covers

Permanent residency in Paraguay carries <Fact k="permanent.presence_rule" />. This is
the detail that matters most for anyone planning to hold the card in reserve rather than
live here full time — it is the difference between a residency that quietly stays valid
in the background and one that lapses without you noticing until you need it.

Temporary residency, which runs <Fact k="temporary.duration" /> before permanent status
becomes available, has its own separate expectations around how the initial period
functions. Because the specifics affect real decisions — how often you plan trips,
whether the card still serves its purpose as a rarely-used reserve — we walk through
exactly what applies to your case on a call rather than compressing it into a single
paragraph that fits every situation identically.

## Why we lead with this rather than bury it

Plenty of sites in this space would rather not mention a presence requirement at all,
because it complicates the pitch. We think that is exactly backwards: a client who
understands the actual commitment before filing ends up satisfied with what they got. A
client who was sold "get it and forget it" and then finds out otherwise is not, and that
is a worse outcome for everyone, including us.

## How this should change your planning

If minimal ongoing presence is the whole point of your plan B, the presence rule is not
a footnote — it is the single fact that determines whether the strategy works for you at
all. It affects how you think about travel planning, whether permanent or temporary
status better fits your intended pattern, and whether a status held purely in reserve
makes sense compared to one you use more actively.

This is also where a route that looks identical to two different applicants can turn out
to fit one and not the other. Someone planning quarterly visits and someone planning to
visit once every few years are, in practice, planning for two different outcomes, even
though both start from the same application.
