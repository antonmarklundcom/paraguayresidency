For years the question was "how much do I have to deposit?" That deposit no longer exists, and the replacement is more flexible but also more particular. This page explains how to show means for Paraguay residency, whether your money comes from a pension, savings, rent or a laptop.

## What the rule is now

<Fact k="solvency.requirement" />. And on the old rule: <Fact k="solvency.deposit_abolished" />.

The shift matters. Instead of proving you can park a sum, you prove that your income or assets are real and checkable.

## Matching you to a category

| Your situation | What tends to help |
|---|---|
| Pension or Social Security | Award letter and statements showing regular deposits |
| Employed remotely | Employer letter, contract and pay statements |
| Contractor or business owner | Contracts, invoices, company records and statements |
| Rental or investment income | Leases or holdings statements and bank records |
| Savings | Account and asset statements, with their source explained |

These are typical documents, not a promise; the category decides what a reviewer wants. Retirees can read [retiring on Social Security](/stories/retiring-in-paraguay-on-social-security) and remote workers [working remotely as an American](/stories/working-remotely-from-paraguay-as-an-american).

## Making the evidence checkable

A reviewer should be able to trace each number to a source in one step:

1. **Say where the money comes from** with a letter or contract.
2. **Show it arriving** with statements.
3. **Keep the dates current**; stale statements slow the file.
4. **Legalise foreign papers**: apostille, then translation. The US chain is in [the US document chain](/stories/us-document-chain-before-paraguay).

## Common mistakes

- Gathering statements before choosing a category.
- Relying on a single bank statement.
- Mixing sources without explaining them.
- Ordering apostilles too early, so they age out.
