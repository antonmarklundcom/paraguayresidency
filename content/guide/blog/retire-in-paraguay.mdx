Retirees make up a real share of the people researching Paraguay residency, and their priorities are genuinely different from a working-age applicant's — less about tax optimization for active income, more about cost of living, healthcare access, and a comfortable, low-stress pace of daily life.

## What actually matters most if you're retiring here?

**Cost of living.** A single person's baseline runs {{fact:costofliving.overview}} before rent — see the [real cost of living breakdown](/blog/real-cost-of-living-in-paraguay) for the detail. For a fixed-income retiree, this is often the single biggest driver of the whole decision.

**Healthcare.** Private healthcare in Asunción covers most needs well by regional standards. The important caveat for retirees specifically: quality and specialist availability drop meaningfully outside the capital, and this matters more the more ongoing medical care you anticipate needing. Confirm private health insurance options and costs directly — see [our health insurance guide](/blog/health-insurance-paraguay).

**The presence requirement.** Permanent residency comes with {{fact:permanent.presence_rule}}, which is genuinely important to retirees who plan to split time between Paraguay and a home country to stay near family. Read this rule carefully before assuming you can be away as long as you like.

**Pace of life.** Qualitative, but it's the reason many retirees give for choosing Paraguay over louder or more touristy alternatives — a slower, quieter daily rhythm, particularly outside Asunción's busier core.

## Does Paraguay have a specific retiree residency category?

Under the current permanent-residency framework, retirees are one of the recognised applicant categories for proof of means — alongside employees, the self-employed, property owners and others. That means a pension statement can satisfy the documentation requirement, but it needs to match what the category expects; confirm the current documentation with your case handler rather than assuming a general pension letter is automatically enough.

## Who this suits, and who it doesn't

| Suits well | Suits poorly |
|---|---|
| Wants low costs and a calm pace | Needs proximity to world-class specialist medical care outside a capital |
| Comfortable planning trips home around the presence rule | Wants a large existing English-speaking retiree community as a hard requirement |
| Has a documented pension or other qualifying income | Expects to rely on savings alone without documentation matching a category |

This page is informational, not financial or medical advice — talk to your own financial advisor about pension and tax implications of relocating, and to a healthcare provider about your specific ongoing medical needs before deciding.
