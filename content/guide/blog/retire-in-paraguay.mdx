Retirees make up a real share of the people researching Paraguay residency,
and their priorities are genuinely different from a working-age applicant's —
less about tax optimization for active income, more about cost of living,
healthcare access, and a comfortable, low-stress pace of daily life.

## What actually matters if you're retiring here

**Cost of living.** Paraguay is consistently cheaper than most alternatives
retirees compare it against, particularly for day-to-day expenses — see the
[real cost of living breakdown](/blog/real-cost-of-living-in-paraguay) for the
detail. For a fixed-income retiree, this is often the single biggest driver
of the whole decision.

**Healthcare.** Private healthcare in Asunción covers most needs well by
regional standards. The important caveat for retirees specifically: quality
and specialist availability drop meaningfully outside the capital, and this
matters more the more ongoing medical care you anticipate needing as you age.
Confirm private health insurance options and costs directly before relying on
general reassurance on this point — see [our health insurance
guide](/blog/health-insurance-paraguay).

**The presence requirement.** Permanent residency carries
<Fact k="permanent.presence_rule" />, which is genuinely important to
retirees who plan to split time between Paraguay and a home country to stay
near family. Read this rule carefully before assuming you can be away as long
as you like.

**Pace of life.** This is qualitative, but it's the reason many retirees give
for choosing Paraguay over louder or more touristy alternatives — a slower,
quieter daily rhythm, particularly outside Asunción's busier core.

## Who this suits, and who it doesn't

Paraguay tends to suit retirees who want low costs and a calm pace and are
comfortable being somewhat off the beaten path compared to larger expat-heavy
retirement destinations. It suits people less well if proximity to
world-class specialist medical care outside a capital city, or a large
existing English-speaking retiree community, is a hard requirement.

This page is informational, not financial or medical advice — talk to your
own financial advisor about pension and tax implications of relocating, and
to a healthcare provider about your specific ongoing medical needs before
deciding.
