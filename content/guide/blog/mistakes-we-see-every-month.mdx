We see the same mistakes repeat, month after month, across people who have never spoken to each other. That's useful information: if the same handful of errors keep happening independently, they're predictable, and predictable mistakes are avoidable.

## 1. Apostilling the wrong document, or from the wrong authority

An apostille has to come from the correct authority in the country that issued the original document — not wherever is convenient, and not a Paraguayan consulate. People occasionally apostille a photocopy instead of an original, or the wrong supporting document, because a generic checklist didn't specify their nationality's exact requirement. This is caught late, and fixing it means restarting that document's process.

## 2. Assuming one country's document timeline applies to everyone

Background-check and document-issuance timelines vary enormously by country of origin. Someone who reads a forum post from a different nationality and assumes the same window will apply to their own case has, more than once, arrived in Paraguay with an incomplete file and no way to speed up a foreign government office remotely.

## 3. Treating temporary residency's presence rule as optional

Temporary residency isn't presence-free either: {{fact:temporary.presence_rule}}. Some people file it and then largely ignore Paraguay until they think about upgrading to permanent, and are surprised when that gap has already put their status at risk.

## 4. Not planning for the permanent-residency presence rule early

{{fact:permanent.presence_rule}}. This is the single most misquoted rule in this niche — people repeat an old or wrong version of it online with total confidence. Get the current version confirmed for your specific case rather than trusting a comment thread.

## 5. Banking before the cédula is ready

Cédula issuance takes {{fact:cedula.timeline}}, and trying to force full banking access before that step is resolved leads to repeat branch visits and, occasionally, a bank simply declining until the cédula is in hand. See the [banking article](/blog/opening-a-bank-account-in-paraguay) for the actual sequence.

## 6. Assuming "territorial tax" means "no tax"

Paraguay's system means {{fact:tax.foreign_income_treatment}} — a real and meaningful feature, and one routinely oversimplified into "tax-free," which it is not. What it means for your specific income and your home country's own rules is a conversation with an accountant, not a one-line summary from a blog comment.

## 7. Picking the wrong route from the start

Some applicants spend months preparing documents for permanent residency when temporary residency ({{fact:temporary.duration}}) would have served just as well and faster — or underestimate that the Investor Pass ({{fact:investorpass.min_investment_usd}}) was a better fit for their capital and timeline. This single decision affects almost everything downstream, which is exactly why the [Route Finder](/route-finder) exists: two minutes, before you commit months to the wrong path.

## 8. Waiting to ask questions until after something has gone wrong

The cheapest fix for almost every mistake on this list is asking before you file, not after.

## Where these mistakes actually cost you

| Mistake | Typical consequence |
|---|---|
| Wrong apostille authority | Redo that document entirely — weeks lost |
| Ignoring the presence rule | Status cancellation, not just delay |
| Banking before cédula | Repeat branch visits, lower limits |
| Wrong route from the start | Months of misdirected document preparation |
