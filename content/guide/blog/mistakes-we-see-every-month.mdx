We see the same mistakes repeat, month after month, across people who have never spoken to each
other. That is useful information: if the same handful of errors keep happening independently,
they are predictable, and predictable mistakes are avoidable.

## 1. Apostilling documents in the wrong order, or the wrong document entirely

An apostille has to be obtained in the country that issued the original document, following
that country's specific process — not wherever is convenient. People occasionally apostille a
photocopy instead of an original, or apostille the wrong supporting document because a generic
online checklist did not specify their nationality's exact requirement. This is caught late, and
fixing it means starting that document's process over.

## 2. Assuming one country's timeline applies to everyone

Background-check and document-issuance timelines vary enormously by country of origin. Someone
who read a forum post from a different nationality and assumed the same weeks-long window would
apply to their own case has, more than once, arrived in Paraguay with an incomplete file and no
way to speed up a foreign government office remotely.

## 3. Treating the temporary stage as a formality

<Fact k="temporary.duration" /> is real, functioning residency with its own presence
expectations, not a waiting room. Some people file it and then largely ignore Paraguay until
they think about upgrading to permanent, and are surprised when that gap affects their case.

## 4. Not planning for the presence requirement early

<Fact k="permanent.presence_rule" /> matters for permanent residency, and it is the single most
misquoted rule in this niche — people repeat an old or wrong version of it to each other online
with total confidence. Get the current version confirmed for your specific case rather than
trusting a comment thread.

## 5. Banking before the cédula is ready

<Fact k="cedula.timeline" />, and trying to force full banking access before that step is
resolved leads to repeat branch visits and, occasionally, a bank simply declining until the
cédula is in hand. See the [banking article](/blog/opening-a-bank-account-in-paraguay) for the
actual sequence.

## 6. Assuming "territorial tax" means "no tax"

<Fact k="tax.territorial_rate" /> is a real and meaningful feature of Paraguay's system, and it
is also routinely oversimplified into "tax-free," which it is not. What it means for your
specific income and your home country's own tax rules is a conversation with an accountant, not
a one-line summary from a blog comment.

## 7. Picking the wrong route from the start

Some applicants spend months preparing documents for permanent residency when their situation
and goals would have been served just as well, and faster, by starting with the temporary route
first — or the reverse, underestimating that an Investor Pass route was available and a better
fit for their capital and timeline. This single decision affects almost everything downstream,
which is exactly why the [Route Finder](/route-finder) exists: two minutes, before you commit
months to the wrong path.

## 8. Waiting to ask questions until after something has already gone wrong

The cheapest fix for almost every mistake on this list is asking before you file, not after.
None of these errors are exotic — they repeat because they are the natural result of good-faith
guesses filling gaps that a straight answer would have closed in five minutes.

## The pattern underneath all of it

Every mistake here comes from the same root cause: treating Paraguay residency as a single
generic process rather than a nationality-specific, document-specific sequence with a real
order of operations. That is the entire reason this guide exists chapter by chapter instead of
as one general overview — see [what's inside](/#inside) for the full structure.
