Education is one of the first practical questions families ask once the
residency question is settled, and Paraguay's options are narrower than in a
large country but genuinely workable for most family situations — provided
you plan around language and location rather than assuming it'll sort itself
out after arrival.

## The three general options

**Public schools** are free and open to residents, but instruction is in
Spanish (with Guaraní also present in the curriculum). This works well for
families planning a longer-term stay who want their children to integrate
and become fluent quickly, and less well for a short-term stay where language
immersion isn't the goal.

**Private bilingual schools** exist mainly in Asunción and a few other larger
cities, offering a mix of Spanish and English (or another language)
instruction. These are a common middle-ground choice for expat families who
want some local integration without a full language jump on day one.

**International schools** follow a foreign curriculum (commonly American,
British, or an IB-style program) largely or entirely in English. These are
concentrated in Asunción, cost more than the alternatives, and suit families
prioritizing curriculum continuity — especially if a return move or a
transfer to another international posting is part of the family's plan.

## What to check before choosing

- **Location relative to where you'll actually live.** School options thin
  out quickly outside Asunción — factor commute distance into where you
  choose to settle, not just into which school to pick afterward.
- **Enrollment timing and any entrance requirements**, which vary school by
  school and are worth confirming directly with each school rather than
  assuming a single national process applies.
- **Language transition support**, if your children aren't yet fluent in
  Spanish — ask specifically how the school handles this, since answers vary
  widely between schools that advertise as "bilingual."

## The honest trade-off

If schooling quality and variety are a top priority and you have young
children, this is a real factor to weigh into the broader decision — see
[is Paraguay residency worth it for your situation](/blog/is-paraguay-residency-worth-it)
for the wider picture. Families who prioritize cost of living and pace of
life over school variety tend to be the ones who report being happiest with
the trade-off; families expecting big-city international-school density
outside Asunción tend to be disappointed.
