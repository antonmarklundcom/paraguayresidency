Education is one of the first practical questions families ask once the residency question is settled, and Paraguay's options are narrower than in a large country but genuinely workable for most family situations — provided you plan around language and location rather than assuming it'll sort itself out after arrival.

## What are the three general schooling options?

| Option | Language | Cost | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Public schools | Spanish (Guaraní also present) | Free | Longer-term stays; children integrating and becoming fluent |
| Private bilingual schools | Spanish + English (or another language) | Mid-range | Expat families wanting local integration without a full language jump |
| International schools | Largely or entirely English | Highest | Families prioritising curriculum continuity, a return move, or another international posting |

**Public schools** are free and open to residents, but instruction is in Spanish, with Guaraní also present in the curriculum. This works well for families planning a longer-term stay who want their children to integrate and become fluent quickly, and less well for a short stay where language immersion isn't the goal.

**Private bilingual schools** exist mainly in Asunción and a few other larger cities. These are a common middle-ground choice for expat families wanting some local integration without a full language jump on day one.

**International schools** follow a foreign curriculum (commonly American, British, or an IB-style program) largely or entirely in English. These are concentrated in Asunción, cost more than the alternatives, and suit families prioritising curriculum continuity.

## What should we check before choosing a school?

- **Location relative to where you'll actually live.** Options thin out quickly outside Asunción — factor commute distance into where you choose to settle, not just which school to pick afterward.
- **Enrollment timing and entrance requirements**, which vary school by school — confirm directly with each school rather than assuming a single national process applies.
- **Language transition support**, if your children aren't yet fluent in Spanish — ask specifically how the school handles this, since answers vary widely between schools that advertise as "bilingual."

## How does this affect where we should settle?

If schooling quality and variety are a top priority and you have young children, this is a real factor to weigh into the broader decision — see [is Paraguay residency worth it for your situation](/blog/is-paraguay-residency-worth-it) for the wider picture. Families who prioritise cost of living and pace of life over school variety tend to report being happiest with the trade-off; families expecting big-city international-school density outside Asunción tend to be disappointed.
