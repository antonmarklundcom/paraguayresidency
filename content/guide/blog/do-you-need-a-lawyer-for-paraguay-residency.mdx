We run a service that files these cases for a living, which makes us a biased source on this
question — so here is the honest answer rather than the self-serving one.

## The case for doing it yourself

Standard temporary residency, for a straightforward case — one applicant, one nationality with
well-understood document requirements, no unusual income situation, no prior legal complications
in Paraguay or elsewhere — is genuinely filable without a lawyer. The document list is knowable
(see the [full checklist](/blog/documents-you-need-for-paraguay-residency)), the process is
knowable, and the main risk is the ordinary sequencing mistakes covered in the
[mistakes article](/blog/mistakes-we-see-every-month) — not something that requires legal
judgment to avoid.

If your case fits that description and you have the time to manage the apostille and
translation steps carefully, doing it yourself is a reasonable choice, and this guide is
written to make that genuinely possible rather than being a funnel back to hiring us regardless.

## The case for professional help

A few situations change the calculation:

- **Anything involving a prior legal or immigration complication** — a previous visa denial
  anywhere, a legal name change with incomplete records, a criminal record that needs
  explaining rather than just disclosing. This is where local judgment about how an office will
  actually interpret your file matters more than a checklist can capture.
- **The Investor Pass and other investment-based routes**, where the investment itself needs
  structuring correctly and <Fact k="investorpass.min_investment_usd" /> — getting the
  investment vehicle wrong is a materially more expensive mistake than a document delay.
- **Family cases with more than one dependent nationality**, where the document requirements
  compound rather than simply repeat.
- **Time-sensitive situations**, where a paid service's ability to expedite specific steps
  (courier speed, appointment scheduling, catching a document error before submission) is worth
  more to you than the money it costs.
- **Simply not having the time** to manage a multi-week document-preparation process carefully
  while also handling a job, a move and everything else. This is a completely legitimate reason
  on its own, independent of how complex your case actually is.

## What a lawyer or filing service actually buys you

Not a different outcome from the government — no service can guarantee approval or its timing,
and anyone who implies otherwise is not being straight with you. What it buys is: someone who
has seen your specific document combination before and knows the local interpretation of
edge cases, someone managing the sequencing so a stalled step gets caught early rather than
discovered at filing, and time back that you spend elsewhere. <Fact k="cedula.timeline" />
regardless of who files your case — professional help changes how smoothly you get there, not
the government's own clock.

## How to actually decide

Ask yourself three questions. Does your case have any of the complications listed above? Do you
have real time to manage document preparation carefully over several weeks? Is the cost of
professional help meaningfully more than the cost of your own time and the risk of a delay? If
your case is simple, you have the time, and the cost matters to you — file it yourself, using
this guide. If any of the complications apply, or your time is worth more than the fee, hiring
help is not a failure of independence, it is a normal tradeoff.
