We run a service that files these cases for a living, which makes us a biased source on this question — so here is the honest answer rather than the self-serving one.

## Can I really file Paraguay residency myself?

Yes, for a straightforward case: one applicant, one nationality with well-understood document requirements, no unusual income situation, and no prior legal complications in Paraguay or elsewhere. The document list is knowable (see the [full checklist](/blog/documents-you-need-for-paraguay-residency)), the process is knowable, and doing it yourself typically costs {{fact:costs.diy_total}} in government fees, translations and apostilles — a fraction of a filing service's fee. The main risk is the ordinary sequencing mistakes covered in the [mistakes article](/blog/mistakes-we-see-every-month), not something that requires legal judgment to avoid.

## When does professional help actually pay for itself?

| Situation | DIY risk | Why help earns its cost |
|---|---|---|
| Straightforward single applicant | Low | This guide covers it; DIY is reasonable |
| Prior visa denial, name change, or criminal record needing explanation | High | Local judgment on how an office interprets your file matters more than a checklist |
| Investor Pass ({{fact:investorpass.min_investment_usd}}) | High | A structuring mistake in the investment itself is far more expensive than a document delay |
| Family case with mixed nationalities | Medium | Document requirements compound rather than simply repeat |
| No time to manage a multi-week process | Medium | A completely legitimate reason on its own, independent of case complexity |

## What does a filing service actually buy you if the government's timeline doesn't change?

Not a different outcome from the government — no service can guarantee approval or its timing, and anyone who implies otherwise isn't being straight with you. DNM review still takes {{fact:residency.timeline}} regardless of who files. What professional help buys is someone who has seen your specific document combination before and knows the local interpretation of edge cases, someone managing the sequencing so a stalled step gets caught early rather than discovered at filing, and time back that you spend elsewhere. Cédula timing afterward is still {{fact:cedula.timeline}} either way — professional help changes how smoothly you get there, not the government's own clock.

## How do I actually decide?

Ask three questions. Does your case have any of the complications in the table above? Do you have real time to manage document preparation carefully over several weeks? Is the cost of professional help meaningfully more than the cost of your own time and the risk of a delay? If your case is simple, you have the time, and the cost matters to you — file it yourself, using this guide. If any complication applies, or your time is worth more than the fee, hiring help is a normal tradeoff, not a failure of independence.
