The residency process has a fairly consistent shape once you strip away the
nationality-specific details: prepare documents, enter the country, file in
person, then wait for a decision. Here's the overview — the [week-by-week
detail lives inside the guide's members area](/insider) for people who want
the full checklist.

## Before you travel

This is where most of the real time gets spent, and where the most avoidable
delays happen. Background checks need to be recent enough to still be valid
by the time you file (they have a limited validity window), and documents
issued abroad typically need an apostille and, often, a certified Spanish
translation. Start this stage earlier than feels necessary — see [the
mistakes we see every month](/blog/mistakes-we-see-every-month) for the
specific ways people get this stage wrong.

## Entering the country

Confirm whether your nationality needs an entry visa or gets a visa-exempt
stamp — see our [visa guide](/blog/paraguay-visa-guide) for the distinction.
Either way, entry is separate from the residency filing itself, which happens
in person once you're there.

## Filing in person

This stage involves appointments with the relevant immigration authority,
submitting your prepared documents, and — depending on the route — additional
steps like a police background check conducted locally. Document requirements
by route are covered in [Paraguay residency requirements](/blog/requirements-residency-paraguay).

## Waiting for a decision

Government processing time is the one part of this process nobody can
shorten by paying more or being more prepared — see [how long Paraguay
residency actually takes](/blog/how-long-paraguay-residency-actually-takes)
for a realistic picture of this stage.

## After approval

Once approved, you'll move through cédula issuance
(<Fact k="cedula.timeline" />) and — if relevant to your situation — banking
and tax registration steps. These are covered in more depth for [entry-guide
members](/insider).

This is a general overview, not a substitute for the specific document
checklist and current government requirements for your nationality. Confirm
specifics before filing.
