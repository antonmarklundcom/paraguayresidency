The residency process has a consistent shape once you strip away nationality-specific details: prepare documents, enter the country, file in person, wait for a decision, then handle the cédula. Here is the concrete, numbered version.

## What are the actual steps, in order?

1. **Gather and apostille your documents** — passport, police certificate, birth certificate, proof of means. This is the step people most underestimate; see the [full checklist](/blog/documents-you-need-for-paraguay-residency).
2. **Enter Paraguay** on a visa-exempt stamp or entry visa, depending on your nationality — see our [visa guide](/blog/paraguay-visa-guide).
3. **File in person** with your prepared documents, plus any locally-obtained items such as an Interpol certificate (<Fact k="fees.interpol_certificate" />).
4. **Wait for DNM review** — runs <Fact k="residency.timeline" />, the one stage nobody can shorten by paying more.
5. **Receive approval**, then pay the temporary residency government fee: <Fact k="fees.temporary_residency" />.
6. **Apply for and receive your cédula** — a separate step taking <Fact k="cedula.timeline" />.
7. **Register for banking and tax** once your cédula is in hand — see the [banking article](/blog/opening-a-bank-account-in-paraguay).

## What happens before you travel?

This is where most of the real time gets spent, and where the most avoidable delays happen. Background checks need to be recent enough to still be valid by the time you file, and documents issued abroad typically need an apostille and, often, a certified Spanish translation. Start this stage earlier than feels necessary — see [the mistakes we see every month](/blog/mistakes-we-see-every-month) for the specific ways people get this stage wrong.

## What happens when you enter the country?

Confirm whether your nationality needs an entry visa or gets a visa-exempt stamp — see our [visa guide](/blog/paraguay-visa-guide) for the distinction. Either way, entry is separate from the residency filing itself, which happens in person once you're there.

## What happens at the in-person filing?

This stage involves appointments with the relevant immigration authority, submitting your prepared documents, and — depending on the route — additional steps like a police background check conducted locally. Document requirements by route are covered in [Paraguay residency requirements](/blog/requirements-residency-paraguay).

## What happens after approval?

Once approved, you move through cédula issuance (<Fact k="cedula.timeline" />) and, once that's in hand, banking and tax registration steps. Government processing time at every stage is the one part of this process nobody can shorten by paying more or being more prepared.

## The honest bottom line

None of these seven steps are individually hard. What trips people up is sequencing — starting document preparation too late, or trying to bank before the cédula arrives. Follow the order above and each step is a predictable task rather than a surprise.
