Short answer: no, you do not need to make a fixed bank deposit to get Paraguay residency. That requirement is {{fact:solvency.deposit_abolished}}. If you read it on a forum, an old blog post, or even an earlier version of this site, it is describing a rule that no longer exists.

## Where did the old fixed-deposit figure come from?

It was a feature of the pre-2022 residency regime under Ley 978/1996, where a fixed bank deposit was one accepted way to demonstrate means of support. Ley 6984/2022 replaced that entire framework, and the fixed-deposit option went with it. Anyone quoting a fixed deposit figure in a Paraguayan bank as a current step is at least several years out of date.

## What actually replaces it in 2026?

For permanent residency, and for Mercosur nationals converting to permanent status, the current rule is Res. DNM 407/2026, which applies to filings from 6 July 2026 onward: {{fact:solvency.requirement}}.

That is a meaningfully different test from a deposit. Instead of one number everyone wires into a bank account, the authority now wants to see *how* you actually support yourself — and expects the paperwork to match one of a defined set of applicant profiles.

| Regime | Period | What it asked for |
|---|---|---|
| Ley 978/1996 (old) | Until 2022 | A fixed bank deposit, of a figure commonly quoted in older guides |
| Ley 6984/2022 | 2022–6 Jul 2026 | Documented means of support, evaluated case by case, no published fixed figure |
| Res. DNM 407/2026 | From 6 Jul 2026 | Documented, verifiable income or assets matched to one of 12 applicant categories |

## What "one of 12 categories" means in practice

The categories cover professionals, technicians, employees, the self-employed, teleworkers and digital nomads, property owners, shareholders, farmers, clergy, retirees, dependants and students. You pick the one that describes your actual situation and bring the evidence that category calls for — payslips and an employment letter if you are an employee, an active RUC and invoices if you are self-employed, a pension statement if you are retired, and so on. A degree certificate by itself is generally no longer treated as sufficient proof on its own.

## The mistake this creates

People who read an old figure online sometimes over-prepare for a requirement that no longer exists — wiring money into a Paraguayan account they didn't need to, or delaying their application while they save toward a number nobody is asking for anymore. The opposite mistake also happens: assuming "no fixed amount" means "no real requirement," and arriving without documentation that matches any of the 12 categories, which stalls the file just as effectively as a missing deposit once did.

## Who this is for

This matters most to anyone converting from temporary to permanent residency, or a Mercosur national converting a temporary Mercosur card to permanent, after 6 July 2026. If you are still at the temporary-residency stage, this specific rule is not yet the one you need to satisfy — see the [route comparison](/blog/requirements-residency-paraguay) for what applies at each stage.
