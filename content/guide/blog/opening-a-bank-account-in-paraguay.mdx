Banking is the step new residents underestimate the most. It is not hard, but it is
sequential — you cannot skip ahead, and showing up without the right paperwork means a second
trip and a lost afternoon.

## What you need, in the order banks actually ask for it

1. Your passport, valid and with copies made in advance (branches rarely have a working
   photocopier when you need one).
2. Proof of a Paraguayan address — a utility bill in your name, or a signed lease, dated
   recently.
3. Your cédula, or at minimum an official document showing your residency application is
   in process. This is the step most people get stuck on: a tourist stamp alone is not enough
   at most banks.
4. A minimum opening deposit, which varies by bank and account type.
5. Sometimes, a reference — a local contact or professional who can vouch for you. Not every
   bank asks; some do.

## Which banks work with foreigners

Several of the larger Paraguayan banks are used to foreign residents and have staff who have
done this before; several of the smaller ones are not, and a first-time visit can turn into an
education for the teller as much as for you. Asking other recent residents which branch they
used, specifically, is more useful than picking a bank by name alone — the experience varies by
branch, not just by institution.

## Cédula timing matters more than anything else here

Almost every banking friction point traces back to one thing: your cédula. <Fact k="cedula.timeline" />,
and until you have it, you are working with a smaller set of banking options and, often, lower
limits. This is one more reason the timeline in chapter 5 of the guide starts document
preparation in week one rather than week three — banking is downstream of everything else, not
a parallel track you can rush independently.

## Currency and moving money

Paraguay's currency is the guaraní; most banks also offer USD-denominated accounts or at least
USD services, which matters if your income arrives from abroad. International transfers work,
but expect fees and a few business days, not an instant transfer — plan around that rather than
being surprised by it the first time you need money to arrive quickly.

## What surprises people

- **Hours.** Banks keep shorter public hours than many countries; arriving at the end of the day
  is a common way to lose a full day.
- **Paper.** Paraguay's banking system still relies on physical documents and in-person visits
  more than a fully digital-first country would. Budget time for it, especially in your first
  month.
- **Small differences between branches of the same bank.** What one branch requires, another
  might not insist on as strictly. This is frustrating and also just true — build in slack.

## The honest bottom line

None of this is unusually difficult once you know the sequence, and thousands of foreign
residents bank normally here every year. The people who struggle are the ones who tried to open
an account before their paperwork was ready, or who assumed one bank's rules applied everywhere.
Get your documents in order first — see the [full document checklist](/blog/documents-you-need-for-paraguay-residency) —
and the account itself is a routine afternoon, not an ordeal.
