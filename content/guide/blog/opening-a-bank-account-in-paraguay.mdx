Banking is the step new residents underestimate the most. It isn't hard, but it's sequential — you can't skip ahead, and showing up without the right paperwork means a second trip and a lost afternoon.

## What do I need to open a bank account, in the order banks actually ask for it?

1. **Your passport**, valid, with copies made in advance — branches rarely have a working photocopier when you need one.
2. **Proof of a Paraguayan address** — a utility bill in your name, or a signed lease, dated recently.
3. **Your cédula**, or at minimum an official document showing your residency application is in process. A tourist stamp alone is not enough at most banks — this is the step most people get stuck on.
4. **A minimum opening deposit**, which varies by bank and account type.
5. **Sometimes, a reference** — a local contact or professional who can vouch for you. Not every bank asks; some do.

## Why does the cédula matter so much more than anything else here?

Almost every banking friction point traces back to one thing: your cédula, which takes <Fact k="cedula.timeline" /> after approval. Until you have it, you're working with a smaller set of banking options and, often, lower limits. This is one more reason to start document preparation in week one of your timeline rather than week three — banking is downstream of everything else, not a parallel track you can rush independently. ## Which banks work with foreigners?

Several of the larger Paraguayan banks are used to foreign residents and have staff who've done this before; several smaller ones are not, and a first-time visit can turn into an education for the teller as much as for you. Asking recent residents which specific branch they used is more useful than picking a bank by name alone — the experience varies by branch, not just institution.

## What surprises people about banking here?

| Surprise | What to do about it |
|---|---|
| Shorter public hours than many countries | Go early in the day, not at closing time |
| Heavy reliance on physical documents | Budget real time for it, especially your first month |
| Branches of the same bank differ | Ask other residents which specific branch, not just which bank |
| International transfers take days, not seconds | Plan around it if you need money to arrive quickly |

## How does currency and moving money actually work?

Paraguay's currency is the guaraní; most banks also offer USD-denominated accounts or at least USD services, which matters if your income arrives from abroad. International transfers work, but expect fees and a few business days.

## The honest bottom line

None of this is unusually difficult once you know the sequence, and thousands of foreign residents bank normally here every year. The people who struggle are the ones who tried to open an account before their paperwork was ready.
