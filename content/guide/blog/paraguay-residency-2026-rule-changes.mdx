Residency rules that felt settled for years all moved in the same twelve-month window in 2026. If you read an older article — including some on this site before this update — assume it may describe the pre-2026 rules and check the current ones here first.

## What changed with the presence/absence rules?

DNM Resolution 376/2026 sets out, in more specific terms than before, how long you can be away from Paraguay before your residency status is affected. For temporary residency: <Fact k="temporary.presence_rule" />. For permanent residency: <Fact k="permanent.presence_rule" />. The resolution also states that compliance is checked periodically rather than only at renewal, which is the detail that surprises people who assumed an occasional long trip abroad wouldn't be noticed.

This matters most for anyone not planning to live in Paraguay full-time — see [our guide for remote workers](/blog/paraguay-residency-for-remote-workers) if that's your situation, since travel patterns and presence rules interact directly with how comfortably a foreign-income lifestyle fits the requirement.

## What changed with the solvency/income requirement?

DNM Resolution 407/2026, effective from July 2026, replaced the older approach — which pointed toward a fixed minimum amount — with a documented, category-based standard: <Fact k="solvency.requirement" />. In practice this means demonstrating your income or assets fit a recognized category (employment income, a pension, business ownership, savings, and others) with real supporting documents, rather than simply showing one fixed number in a bank account.

This is a genuinely different way of qualifying than the old deposit-based approach, and it followed an earlier change that removed the fixed deposit entirely: <Fact k="solvency.deposit_abolished" />. If you read an article describing a required deposit amount, it's describing a rule that no longer applies — see [our dedicated piece on that specific change](/blog/5000-deposit).

## What changed with fees?

Paraguay's residency-related government fees aren't set as fixed guaraní amounts written into a form once — <Fact k="fees.basis" />. Because the minimum wage was adjusted mid-2026, every fee tied to it rose on 1 July 2026. This isn't a one-time event; expect the same mechanism to push fees up again whenever the minimum wage next adjusts. See [the full current fee breakdown](/blog/paraguay-residency-cost) for what each fee costs today.

## What changed with the Investor Pass?

The Investor Pass launched as a distinct, formalized route via <Fact k="investorpass.legal_instrument" />. Before this, investment-based residency existed but wasn't packaged as clearly into named routes with specific thresholds. The Investor Pass now defines four routes — a productive business route, a tourism-project route, a real-estate route, and a financial-instruments route — each with its own qualifying investment and its own timeline. [Our dedicated Investor Pass article](/blog/paraguay-investor-pass-explained) breaks down all four; if that's your route, [paraguayinvestorpass.com](https://paraguayinvestorpass.com) is the dedicated site for it.

## Summary table: the four 2026 changes

| Change | What it replaced | Effective |
|---|---|---|
| Documented absence limits (Res. 376/2026) | Less specific presence expectations | 2026 |
| Category-based solvency (Res. 407/2026) | A fixed minimum-amount expectation | From July 2026 |
| Fee increase (minimum-wage peg) | The previous jornal-based fee level | 1 July 2026 |
| Investor Pass formalized (Res. 0283/2026) | Less clearly packaged investment routes | 2026 |

## Why did all of this change in the same year?

It isn't a coincidence that four separate changes landed in the same twelve-month window. Paraguay's immigration and investment framework had gone several years with rules that were increasingly out of step with how many applicants actually qualify — a fixed deposit that didn't reflect real financial situations, presence expectations that weren't specific enough to enforce consistently, and an investment-residency category that existed without being clearly packaged into named routes. 2026's resolutions read as a coordinated modernization rather than four unrelated tweaks, which is also why treating them as a single "what's new" picture, rather than four disconnected facts, makes more sense when you're deciding how they affect your own application.

## How to check whether a source you're reading is current

Given how much moved in 2026, treat any Paraguay residency content — including other pages on this site — with a simple test: does it cite a specific resolution number and date, or does it just state a rule as if it's timeless? A source that references DNM Resolution 376/2026, 407/2026, or MIC Resolution 0283/2026 by name is telling you it's accounting for the current framework. A source that describes a fixed deposit amount, a vague "you must visit occasionally" presence rule, or investment residency as one undifferentiated category is very likely describing the pre-2026 rules.

## The mistake that costs people the most

Relying on an older article, a forum post from a few years ago, or a provider's outdated marketing material that still describes the fixed deposit, the pre-2026 fee level, or a vaguer presence expectation. Rules in this space genuinely change, and 2026 changed more of them at once than a typical year. Check the effective date on anything you're reading, including this page, and confirm anything decision-critical against a current source or adviser.

## Who needs to pay closest attention to these changes

Anyone applying for the first time in the second half of 2026 or later is filing entirely under the new rules and should treat this page, not an older one, as the baseline. Anyone who already holds temporary or permanent residency should specifically check the new absence rules against their own travel plans — this is the change most likely to catch an existing resident off guard.
