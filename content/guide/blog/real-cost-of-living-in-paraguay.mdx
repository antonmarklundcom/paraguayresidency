## What does housing actually cost?

A one-bedroom apartment in a normal Asunción neighborhood runs <Fact k="costofliving.rent" /> unfurnished — more for furnished short-term rentals aimed at foreigners. That gap matters: if you rent through a listing site built for expats before you have a local contact, you'll pay noticeably more than the market rate. The single best cost-saving move new residents make is waiting two or three weeks, meeting people, and renting through a local rather than a website. Outside Asunción — Encarnación, Ciudad del Este, smaller interior towns — rent drops further, sometimes by half.

## What does food actually cost?

Groceries for one person run <Fact k="costofliving.groceries" /> a month, and a set lunch at a simple restaurant costs correspondingly little. Markets and local supermarkets are inexpensive for anything produced in Paraguay or its neighbors — beef, dairy, fruit, vegetables, yerba mate. Imported goods cost noticeably more, the same pattern as most of South America.

## What does transport cost?

A used car costs more in Paraguay than the same car in the US, largely because of import taxes on the vehicle itself — fuel and routine maintenance are reasonable. Public buses in Asunción are inexpensive but not always predictable on schedule. Many new residents go without a car for the first few months and decide once they know which part of the city they actually live and work in.

## What does health insurance cost?

Private health insurance is one of the genuinely affordable parts of living here relative to a US-based plan, and private clinics in Asunción are generally well regarded — see the [health insurance guide](/blog/health-insurance-paraguay) for what plans typically cover and what to check before choosing one. Get quotes once you're here rather than months in advance; premiums and available plans change.

## The honest summary

Paraguay is inexpensive relative to the US, Canada, the UK and most of Western Europe, and noticeably less expensive than Argentina was for a long stretch. It is not the cheapest country in the hemisphere, and treating every price you see online as "too cheap to be true" usually means you're looking at a listing built for foreigners rather than the market rate locals actually pay. Give it a real visit, rent like a local, and the numbers above hold up.
