Every relocation forum has a thread arguing about how cheap Paraguay is. Most of them are
either selling something or comparing it to a country with a much stronger currency. Here is
what things actually cost, from people who live here and pay these bills every month — not a
tourist estimate.

## Housing

A one-bedroom apartment in a normal Asunción neighborhood (not the most expensive barrios, not
the cheapest) runs in the low hundreds of dollars per month unfurnished, more for furnished
short-term rentals aimed at foreigners. That gap matters: if you rent through a listing site
built for expats before you have a local contact, you will pay noticeably more than the market
rate. The single best cost-saving move new residents make is waiting two or three weeks,
meeting people, and renting through a local rather than a website.

Outside Asunción — Encarnación, Ciudad del Este, smaller interior towns — rent drops further,
sometimes by half, at the cost of fewer imported goods and a smaller expat community.

## Food

Markets and local supermarkets are inexpensive for anything produced in Paraguay or its
neighbors: beef, dairy, fruit, vegetables, yerba mate. Imported goods — specific breakfast
cereals, some electronics, certain packaged foods — cost noticeably more, the same pattern as
most of South America. Eating at local restaurants (not the international chains near the
malls) is cheap by North American or European standards; eating at those chains is not
meaningfully cheaper than at home.

## Transport

A used car costs more in Paraguay than the same car would in the US, largely because of import
taxes on the vehicle itself — fuel and routine maintenance are reasonable. Public buses in
Asunción are inexpensive but not always predictable on schedule. Many new residents go without
a car for the first few months and decide once they know which part of the city they actually
live and work in.

## Health insurance

Private health insurance is one of the genuinely affordable parts of living here relative to a
US-based plan, and private clinics in Asunción are generally well regarded. This is one area
where "cheap" is not a euphemism for "worse" — it reflects Paraguay's overall cost structure,
not a quality tradeoff. Get quotes once you are here rather than online months in advance;
premiums and available plans change.

## The honest summary

Paraguay is inexpensive relative to the US, Canada, the UK and most of Western Europe, and
noticeably less expensive than its regional peer Argentina was for a long stretch and than
Uruguay generally is. It is not the cheapest country in the hemisphere, and treating every price
you see as "too cheap to be true" usually means you are looking at a listing built for
foreigners rather than the market rate locals actually pay. Give it a real visit, rent like a
local, and the numbers above hold up.
