If you're investing in Paraguay anyway — starting a business, buying commercial property, backing a tourism project, or holding qualifying financial instruments — the Investor Pass is designed to convert that investment directly into permanent residency, without going through the standard temporary-then-permanent sequence most applicants follow.

## What is the Investor Pass?

It's a dedicated permanent-residency route created by <Fact k="investorpass.legal_instrument" />. Rather than one generic "investment residency" category, it defines four specific routes, each with its own qualifying investment type and minimum. The residency granted is permanent from the start: <Fact k="investorpass.validity_years" />.

## The four routes compared

| Route | Qualifying investment | Minimum |
|---|---|---|
| Productive business | Business plan, formal jobs created | <Fact k="investorpass.route_business_usd" /> |
| Tourism project | Business plan for a tourism venture | <Fact k="investorpass.route_tourism_usd" /> |
| Real estate | Property used for economic activity, not your own home | <Fact k="investorpass.route_real_estate_usd" /> |
| Financial instruments | Qualifying instruments, held for a minimum period | <Fact k="investorpass.route_financial_usd" /> |

Across all four, the overall minimum investment described by the program is <Fact k="investorpass.min_investment_usd" /> — the specific figure that applies to you depends on which route fits your actual investment.

## How does the business route work?

The productive-business route continues the earlier SUACE framework, now inside the Investor Pass: <Fact k="suace.status" />. This route requires more than parking capital — you need a real business plan and you need to actually create the formal jobs the route requires, which means your timeline depends partly on how quickly you can stand up the operation, not just on paperwork speed.

## What about the passive routes — tourism, real estate, financial?

These three don't require job creation the way the business route does, but each has its own conditions: the tourism route needs a genuine project with its own business plan, the real estate route explicitly excludes property bought as your personal home, and the financial route requires the qualifying instruments to be held for a minimum period rather than bought and immediately liquidated. None of the four is a simple "wire money, get a card" transaction — each has real conditions attached.

## How long does the whole process take?

<Fact k="investorpass.timeline" />. The investor certificate step is comparatively fast; the residency resolution afterward is the longer part of the timeline, and — for the business route specifically — the job-creation and investment-execution requirements run on their own separate clock.

## Investor Pass vs the standard temporary-to-permanent route

| | Standard route | Investor Pass |
|---|---|---|
| Starting status | Temporary, then permanent later | Permanent directly |
| Core requirement | Standard document set, solvency category | Qualifying investment in one of four routes |
| Best fit | Most individual applicants | People already investing meaningfully in Paraguay |
| Timeline shape | Two stages over a longer period | One process, front-loaded investment commitment |

If you're not investing at this scale, the [standard routes comparison](/blog/requirements-residency-paraguay) is the more relevant starting point — the Investor Pass isn't a shortcut for someone without a genuine qualifying investment.

## What documents does an Investor Pass application still need?

Beyond the investment-specific paperwork (business plan, proof of funds, property deed, or financial-instrument statements depending on your route), you still need the standard supporting documents most applicants provide: a valid passport, an apostilled background check, and any civil-status documents relevant to family members joining you. The Investor Pass changes how you demonstrate eligibility for permanent residency — it doesn't remove the underlying identity and background-check requirements every applicant goes through. See [the full document checklist](/blog/documents-you-need-for-paraguay-residency) for the baseline that still applies alongside your investment paperwork.

## Can family members be included under an Investor Pass application?

Family-based residency provisions generally allow a spouse and dependent children to apply alongside a primary applicant across Paraguay's residency routes, and the Investor Pass is no exception in principle — but confirm the current specific process for including family members under this particular route, since the investment-based routes sometimes carry their own procedural details that differ from the standard family-residency process used with temporary or permanent applications.

## The mistake that costs people the most

Treating the minimum investment figure as the whole cost. Beyond the qualifying investment itself, you still need the standard supporting documents, still need to demonstrate the investment meets its route's specific conditions (jobs created, non-residential use, holding period), and still face the same document/apostille/translation work as any other applicant. The investment doesn't replace the paperwork — it replaces the solvency-category requirement other routes use.

## How does the Investor Pass interact with tax residency?

Holding permanent residency through the Investor Pass doesn't automatically make you a Paraguayan tax resident — that's still a separate step involving a cédula, an active RUC, and the tax residency certificate process described in [our dedicated tax residency article](/blog/paraguay-tax-residency-certificate). Investors sometimes assume the scale of their investment settles the tax question too; it doesn't, and Paraguay's territorial tax treatment of your specific business or investment income still needs its own review with an adviser familiar with both Paraguay's rules and your home country's.

## Who this is for — and who it isn't

This route makes sense if you already have capital you're planning to deploy in Paraguay regardless of the residency outcome — a business you'd start anyway, property you'd buy anyway, an investment portfolio you're comfortable allocating here. It's a poor fit if the only reason you'd make the investment is to get residency faster; in that case, compare the total cost and risk against the standard route's slower but lower-commitment path first.
