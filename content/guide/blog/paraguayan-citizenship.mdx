Citizenship by naturalization is the step some permanent residents eventually
take after residency, and it's a genuinely separate process with its own
requirements — not an automatic upgrade that happens after enough time
passes. If residency is the destination for most people in this guide,
naturalization is the optional next stop for those who specifically want a
Paraguayan passport.

## How this differs from residency

Permanent residency gives you the right to live in Paraguay indefinitely.
Citizenship is a distinct legal status with its own application, its own
review, and its own qualifying period built on top of an existing permanent
residency. The two are sequential, not interchangeable, and conflating them
is one of the more common misunderstandings people bring into this research.

- A minimum period holding permanent residency status first — the exact

- A formal application with its own document set, generally including proof
  of your residency history, identity documents, and background checks —
  expect meaningful overlap with what you already assembled for residency,
  plus items specific to naturalization.
- A government review period that, like the residency process itself, is
  outside anyone's ability to guarantee a timeline for.

## Why people choose to naturalize

Reasons vary — a Paraguayan passport, deeper integration for a family that's
settled long-term, or simply wanting the most complete legal tie to a country
they've made home. It is not something most residents need in order to enjoy
the day-to-day benefits of living in Paraguay; residency alone covers that.

## Before you start

Talk to your own country's authorities about how naturalizing elsewhere
interacts with your existing citizenship before filing anything — this is a
personal legal question specific to your home country, not something a
general guide can answer for you. This page is informational and not a
substitute for advice from qualified immigration counsel.
