Most people researching Paraguay residency never need to read this page — permanent residency already gives you the right to live here indefinitely, bank, work and stay without a citizenship application. This page is for the smaller group who specifically want a Paraguayan passport or the deepest possible legal tie to the country.

## How many years of residency does naturalization require?

{{fact:citizenship.years}}. That is not from your original temporary residency approval, and not from the date you first entered Paraguay — only the permanent residency resolution date starts the clock. Keep the resolution and its date somewhere you can find it years later; the naturalization filing references it directly.

## How is citizenship different from the permanent residency I already have?

| | Permanent residency | Citizenship (naturalization) |
|---|---|---|
| Grants | Indefinite right to live in Paraguay | Full legal nationality, including a passport |
| Process | Administrative (DNM) | Judicial (Corte Suprema) |
| Eligibility | Documented income/assets, presence rule | {{fact:citizenship.years}} |
| Ongoing obligation | {{fact:permanent.presence_rule}} | None once granted |
| Who needs it | Nearly everyone in this guide | Only those who specifically want a passport or full nationality |

The two statuses are sequential, not interchangeable, and treating naturalization as something that happens automatically once "enough time" passes is one of the more common misunderstandings people bring into this research.

## What does the naturalization process actually involve?

A formal application to the Corte Suprema, including proof of your residency history, identity documents and background checks — with meaningful overlap with what you already assembled for residency, plus items specific to the naturalization filing. Because this is a judicial review rather than a fixed administrative process, we don't publish a specific processing-time figure here; ask your case handler for a current estimate once you file.

## Why do people actually choose to naturalize?

Reasons vary — a Paraguayan passport for travel flexibility, deeper integration for a family that has settled long-term, or wanting the most complete legal tie to a country they've made home. It is not something most residents need in order to enjoy the everyday benefits of living in Paraguay; permanent residency alone covers that.

## The question naturalization doesn't answer for you

Whether naturalizing costs you your original citizenship is entirely your home country's decision, not Paraguay's. Some countries revoke citizenship on foreign naturalization automatically; others allow dual nationality without issue. Confirm your own country's current position with your home country's authorities before filing anything — this is a personal legal question specific to your citizenship, not something any Paraguay-side guide can answer for you.

## Who this is for, and who should skip it

If your goal is to live in Paraguay, bank normally, and hold long-term legal status, permanent residency already does that — see [is Paraguay residency worth it](/blog/is-paraguay-residency-worth-it) for that broader decision. If you specifically want a Paraguayan passport or the fullest possible legal tie after years of residency, naturalization is the next and final step.
