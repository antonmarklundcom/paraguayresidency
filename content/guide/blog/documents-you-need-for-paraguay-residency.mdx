The document list itself isn't the hard part — most applicants can find a version of it online. The hard part is the details a generic checklist leaves out: exactly how long a document stays valid, exactly which apostille authority to use, and what it actually costs when translations and certificates are added up.

## What documents does Paraguay residency require?

- **A valid passport**, with enough validity remaining to cover the process comfortably.
- **A police/background check** from your country of residence (and your country of citizenship, if different), apostilled: {{fact:documents.police_certificate_validity}}.
- **A birth certificate**, apostilled, and a marriage certificate if applicable — both translated if not already in Spanish.
- **Passport-style photographs**, to the specification Paraguayan offices actually use.
- **Proof of means or income**, in the form your specific route requires.
- **A Paraguayan address**, which you'll also need for banking — see the [banking article](/blog/opening-a-bank-account-in-paraguay).

This is the single most time-sensitive document on the list, and if your preparation runs long — because of the apostille or translation steps below, or simply your own schedule — an early certificate can expire before you file, forcing you to redo it and its apostille from scratch.

## What does the apostille process actually cost, and where does it trip people up?

An apostille has to come from the correct authority in the country that issued the original document — not a notary, not a translator, and not a Paraguayan consulate, which typically cannot apostille a document from a third country. Paraguay has been {{fact:apostille.since}}; if your country isn't part of that convention, you need consular legalisation instead. Getting the authority wrong is the single most common reason a document has to be redone entirely — confirm the correct issuing authority before you start, not after a rejection.

Once you're in Paraguay, if you need an Interpol background certificate obtained locally, it costs {{fact:fees.interpol_certificate}}, cash only, issued in one to two working days under normal conditions.

## What does the translation trap look like?

A certified translation needs to meet the standard Paraguayan authorities actually apply — not "any certified translator will do." Some countries' certified-translator systems map cleanly onto what Paraguay expects; others don't, and a translation valid for, say, a US immigration filing can still need redoing for a Paraguayan one. Confirm the required standard before commissioning the translation, not after paying for it.

## What changes by route?

Standard temporary residency ({{fact:temporary.duration}}), permanent residency ({{fact:solvency.requirement}}), and the Investor Pass each ask for a different proof-of-means or proof-of-investment document, on top of the shared core list above. Filing the wrong version of that one document for your route is a smaller mistake than the apostille trap, but still an avoidable delay — see the [route comparison](/blog/requirements-residency-paraguay) to confirm which applies to you.
