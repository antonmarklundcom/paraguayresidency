The document list itself is not the hard part — most applicants can find a version of it online.
The hard part is the details that a generic checklist leaves out: exactly how your country's
apostille process works, exactly how long a document stays valid, and exactly which translation
standard Paraguayan authorities expect. This article covers the core list and the traps; the
guide's full chapter goes nationality by nationality.

## The core list

- **Valid passport**, with enough validity remaining to cover the process comfortably.
- **A police/background check from your country of residence** (and sometimes your country of
  citizenship, if different), apostilled.
- **A birth certificate**, apostilled, and a marriage certificate if applicable — both
  translated if not already in Spanish.
- **Passport-style photographs**, to the specification Paraguayan offices actually use, which
  differs slightly from a standard passport photo in some countries.
- **Proof of means or income**, in the form your specific route requires — this varies more than
  any other item on the list depending on whether you are filing standard temporary residency,
  permanent residency, or the Investor Pass.
- **A Paraguayan address**, which you will also need for banking — see the
  [banking article](/blog/opening-a-bank-account-in-paraguay) for how that connects.

## The apostille trap

An apostille has to come from the correct authority in the country that issued the original
document — not a notary, not a translator, and not a Paraguayan consulate, which typically
cannot apostille a document from a third country. Getting this backwards is the single most
common reason a document has to be redone from scratch, and it is entirely avoidable by
confirming the correct issuing authority before you start rather than after a rejection.

## The translation trap

A certified translation needs to meet the standard Paraguayan authorities actually apply, which
is not identical to "any certified translator will do." Some countries' certified-translator
systems map cleanly onto what Paraguay expects; others do not, and a translation that would be
perfectly valid for, say, a US immigration filing can still need redoing for a Paraguayan one.
Confirm this before commissioning the translation, not after paying for it.

## Validity windows

Background checks in particular are usually only considered current for a limited window after
issuance. If your document preparation stretches out — because of the apostille or translation
traps above, or simply because of your own schedule — an early document can expire before your
filing is complete, forcing you to redo it. This is one more reason starting document
preparation in week one of your timeline matters more than almost anything else; see the
[timeline article](/blog/how-long-paraguay-residency-actually-takes) for the full week-by-week
picture.

## What changes by route

Standard temporary residency (<Fact k="temporary.duration" />), permanent residency
(<Fact k="permanent.presence_rule" /> applies once you hold it), and the Investor Pass each ask
for a different proof-of-means or proof-of-investment document, on top of the shared core list
above. Filing the wrong version of that one document for your route is a smaller mistake than
the apostille trap, but it is still an avoidable delay.

## The shortcut that actually works

There is no shortcut around gathering the documents themselves. The real shortcut is knowing
the traps above before you start, rather than discovering them one rejection at a time — which
is the entire reason this list exists as its own article instead of being buried inside a longer
one. If you are not yet sure which route your documents even need to support, the
[Route Finder](/route-finder) settles that first.
