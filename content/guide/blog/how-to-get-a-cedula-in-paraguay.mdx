Getting residency approved feels like the finish line, but the cédula — your actual Paraguayan identity card — is a separate final step, and it's the document banks, landlords and most other institutions will actually ask you for. Here's the process end to end.

## What is the cédula, exactly?

The cédula de identidad is Paraguay's national identity card, issued to residents (and citizens) by the Dirección General de Identificaciones. Holding residency approval from DNM makes you eligible for it — it doesn't automatically produce the card. You still need to apply for and collect it as its own step.

## Step by step: getting your cédula

1. **Confirm your residency approval is finalized.** You need the approved residency resolution from DNM in hand before applying for the cédula — not just a submission receipt.
2. **Gather the required documents.** Typically your passport, your DNM residency resolution, and any additional supporting documents Identificaciones asks for at this stage — confirm the current list rather than assuming it's identical to your residency-stage document set.
3. **Book or attend your Identificaciones appointment.** This is done in person; biometric data (photo, fingerprints) is captured as part of this step.
4. **Pay the applicable fee**, if any applies to your situation (the first issue is generally free, per the fee above).
5. **Wait for processing** — within the official window described above, though real-world timelines can run longer, especially during busy periods.
6. **Collect your card.** Confirm the current collection process before your appointment — in-person collection requirements have changed in recent years; see [our dedicated article on cédula collection and power of attorney](/blog/cedula-power-of-attorney) for what's changed.

## The mistakes that delay a cédula application

- **Assuming residency approval is enough on its own.** Some people stop preparing paperwork the moment DNM approves residency, then arrive at the cédula appointment without something Identificaciones specifically wants.
- **Name mismatches across documents.** A name spelled slightly differently between your passport, your residency resolution, and a supporting document can stall the process — check every document matches exactly before your appointment.
- **Expired supporting documents.** If time has passed between your residency approval and your cédula appointment, double-check nothing you're bringing has quietly expired in the interim.
- **Assuming the cédula arrives automatically.** It doesn't — it's an application you file and an appointment you attend, not a card mailed to you once residency clears.

## What if something goes wrong at your appointment?

If Identificaciones flags a missing or inconsistent document at your appointment, the practical response is the same regardless of what specifically is wrong: get a clear, specific list of what needs fixing before you leave, rather than a vague "come back later." Some issues (a document you simply forgot) can potentially be resolved same-day if you have it accessible; others (a document that needs a fresh apostille or translation) will genuinely restart part of the clock. Either way, understanding exactly what's missing on the day saves a second wasted trip.

## After you have your cédula

Once your card is in hand, a few things typically follow in sequence: opening or upgrading a bank account now that you have a proper local ID (see [the banking guide](/blog/opening-a-bank-account-in-paraguay)), registering for an active RUC if you plan to work, invoice, or eventually apply for a tax residency certificate, and updating any accounts or services that only accepted your passport as identification while you were mid-process. None of these happen automatically the moment you collect the card — each is its own small next step.

## Why the cédula matters beyond having an ID card

Many everyday things in Paraguay specifically ask for the cédula rather than accepting residency-approval paperwork alone — opening a bank account (see [the banking guide](/blog/opening-a-bank-account-in-paraguay)), some rental agreements, and the tax residency certificate process (see [the tax residency guide](/blog/paraguay-tax-residency-certificate)), which requires an active RUC that itself typically follows cédula issuance. Plan around the cédula as a real, separate milestone in your timeline, not an afterthought.

## Does the cédula ever need to be renewed?

Yes — while the first issue is a one-time event tied to your initial residency, cédulas generally need periodic renewal or replacement (if lost or damaged) after that, each carrying its own small fee separate from the first-issue cost described above. Treat the renewal date as its own reminder to track, the same way you'd track a passport's expiry, rather than assuming the card is valid indefinitely once issued.
