Health insurance is a practical question every new resident eventually has to answer, and the honest picture is: private insurance in Paraguay is accessible, generally good in Asunción, and worth understanding before you need it rather than after.

## Should I use public or private healthcare in Paraguay?

Paraguay has a public healthcare system, but the realistic pattern among foreign residents is private insurance and private clinics — mainly for faster appointment access, generally shorter wait times, and, depending on the provider, English-speaking staff, which matters a great deal in a medical setting if your Spanish isn't yet fluent.

## What do private plans typically cover?

Plans vary by provider and tier, but generally include outpatient consultations, hospitalization, and a range of diagnostic services.

| Check this | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Network hospitals and clinics | Coverage thins out fast outside Asunción |
| Pre-existing condition handling | Varies dramatically provider to provider |
| Emergency and evacuation coverage | More relevant the further you are from a major hospital |
| Cost by age and tier | Changes often — get a current quote, don't rely on an old figure |

Comparing at least two or three plans directly is worth the time before committing to one.

## What should I check before choosing a plan?

- **Network hospitals and clinics**, especially if you plan to live outside Asunción, where private-network coverage is thinner.
- **Pre-existing condition treatment**, handled very differently provider to provider.
- **Emergency and evacuation coverage**, particularly relevant further from Asunción's larger hospitals.
- **Cost**, which varies by age, coverage tier and provider — get a current quote directly rather than relying on a general figure.

## What matters more if I'm retiring here or have ongoing medical needs?

Read the healthcare section of our [retiring in Paraguay guide](/blog/retire-in-paraguay) as well — coverage availability and quality outside Asunción is a bigger factor for this group than for younger, generally healthier applicants.

## Who this suits, and who it doesn't

Private insurance here suits residents who prioritise fast access and don't need constant proximity to the most specialised care available worldwide. It suits people less well if a hard requirement is world-class specialist care outside a capital city, or extensive English-language coverage in the interior.
