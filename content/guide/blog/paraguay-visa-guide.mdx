Most nationalities researching Paraguay residency don't need to secure a visa before flying in — they get a visa-exempt entry stamp on arrival and start the residency process from there. A smaller list of nationalities needs an entry visa in advance from a Paraguayan consulate. Which list you're on depends entirely on your passport, and that list is set by the government, so confirm it directly before you book anything.

## What are the two entry situations?

**Visa-exempt.** You arrive, get a standard entry stamp, and begin the residency filing process locally — no advance paperwork with a consulate.

**Visa-required.** You apply for an entry visa at a Paraguayan consulate in your home country (or the nearest one handling your nationality) before you travel. This adds lead time to your trip planning.

Either way, the entry visa — or its absence — is separate from the residency application itself; it only governs how you get into the country. Filing for residency is a distinct in-person step, [with its own document list](/blog/documents-you-need-for-paraguay-residency).

## Where do people get this wrong?

The most common mistake is confusing "I don't need a visa to enter" with "I don't need to formally file for residency." They're unrelated. A tourist stamp lets you visit; it does not grant residency status on its own, and it has its own limited validity window. If your plan is to live in Paraguay rather than visit, the residency filing is the step that actually matters — see [how long that process actually takes](/blog/how-long-paraguay-residency-actually-takes).

## What should I check before I book a flight?

- Confirm your nationality's current entry requirement directly — a consulate website or a recent, dated source, not an old blog post including this one.
- If you need an entry visa, start that process well before your intended travel date; consulate processing times vary.
- Bring the documents you'll need for the residency filing itself with you — arriving on the right kind of stamp doesn't help if your paperwork isn't ready.

## How does this fit into the bigger process?

Entry is step two of the [seven-step overview](/blog/step-by-step) — document preparation comes first, and it matters far more to your overall timeline than which entry category you fall into.
