"How long does it take?" is the first question almost everyone asks, and most published answers quote only one part of a longer process. Here is the honest, week-by-week version, with the actual government processing windows.

## What is the total timeline, start to finish?

Three stages, each with its own clock: document preparation (weeks, mostly under your control), DNM review of {{fact:residency.timeline}}, then the cédula itself at {{fact:cedula.timeline}}. Add them up and a realistic range from first document gathered to cédula in hand runs several months for a straightforward case — not the "weeks" some marketing pages imply by quoting only the DNM stage.

| Stage | Typical window | Who controls it |
|---|---|---|
| Document preparation (apostilles, translations, background checks) | Days to several weeks, nationality-dependent | You |
| DNM review of temporary residency | {{fact:residency.timeline}} | Government |
| Cédula issuance after approval | {{fact:cedula.timeline}} | Government |
| Investor Pass (CIE + residency resolution) | {{fact:investorpass.timeline}} | Government, on top of your investment setup |

## The part you control: document preparation

This is where the clock actually starts, and where it commonly stalls. Apostilled documents, certified translations and background checks from your home country can take anywhere from days to several weeks depending on your country of origin and how quickly you start. Starting this in week one — before you book a flight, before you decide on a neighborhood — is the single biggest lever you have over your own timeline. People who start document preparation only after arriving in Paraguay routinely add a month or more for no reason other than sequencing.

## How long does DNM actually take to approve temporary residency?

Once your documents are complete and filed, DNM review of temporary residency runs {{fact:residency.timeline}}. Since late 2025, DNM and Interpol also verify background checks directly with the country of origin, which can add unpredictable delay depending on your nationality — one more reason to file a complete, correct package the first time rather than expecting to fix gaps quickly afterward.

## Why does the cédula take longer than people expect?

Approval is not the finish line — your cédula is. Cédula issuance runs {{fact:cedula.timeline}}, and until you have it, several practical things (full banking access, some contracts, some registrations) are harder or impossible. Treat the cédula as its own milestone with its own timeline, not something that happens automatically the moment you're approved — see the [full document checklist](/blog/documents-you-need-for-paraguay-residency) for what feeds into it.

## Where the timeline actually stalls

In order of how often we see it:

1. **A missing or incorrectly apostilled document**, discovered after filing rather than before — entirely preventable with the [document checklist](/blog/documents-you-need-for-paraguay-residency) done properly the first time.
2. **A translation that doesn't match the exact wording conventions Paraguayan authorities expect.** Not every certified translator abroad knows this.
3. **Waiting to open a bank account or handle other logistics** until after arrival, which turns out to depend on documents not brought or prepared in advance.
4. **Travel scheduling around assumptions** rather than around actual required appointments, adding unplanned gaps of weeks between steps.

None of these are about the government being slow. All four are sequencing errors that are completely avoidable once you know they're coming.
