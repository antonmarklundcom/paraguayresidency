"How long does it take?" is the first question almost everyone asks, and the honest answer has
two parts that get conflated constantly: the part you control (getting your documents ready)
and the part you do not (government processing). Most published timelines only describe the
second part, which is why they read faster than reality.

## The part you control: weeks 1–4

This is where the clock actually starts, and where it commonly stalls. Apostilled documents,
certified translations and background checks from your home country can take anywhere from
days to several weeks depending on which country you are coming from and how quickly you start.
Starting this in week one — before you have booked a flight, before you have decided on a
neighborhood — is the single biggest lever you have over your own timeline. People who start
document preparation only after arriving in Paraguay routinely add a month or more to their
process for no reason other than sequencing.

## Filing and the temporary stage

Once your documents are complete, filing for the initial stage is comparatively fast.
<Fact k="temporary.duration" /> — that is the standard first step for most applicants, and the
one <Fact k="permanent.presence_rule" /> eventually applies to once you move toward permanent
status.

## Cédula: the step people forget to plan around

Approval is not the finish line — your cédula is. <Fact k="cedula.timeline" />, and until you
have it, several practical things (banking with full limits, some contracts, some
registrations) are harder or impossible. Treat the cédula as its own milestone with its own
timeline, not an afterthought that happens automatically the moment you are approved.

## Where it actually stalls

In order of how often we see it:

1. **A missing or incorrectly apostilled document**, discovered after filing rather than
   before. This is entirely preventable with the [document checklist](/blog/documents-you-need-for-paraguay-residency)
   done properly the first time.
2. **A translation that does not match the exact wording conventions Paraguayan authorities
   expect.** Not every certified translator abroad knows this; not every generic translation
   service catches it.
3. **Waiting to open a bank account or handle other logistics** until after arrival, which
   turns out to depend on documents that were not brought or prepared in advance.
4. **Travel scheduling around the process** rather than around the actual required
   appointments, adding unplanned gaps of weeks between steps.

None of these are about the government being slow. All four are about sequencing errors that
are completely avoidable once you know they are coming — which is the entire point of writing
them down here instead of letting people discover them one at a time.

## A realistic range, not a promise

Given all of that, a realistic total range — from starting your documents to holding a cédula —
runs from a couple of months for someone who prepares everything in advance and has no
document issues, to considerably longer for someone who starts from zero after arriving and hits
one or two of the stalls above. Anyone who quotes you one specific number without knowing your
nationality, your document status and your travel plan is guessing, not informing you.
