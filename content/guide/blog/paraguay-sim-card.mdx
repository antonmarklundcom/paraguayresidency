Getting connected is one of the first small logistics tasks after landing,
and it's genuinely simple compared to the residency process itself — but a
few practical details save a wasted trip to a carrier store.

## The main carriers

Paraguay's mobile market is served by a small number of national carriers
offering prepaid and postpaid plans. Prepaid is by far the more common
starting choice for new arrivals, since it requires no local credit history
or long-term commitment while you're still settling in.

## What you'll typically need

- Your passport, which is generally accepted for a basic prepaid
  registration at a carrier store or authorized kiosk.
- Some cash or a card for the initial SIM and top-up purchase — prices are
  modest by international standards, though exact current pricing is worth
  checking in-store or on the carrier's own site rather than an old figure
  quoted online. [VERIFY current SIM and data-plan pricing directly with the
  carrier before travel, since these change.]
- Patience for the registration step itself, which can involve more paperwork
  at a carrier store than you'd expect from a similar purchase elsewhere —
  this is standard practice locally, not a sign of anything unusual.

## Where to buy one

Carrier stores and kiosks are common in shopping centers and central
commercial areas in Asunción and other major cities, including at the
international airport, which is the easiest option if you want to be
connected the moment you land.

## A small but useful tip

Keep your SIM active with periodic top-ups even during trips home — a
long-dormant prepaid line can be deactivated by the carrier, and reactivating
or replacing it is more hassle than a small top-up every so often. This
matters more for anyone maintaining a Paraguayan number as a stable contact
point through the residency process.
