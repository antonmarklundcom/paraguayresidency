More applications stall on documents than on anything else. Not because the list is long — it isn't — but because each document has to be the right version, from the right authority, with the right certification, issued within the right window, and matching every other document letter for letter. This chapter gives you the core list, then the specifics for the nationality groups we see most.

One rule to hold onto: **the DNM checklist is the authority, and it changes.** We keep this chapter current, but before you order anything, check the checklist version for your route (or ask whoever is filing for you). We note below where practice varies.

## The core document set (temporary residency, Ley 6984/2022)

**Brought from home:**

1. **Passport**, valid, with enough validity left to cover the process and your travel. Bring the original; copies are made and certified in Paraguay.
2. **Birth certificate**, the full or long-form version that names your parents, apostilled (or legalised) and translated into Spanish.
3. **Marriage certificate** if you are married or applying with a spouse, apostilled and translated. Divorce decrees if your names or status changed.
4. **Police certificate**, national level (not local or state), with a strict acceptance window: <Fact k="documents.police_certificate_validity" />. Young children below the age set in the DNM checklist are exempt.
5. **Proof of means** relevant to your category if your route asks for it at this stage (for conversion to permanent it is mandatory; see chapter 2).

**Obtained in Asunción:**

6. **Paraguayan police certificate** (antecedentes policiales) from the Policía Nacional: <Fact k="fees.police_certificate_py" />.
7. **Interpol certificate** from the Interpol office of the Policía Nacional, which takes your fingerprints: <Fact k="fees.interpol_certificate" />.
8. **Sworn Spanish translations** of every foreign-language document, by a Paraguayan registered public translator (traductor público matriculado).
9. **DNM forms**, a sworn declaration, and your address in Paraguay. DNM captures your photo and fingerprints when you file.

Older guides also list a medical certificate from a Paraguayan clinic. Check the current checklist for your category before paying for one.

## Apostille or legalisation?

Paraguay is <Fact k="apostille.since" />. If your document comes from another Convention member, an apostille from the competent authority in the issuing country is enough. If it does not, you need consular legalisation: the issuing country's foreign ministry, then the Paraguayan consulate. Every country in the groups below is an apostille country.

Three rules that catch people:

- **The apostille goes on the original official document**, or on a certified copy that the issuing authority accepts for apostille. An apostille on a photocopy you certified at a local shop is usually worthless.
- **Each document needs its own apostille.** The birth certificate's apostille does not cover the marriage certificate.
- **The police certificate clock starts at the issue date**, not the apostille date. If your country takes weeks to apostille, that time comes out of your window.

## Translations: do them in Paraguay

Every foreign-language document and its apostille must be translated into Spanish. We use Paraguayan registered public translators, because their translations are what DNM expects to see. A translation done abroad may be rejected or need its own apostille; don't assume otherwise. Documents already in Spanish — from Spain or Spanish-speaking Mercosur countries — need no translation.

Practical points:

- Send the translator **clean scans of the complete apostilled document**, both sides, as soon as you have them. They can start before you land.
- Ask for the translation to reproduce names **exactly** as they appear in your passport.
- Get the translator's quote from the full set, including apostille pages. Quotes are usually per page.

## By nationality group

The table is the quick reference; the notes below explain the traps.

### United States

The FBI report is the national-level certificate DNM wants; a state or local police report is not a substitute. Order it through the FBI or an approved channeler, then have the **US Department of State** apostille it — this federal step is often the slowest link, so start it first and track the police-certificate window. Birth and marriage records are apostilled by the **Secretary of State of the state that issued them**, not the state you live in now. If you have lived in another country in the relevant period, you also need that country's certificate.

US citizens: residency in Paraguay does not change your US tax obligations. Chapter 8 explains, and your US tax adviser confirms.

### United Kingdom

ACRO is the national-level certificate. The **FCDO Legalisation Office** issues apostilles; check its eligibility guidance for whether a document must first be certified by a solicitor or notary. Order the **full** birth certificate — the short version without parents' names is a common reason for a return trip. Scottish and Northern Irish records come from their own registers.

### Canada

Canada now issues apostilles; guides that describe embassy legalisation for Canadian documents are out of date. The RCMP check must be the fingerprint-based certified version. Federal documents go to Global Affairs Canada; birth and marriage certificates go to your province's competent authority where the province has one. French-language documents still need a Spanish translation.

### Australia

Ask for the **national** AFP check, not a state police check, and choose the purpose that fits immigration or overseas use. DFAT apostilles both the AFP check and state-issued civil records. Order the full birth certificate, not the extract.

### Germany and the rest of the EU

Germany and Paraguay only began recognising each other's apostilles in 2022; older advice telling you to legalise through the consulate no longer applies. Order the Führungszeugnis for use abroad and ask about the apostille at the same time. For other EU states, the pattern is the same: national criminal record extract, full civil records, apostille, translation. Multilingual EU standard forms are designed for use inside the EU; don't assume DNM will accept one without the underlying record.

### Spain

Spanish documents need no translation, which removes one step and one cost. Order the **certificado literal** (full) birth certificate rather than an extract, and apostille each document. The criminal record certificate can be requested online with digital ID.

### South Africa

The SAPS clearance is fingerprint-based and can take a long time to arrive. That makes the police-certificate window your main risk: plan backwards from your filing date, and don't book flights until the certificate is in your hand. Order the **unabridged** birth certificate; the abridged version does not name your parents. DIRCO apostilles most documents; confirm with them which court or office must sign first.

### Latin America and Mercosur

Nationals of the Mercosur list in chapter 2 usually apply under the Mercosur route. Spanish-language documents need no translation; Brazilian documents in Portuguese do. You can often enter Paraguay on your national ID card, but check what DNM wants to see as proof of legal entry for your route. Other Latin American nationals follow the standard route with the same apostille-and-translation logic.

## Names: the invisible killer

Every document must describe the same person in the same way. Before you apostille anything, lay out your passport, birth certificate, marriage certificate and police certificate side by side and check:

- full name order and spelling, including accents and hyphens
- middle names present or missing
- married versus birth surname
- date and place of birth in the same format

If anything differs, you need a document that bridges the gap (a marriage certificate, a deed poll, a name-change record), and that bridge document needs its own apostille and translation. Fixing this at home costs days. Fixing it in Asunción costs weeks.

## Lived somewhere else recently?

DNM asks for police certificates from any country you lived in during the relevant period — not only your country of nationality. A Briton who spent the last years in Portugal needs an ACRO certificate **and** a Portuguese one. Each is apostilled in its issuing country, translated in Paraguay, and bound by the same recency window.

## Common document rejections we see

- Police certificate from a local or state authority instead of the national one.
- Police certificate issued too long before filing.
- Short-form birth certificate without parents' names.
- Apostille on the wrong document, or on an uncertified photocopy.
- A translation done abroad, or one that spells your name differently from your passport.
- Missing certificate from a country you lived in recently.
- A surname change with no bridging document.
