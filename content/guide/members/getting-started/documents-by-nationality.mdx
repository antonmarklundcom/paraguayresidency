Every application rests on the same core documents — the differences between nationalities are
almost entirely about how those documents get authenticated, not which ones you need.

**The core set, for almost everyone:** a valid passport, a birth certificate, a police clearance
certificate from every country you have lived in for the last five years, and (if applicable) a
marriage certificate. All four need to be recent — most Paraguayan offices will not accept a
police certificate older than a few months.

**Apostille vs. legalisation:** if your home country is party to the Hague Apostille Convention,
a single apostille stamp is enough. If it is not, your documents need consular legalisation
instead — a slower, multi-step process through your embassy and the Paraguayan consulate. Check
which category your country falls into before you request anything; ordering the wrong
authentication is the single most common delay we see.

**Translation:** every document not already in Spanish needs a certified translation by a
registered Paraguayan translator (a "traductor público") once you arrive — a translation done in
your home country generally will not be accepted here, so do not pay for one before you land.

**A nationality-specific note:** citizens of some countries face additional background-check
requirements or longer police-certificate validity windows. This is exactly the kind of detail
that changes yearly — confirm your country's current requirement with us before you start
collecting documents, not after.
