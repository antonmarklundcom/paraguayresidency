There are really only three doors into Paraguayan residency for most readers of this guide: the standard temporary residency under Ley 6984/2022, the Mercosur agreement if you hold a qualifying nationality, and the new Investor Pass if you are making a qualifying investment. Everything else — permanent residency, the cédula, eventually citizenship — follows from the door you pick.

This chapter compares them honestly, explains what changed in 2026, and ends with a decision table. By the end you should know which route is yours and what you must prove at each stage.

## The short answer

If you are not a Mercosur national and you are not investing a large sum, you will almost certainly use **temporary residency**, then convert to **permanent** before the temporary card runs out. If you are a Mercosur national, the **Mercosur route** is the natural choice. If you are investing at the Investor Pass thresholds anyway, the **Investor Pass** takes you straight to permanent residency and skips the two-stage wait.

## Route 1: temporary residency (Ley 6984/2022)

This is the route most of our clients use.

**What you get.** A temporary residency of <Fact k="temporary.duration" />. You receive a resident card (carnet) from the migration authority (Dirección Nacional de Migraciones, DNM) and can then apply for a Paraguayan identity card, the cédula (chapter 6).

**What you need.** A valid passport, apostilled and translated civil documents (birth certificate, and marriage certificate if relevant), a police certificate that is <Fact k="documents.police_certificate_validity" />, a Paraguayan police certificate and an Interpol certificate obtained in Asunción, and the DNM forms. Chapter 3 goes through this by nationality.

**What it costs.** The DNM fee is <Fact k="fees.temporary_residency" />. Chapter 4 has the full worksheet.

**What it asks of you afterwards.** <Fact k="temporary.presence_rule" />. That is easy to meet if you live here or visit yearly. It is fatal if you file and disappear, because DNM Resolution 376/2026 now has the register swept twice a year and cancels lapsed residencies administratively.

**What it does not need.** Any investment, any job offer, or the old deposit — which is <Fact k="solvency.deposit_abolished" />. If someone is still quoting you a deposit for the standard route, their information is years out of date.

## Stage 2: converting to permanent residency

Temporary residency is designed to lead to permanent. The conversion is filed as a change of category, inside the window described above — near the end of the temporary card, not at any time you like. Miss the window and you are looking at starting again.

**The 2026 change.** Since DNM Resolution 407/2026 took effect in mid-2026, permanent residency requires <Fact k="solvency.requirement" />. Before that, many people converted with little more than their temporary card and clean records. Now you prove how you support yourself.

The categories in the resolution cover, broadly:

- professionals and technicians (a degree or qualification plus evidence you actually earn from it)
- employees (an employment contract and payslips)
- self-employed people and teleworkers or digital nomads (contracts, invoices, bank statements)
- property owners and company shareholders (title deeds, share records, income from them)
- farmers and agricultural producers
- ministers of religion
- retirees and pensioners (pension award letters and payment evidence)
- dependants of a qualifying applicant (chapter 9)
- students

There is no fixed amount of money you must show. What DNM looks for is evidence that is **documented, verifiable and consistent**: the same name, the same source, the same figures across your contract, your bank statements and your tax filings. In practice that often means having a RUC (the Paraguayan tax number, chapter 6) and apostilled income evidence from abroad.

**Our advice:** start building the evidence file on day one of your temporary residency, not in the last few months. Keep every pension statement, every client invoice, every bank statement showing income arriving.

**The fee.** The DNM fee for the permanent application is <Fact k="fees.permanent_residency" />.

**After approval.** Permanent residency is <Fact k="investorpass.validity_years" />. The card renewal costs <Fact k="fees.permanent_card_renewal" />. The presence rule relaxes to <Fact k="permanent.presence_rule" />.

## Route 2: Mercosur residency

If you hold a qualifying passport, this is your route: <Fact k="mercosur.residency_route" />.

The practical advantages are real: documents from Spanish-speaking Mercosur states need no translation, Mercosur nationals can often enter on their national ID card, and the DNM fee is <Fact k="fees.mercosur_residency" />. Brazilians need Spanish translations of Portuguese documents.

The disadvantage people miss: since mid-2026 the **same** proof-of-means rules under Res. 407/2026 apply to Mercosur conversions to permanent. The Mercosur route is no longer lighter on evidence at the conversion stage. And the conversion window is strict — miss it and you lose the right to convert under the Agreement.

Holding a second, non-Mercosur passport? You can usually choose which nationality to apply under. Pick the one whose documents are easiest to obtain and cleanest to match.

## Route 3: the Investor Pass

The Investor Pass is built on <Fact k="investorpass.legal_instrument" />. It gives direct access to permanent residency, skipping the temporary stage, for people investing <Fact k="investorpass.min_investment_usd" />. There are four investment routes (chapter 10 compares them in detail), a certificate issued by the Ministry of Industry and Commerce (MIC), and then the residency itself from DNM.

It is worth it when the investment makes sense on its own terms and skipping the temporary stage matters to you — for example, because you want permanent status settled before you start the clock toward citizenship, which requires <Fact k="citizenship.years" />. It is not worth it if the investment only exists to get a card that the standard route would have given you anyway.

## How to choose, step by step

1. **Check your nationality.** If you hold a Mercosur-list passport, start with the Mercosur route.
2. **Check your record.** Any conviction on any police certificate, from any country you have lived in during the relevant period, needs a conversation before you pay for anything.
3. **Ask whether you will invest anyway.** If you are already planning a productive business, a tourism project, a qualifying property used for business or a financial investment at the Investor Pass levels, compare the Investor Pass seriously.
4. **Map your next three years of travel.** Put it against the presence rules. If you can't meet them, no route will hold.
5. **Plan your conversion evidence now.** Which of the Res. 407/2026 categories will you fit in two years' time? What documents will prove it? Start that folder today.
6. **Decide who does the legwork.** Chapter 4 prices doing it yourself against having it done.

## Common route mistakes

- **Choosing a route by the word "permanent".** Permanent residency is not better for everyone. The temporary stage costs you little and gives you time to see if Paraguay suits you.
- **Assuming the Mercosur route is always easier.** It is easier at the start. At conversion, 2026 rules made it equivalent.
- **Buying property to qualify for the Investor Pass and living in it.** The real-estate route requires <Fact k="investorpass.route_real_estate_usd" />. Your own home does not count.
- **Treating the temporary card as a two-year holiday from paperwork.** It is two years of building a file for your conversion.

## If you are not ready to convert in time

Life happens. If your temporary card is heading for expiry and your conversion evidence isn't ready, the temporary residency can be extended: <Fact k="fees.temporary_extension" />. An extension buys time, but it doesn't remove the Res. 407/2026 requirement — you will still need the evidence at the end. Treat it as a fallback, not a plan, and ask about it well before the card expires rather than in the final weeks.

## Two passports, two options

If you hold more than one nationality, you usually choose which passport to apply under. Things to weigh:

- **Which nationality's documents are easiest to get inside the police-certificate window?** A fingerprint-based check with a slow federal apostille is harder to time than an online certificate with an electronic apostille.
- **Which set of documents matches most cleanly?** If one passport carries a married name and the other doesn't, the cleaner match wins.
- **Mercosur eligibility.** A Mercosur-list passport opens the Mercosur route.
- **Home-country tax and banking.** US citizens stay US persons for tax and banking whichever passport they use in Paraguay. Chapter 8 and chapter 7 apply either way.

Use the same passport for every step afterwards — residency, cédula, bank, RUC. Switching passports mid-process creates exactly the kind of data mismatch that stalls files.

## Changing route later

Routes are not a one-way door. A temporary resident who later makes a qualifying investment can look at the Investor Pass. A Mercosur national who moves under the standard route because of a second nationality can still convert to permanent in the usual way. What you cannot do is combine the benefits of two routes in one application. Pick the one that fits now, and revisit if your circumstances change.
