Paraguay belongs on your shortlist if you want a usable base and are willing to understand the trade-offs. It should not win simply because a residency advertisement makes it sound easy. The question we would ask before discussing paperwork is this: what would you actually do differently if your application were approved?

If your answer is live here, spend meaningful time here, or build a credible alternative home, there is something concrete to assess. If your answer is make every other country's obligations disappear, start again. Residency can support a plan; it cannot substitute for one.

## It suits you if

**You have income that can move with you.** Remote work, an established business or retirement income can give you room to settle without immediately finding local employment. But check that the arrangement really travels: your employer's permission, client contracts, working hours and access to payment services matter as much as your laptop. An income stream that depends on an address you will no longer have needs attention before the move.

**You value flexibility more than frictionless administration.** You can enjoy living somewhere while finding its paperwork frustrating. It helps if you can accommodate appointments, ask for clarification in Spanish and keep records without treating every unexpected request as a crisis. Professional help can reduce the burden; it cannot make public offices operate to your personal deadline.

**You are choosing an actual place to live.** Housing, neighbourhoods and daily routines deserve the same scrutiny as the residency route. A place that works during a relaxed scouting visit may feel different when you need to work, shop, attend appointments and get a child to school.

## It does not suit you if

**Your plan only works under a headline tax promise.** Our starting point is <Fact k="tax.territorial_rate" />. Read chapter 8, Taxes for residents, before treating that as a conclusion about your salary, company or investments. We would rather you discover that your assumptions need work now than after you have reorganised your life around them.

**You need a service you have not checked locally.** This is particularly important for specialist healthcare, accessibility, schooling and dependable connections to family abroad. Identify the actual provider and ask about your needs. A general assurance that a city has good hospitals or international schools is not enough to make a household decision.

**You cannot absorb uncertainty.** If your employer needs a definite return date or your current accommodation ends without flexibility, build a fallback before filing. A residency process with an uncertain finish is a poor foundation for commitments that cannot move.

## Separate the card from the life

Write down what success means under separate headings: immigration status, daily life, finances and family. Under immigration, you might want a lawful long-term base. Under daily life, you might need a quiet workspace and reliable transport. Under finances, you might want simpler administration. These are different outcomes, requiring different evidence.

A useful test is to imagine approval arriving tomorrow. Where would you sleep? How would you access money? Who would help if your phone stopped working? What would your partner do during the day? Those questions reveal gaps that a comparison of residency programmes will never show.

Chapter 2, The routes compared, helps you choose the legal path once the purpose is clear. Chapter 4, Costs, real ones, helps you price the whole transition. Neither should be used to talk yourself into a destination that fails an essential household requirement.

## Make the scouting visit answer questions

Treat a visit as research into ordinary life. Try the journey between likely housing and the places you would use. Work your usual hours from the accommodation. Test connectivity with your actual work tools, rather than relying on a speed-test screenshot supplied by a landlord.

Ask about lease terms, maintenance, water, cooling and what happens when something breaks. Visit potential schools or service providers with your own questions prepared. Keep separate notes for what you observed, what someone promised and what still needs checking. A pleasant conversation is useful, but it is not the same as a confirmed service.

Include your household in the evaluation. Someone who does not handle the residency paperwork may still bear most of the disruption. Chapter 9, Family, covers the application side; here the question is whether each person has a workable daily life.

## Be honest about a second-base plan

You may want residency without moving your whole life immediately. That can be a coherent objective, provided your travel pattern and ongoing responsibilities support it. <Fact k="permanent.presence_rule" /> Ask how the rule applies to your route and keep the answer with your travel records.

Do not equate maintaining immigration status with establishing tax residence or satisfying a bank's requirements. Those questions have their own evidence. Nor should you assume that a card held in reserve will make a future move effortless: documents, contact details and practical arrangements still need attention.

**Your decision at the end of this chapter:** write a short reason to proceed, the strongest reason to pause, and the missing evidence that could change your mind. If you cannot name a real benefit beyond the advertisement, pause. If the trade-offs are acceptable and the essentials check out, you have a sound basis for comparing routes.
