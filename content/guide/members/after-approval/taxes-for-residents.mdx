<Fact k="tax.territorial_rate" /> That is a starting point for a conversation, not a conclusion about your own income. The useful work is identifying your activities, where they happen and which jurisdictions need to assess them.

We want you to leave this chapter able to brief an adviser properly and recognise an answer that rests on missing facts. We do not want you to leave with a generic claim that you can paste over a salary, company, pension and investment portfolio as though they were the same thing.

## Separate the questions before discussing outcomes

Start with immigration permission, tax residence, income classification and administrative obligations as separate headings. A residency approval answers an immigration question. A RUC registration creates an administrative record. Neither should be used by itself to conclude that every tax question has been settled.

Ask the Paraguayan adviser what establishes your position locally and what evidence supports it. Ask an adviser familiar with your existing jurisdiction what changes when you leave, retain connections or continue activities there. If you need advice in another country, arrange it rather than expecting the immigration representative to cover it.

<Fact k="tax.foreign_income_treatment" /> Keep that qualification attached to the discussion. Where a payment arrives and the currency used do not, by themselves, provide enough information for an adviser to classify the underlying activity.

## Build an income map

List each stream separately: employment, freelance services, business distributions, rent, investments, pensions and proceeds from disposals where relevant. For each, explain who pays, why they pay, what you do to earn it, where you do that work and which entity or person owns the underlying asset.

Attach supporting records that explain the arrangement. A contract, invoice, payroll record or ownership document can reveal details that a label such as "overseas income" hides. Include anticipated changes after the move, not just the arrangement before departure.

A useful briefing might say: "I expect to perform my consulting work while living in Paraguay, under contracts with clients abroad." That gives the adviser a question to analyse. "All my clients are foreign, so nothing is taxable here" assumes the answer before the relevant work has been done.

## Give company arrangements their own review

If you own or manage a business, describe your role as well as the company's registration address. Explain where decisions will be made, who signs contracts, where staff work and how you receive money from the business.

Ask whether the proposed move changes the company's position, your personal position or both. Have the advisers identify which issues they are covering and which require specialist review. A personal residency service should not be mistaken for a review of a cross-border company structure.

Do not change invoicing, contracts or payment descriptions simply to fit a headline interpretation. Get the proposed arrangement reviewed first, then document any changes accurately. Chapter 7, Banking, explains why a consistent account of your activity matters operationally as well.

## Review what you are leaving behind

Prepare a factual account of your continuing connections: housing, family, employment, business responsibilities and expected travel. Tell the home-country adviser about citizenships and existing filing obligations. Ask what evidence they need before reaching a conclusion about departure.

The decision may depend on facts that immigration paperwork never asks about. Keep a record of travel and changes in living arrangements while they are easy to reconstruct. Do not wait until a filing question appears to piece together where you worked and lived.

Ask what remains to be filed or reported, whether any transition rules need attention and what event could change the advice. An adviser should be able to distinguish a conclusion from an assumption that you still need to satisfy.

## Turn advice into an operating plan

Request a written summary identifying the facts considered, conclusions reached, unresolved questions and actions assigned to you. Where advisers in different countries are involved, ask how their assumptions align. Conflicting assumptions can produce advice that looks sensible in isolation but fails as a combined plan.

For local administration, ask about required registrations, invoicing, record retention and filings. Have the accountant explain what applies to your activities, including what happens during periods with no activity. Chapter 6, Cédula and RUC, covers the practical registration handover.

Agree what records you will provide and how. Keep personal and business documentation organised so that the accountant can trace payments to the underlying transaction. Also agree how you will learn about a filing problem or missing information.

## Know when the answer needs revisiting

Advice based on remote employment may need review when you begin consulting independently. A change in where work is performed, a new business activity, an asset sale or a different travel pattern can make earlier assumptions incomplete.

Keep a short change log and send material changes to the relevant adviser before acting where possible. Ask for confirmation that the previous advice still applies rather than relying on silence.

**It suits you to proceed if:** you understand the advice, the evidence behind it and the administration you must maintain. **It does not suit you to proceed on a tax assumption if:** your only support is a residency salesperson's summary. The deliverable is a defensible plan for your actual life, not a slogan.
