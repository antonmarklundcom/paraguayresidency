This is the chapter people buy the guide for, and the one where bad information does the most damage. So we will be precise about Paraguay's side, which we deal with every week, and deliberately cautious about your home country's side, which depends on rules we don't practise. We are not tax advisers. We give you the Paraguayan framework and a briefing list for the adviser who looks at your whole situation.

Two words you will not find in this chapter are the ones used most in Paraguay marketing: a promise that you pay nothing. Paraguay has a real tax system. For many foreign residents it is a light one. That depends on where your income comes from — and on what your home country thinks.

## The territorial principle

Paraguay taxes income by its **source**, not by the taxpayer's nationality or residence. Under Ley 6380/2019, <Fact k="tax.foreign_income_treatment" />.

What counts as Paraguay-source is the key question. Broadly:

- work physically carried out in Paraguay for a Paraguayan payer is Paraguay-source
- rent from property in Paraguay is Paraguay-source
- interest, dividends and gains from Paraguayan entities and assets are Paraguay-source
- a pension paid from abroad, dividends from a foreign company you hold directly, and interest on a foreign bank account you hold directly are generally foreign-source

**The grey zone: remote work.** If you live in Paraguay and work for a foreign employer or foreign clients, the Paraguayan analysis turns on where the service is used and who pays for it. Many remote workers fall outside Paraguayan income tax on that income, but it depends on the facts. Ask a Paraguayan accountant (contador) to look at your specific contracts before you rely on it.

**The trap: routing investments through a Paraguayan company.** Ley 6380 treats certain foreign income — interest on deposits abroad, exchange differences, foreign dividends — as Paraguay-source **when it is earned by an entity constituted or resident in Paraguay**. If you set up a Paraguayan company and move your portfolio into it, you can bring into tax income that would have been outside it if you had held it personally. Take advice before structuring anything.

## Personal income tax (IRP)

If you have Paraguay-source income, the personal income tax (Impuesto a la Renta Personal, IRP) comes in two parts.

**Personal services** — salaries, fees and other income from work in Paraguay — are taxed on net income at <Fact k="tax.irp_brackets" />. IRP on personal services applies only once your gross annual income from those services exceeds a threshold; below it, you are not liable for IRP on that income, although other filings may still apply. Your accountant confirms the current threshold.

**Capital income** — rent, interest, dividends and capital gains from Paraguayan sources — is taxed at <Fact k="tax.capital_income_rate" />.

| Income type | Paraguayan treatment |
|---|---|
| Salary or fees for work in Paraguay | IRP on net income: <Fact k="tax.irp_brackets" /> |

| Foreign dividends and interest, held directly | Generally foreign-source (see the company trap above) |

## Business income (IRE)

If you run a business through a Paraguayan company or as a registered sole trader, profits are taxed under IRE at <Fact k="tax.ire_rate" />. There are simplified regimes for small businesses, with different bookkeeping and payment rules; which one fits depends on turnover and activity, and your accountant should choose it with you.

**Dividends.** When a Paraguayan company pays out profits, the dividend is taxed at <Fact k="tax.dividends" />. The company-level tax and the dividend tax together make the effective rate on distributed profit higher than the headline IRE rate. Model it before you choose a structure.

## VAT (IVA)

Paraguay has a value-added tax (IVA) on most sales of goods and services. As a consumer you pay it in prices. If you register a RUC and invoice, you may have to charge it and file monthly returns. Electronic invoicing is being rolled out; your accountant will tell you what applies to your activity.

## Your obligations as a resident

- **No income in Paraguay, no RUC:** many retirees and remote workers in this position have no Paraguayan filing obligation at all. Confirm it with an accountant once, in writing.
- **With a RUC:** you have filing obligations for the taxes attached to your declared activity, **including months with no income**. Missed filings produce fines and block tax certificates.
- **Paraguay-source income above the IRP threshold:** annual IRP return.
- **A Paraguayan company:** bookkeeping, monthly and annual returns, and a Paraguayan accountant in practice.

## The tax residency certificate

DNIT issues a certificate of tax residency <Fact k="tax.residency_certificate" />. Chapter 6 covers the practicalities. The certificate is evidence that Paraguay treats you as resident. **It does not, by itself, end your tax residency anywhere else.**

## Your home country: the questions to take to your adviser

This is the half of the picture we can't answer for you. Moving to Paraguay changes your tax position only if your home country's rules say it does. Below are the questions that come up most often by nationality. Each one ends the same way: **confirm with your own adviser.**

**United States.** The US taxes its citizens and green-card holders on worldwide income wherever they live. Paraguayan residency does not change that. Ask about the foreign earned income exclusion, foreign tax credits, FBAR and FATCA reporting on foreign accounts, and how a Paraguayan company would be treated. Confirm with your own adviser.

**United Kingdom.** UK tax residence is decided by the statutory residence test, based on days and ties, not by a foreign residency card. Ask about split-year treatment, the rules for people who return within a few years, the treatment of UK-source income such as rent and pensions, and the recent changes to the regime for foreign income. Confirm with your own adviser.

**Canada.** Leaving Canada can trigger a deemed disposition ("departure tax") on certain assets. Residential ties such as a home, a spouse or dependants in Canada can keep you resident. Ask about the timing of your departure and reporting of foreign property. Confirm with your own adviser.

**Australia.** Residency for tax is a facts-based test. Ask about the domicile test, capital gains on ceasing residence, superannuation access and withholding on Australian-source income. Confirm with your own adviser.

**Germany.** Ask about the rules for people who move to a country with lower taxation while keeping economic interests in Germany, the exit tax on substantial shareholdings, and whether you keep a residence (Wohnsitz) in Germany. Confirm with your own adviser.

**Spain.** Ask about the rules that can keep Spanish nationals taxable for several years after moving to a jurisdiction Spain treats as low-tax, and about the exit tax on large shareholdings. Confirm with your own adviser.

**South Africa.** Ask about ceasing residence (and the deemed disposal on exit), the financial emigration changes, and exchange-control approvals for moving funds. Confirm with your own adviser.

**LatAm and Mercosur.** Several countries in the region tax worldwide income of residents, and some have specific rules for people who move to countries they classify as preferential. Ask how your country classifies Paraguay. Confirm with your own adviser.

## A briefing sheet for your adviser

Before the meeting, write one page with:

1. Your citizenships and every country where you have a home available to you.
2. Your expected days in each country for the next two tax years.
3. Each income source: type, payer, country of payer, where the work is done, amount.
4. Each asset: type, location, how held (personally or via a company).
5. Family: where your spouse and children live and go to school.
6. What you plan to set up in Paraguay: RUC, company, property, bank accounts.
7. The specific question: "When do I stop being tax resident at home, and what do I owe on the way out?"

An adviser with that page can give you a real answer. An adviser asked "is Paraguay tax-efficient?" can only give you a generic one.

## Four profiles, Paraguay's side only

These are illustrations of how the Paraguayan rules apply, not advice for your case.

| Profile | Paraguay-source income? | Paraguayan position, broadly |
|---|---|---|
| Retiree on a foreign pension, no local income | No | Generally outside Paraguayan income tax; often no RUC needed. |
| Remote worker for a foreign employer | Depends on the facts | Often outside Paraguayan income tax; confirm with a contador on your contracts. |
| Owner of a rented flat in Asunción | Yes — the rent | Capital income: <Fact k="tax.capital_income_rate" />, with a RUC and filings. |
| Owner of a Paraguayan trading company | Yes — company profits | IRE on profits (<Fact k="tax.ire_rate" />), then tax on dividends paid out: <Fact k="tax.dividends" />. |

In every row, your home country may see things differently. That is the next section.
