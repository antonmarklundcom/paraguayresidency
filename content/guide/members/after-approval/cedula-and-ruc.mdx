Approval is a milestone, but it is not a complete administrative handover. Your residency decision, cédula and RUC serve different purposes. This chapter helps you leave the process with accurate documents, working access and a clear owner for anything still outstanding.

The cédula de identidad is the Paraguayan identity card. The RUC, Registro Único del Contribuyente, is the taxpayer registration identifier. Do not use those names interchangeably or assume that possessing either answers every question about your immigration or tax position.

## Start with the approval file

Ask for the decision or approval document, any accompanying instructions and confirmation of what happens next. Check your name, identity details and the status granted against the application. If something is wrong, ask how to correct it before that discrepancy is copied into another record.

Keep an organised copy of the filing evidence and decision. Ask which originals you must retain and which documents should accompany the next application. A message saying "approved" is welcome news; you still need the actual record and an explanation of its practical effect.

Record who is responsible for initiating the cédula step. If a service ends at residency approval, make the next arrangement explicitly. Otherwise, each person can assume that someone else is handling it while nothing moves.

## Prepare the cédula step

Cédula timing: <Fact k="cedula.timeline" />. Ask for the requirements and attendance arrangements applicable to your case. Do not turn a general post-approval sequence into a promise that every appointment or collection can be combined.

Confirm the identity details to be used, required supporting documents and how you will learn that the card is available. If travel is involved, distinguish an estimate from a confirmed appointment or collection instruction. Chapter 5, Timeline, week by week, explains how to plan around those dependencies.

Before leaving an appointment, ask what evidence you receive, whether anything remains outstanding and how a problem will be communicated. Keep the reference information somewhere accessible without relying on a single phone or message thread.

## Check the card before relying on it

When you receive the cédula, compare the details with your supporting records. Look for spelling, date and other identity errors. Report a discrepancy through the advised process rather than assuming that a bank or another office will overlook it.

Store a secure copy and ask about replacement arrangements if it is lost. Use the identity document requested for the transaction at hand; do not assume the cédula replaces your passport for travel or every cross-border purpose.

A card can make local administration easier without obliging another institution to accept your application. Chapter 7, Banking, explains the separate account-opening assessment. Carrying an identity card and satisfying a bank's documentation requirements are related but different tasks.

## Decide what RUC work your situation needs

Discuss your intended activities with an accountant before treating registration as another automatic box to tick. Explain whether you will work, invoice clients, operate a business, receive particular income or simply establish a home. Ask whether you need registration and which activities and obligations should be recorded.

The [DNIT RUC information page](https://www.dnit.gov.py/en/web/portal-institucional/registro-%C3%BAnico-de-contribuyentes-ruc-) is the official starting point for registration guidance. Your accountant should identify the procedure and identity evidence appropriate to you.

Do not assume that RUC registration is a certificate proving you have ceased to be taxable elsewhere. Chapter 8, Taxes for residents, owns that analysis. The purpose here is to ensure that the administrative record accurately reflects the position your adviser has assessed.

The timing is separate from immigration approval: <Fact k="tax.timeline" />. Ask what information can be prepared now and what depends on an earlier step.

## Make the accountant's handover usable

Ask for a copy of the completed registration and a plain-language explanation of the registered activities, effective dates and obligations. Have the accountant identify what you must report, how you will provide records and who submits any required filings.

Make sure your contact information is correct and you understand how to receive official messages. Arrange legitimate access to your own account and recovery methods. Do not leave your only route to essential records inside a former representative's inbox.

Agree how changes are handled. A new activity, address or working arrangement may require review. Ask what you should tell the accountant and how to confirm that an update has been completed. Keep passwords and access credentials out of routine document bundles.

## Close the loop before calling the setup finished

Review the remaining tasks against chapter 12, Checklists. You should have accurate approval and identity records, a clear RUC decision, the evidence of any registration completed and an agreed plan for ongoing administration.

**It suits you to handle parts yourself if:** you can follow the procedure, keep the records and recognise when a question needs professional interpretation. **It does not suit you if:** you are ticking forms without understanding the activities or obligations being registered.

Our preferred finish is simple: you can find the documents, explain what each does and name the next person to contact. That is what turns approval into a usable foundation.
