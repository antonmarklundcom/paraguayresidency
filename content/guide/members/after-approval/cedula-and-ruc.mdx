Residency approval gives you a resident card from DNM. Useful, but in daily life almost nobody asks for it. What banks, landlords, phone companies, notaries and the tax office ask for is the **cédula de identidad** — the Paraguayan identity card with your ID number. After that comes the **RUC**, your tax number, which you need if you will invoice, run a company, prove income for permanent residency, or request a tax residency certificate.

This chapter covers both, in order, with what to bring to each office.

## The cédula: what it is and why it matters

The cédula is issued by the Departamento de Identificaciones of the Policía Nacional, not by DNM. Foreign residents receive one with the same format as Paraguayans. With it you can:

- open a bank account (chapter 7)
- sign a lease or buy property in your own name without extra identity paperwork
- get a phone contract, register for utilities, enrol children with less friction
- register for a RUC

## When to apply

Apply once your residency is approved and your resident card is issued. Don't let it sit: agencies report that Identificaciones expects the application within months of the residency resolution, and every week of delay pushes back your bank account and RUC. We plan trip 2 (chapter 5) around collecting the resident card and filing for the cédula in the same visit.

## What to bring to Identificaciones

Bring originals and copies of:

- [ ] Passport (the one you used for the residency application)
- [ ] Resident card (carnet) and the DNM residency resolution
- [ ] Birth certificate, apostilled, with its Paraguayan sworn translation
- [ ] Marriage certificate, apostilled and translated, if married
- [ ] Paraguayan police certificate (antecedentes), current — <Fact k="fees.police_certificate_py" />
- [ ] Interpol certificate, current — <Fact k="fees.interpol_certificate" />
- [ ] A Paraguayan address and phone number

Your police and Interpol certificates from the residency application may be too old by the time you apply for the cédula. Ask before trip 2 whether you need fresh ones; if so, get them on day one.

Identificaciones takes your photo, fingerprints and signature. Check every letter of your name and your date of birth on the form before you sign. A typo on the cédula will follow you into bank records, tax records and property deeds, and correcting it is a separate process.

## Step by step

1. **Confirm the resident card is ready** before you fly.
2. **Refresh police and Interpol certificates** if needed, on arrival.
3. **Attend Identificaciones** with the document set above. Queues form early.
4. **Check the data on the form** word by word.
5. **Keep the receipt** with your file number. It proves the cédula is in process.
6. **Collect the cédula** when it is ready, in person or through a representative where that is accepted. Some offices accept a notarised power of attorney; confirm the current practice first.

## The 2026 reality

Identificaciones has been slow in 2026, partly because of volume and partly because of the enhanced Interpol verification with countries of origin. The official figure is <Fact k="cedula.timeline" />. Plan your banking and any purchase around the cédula being late rather than early.

## The RUC: your tax number

The RUC (Registro Único de Contribuyentes) is issued by DNIT, the national tax authority that replaced the old SET. It is your identity in the tax system.

**Do you need one?** You do if you will:

- invoice clients in Paraguay or run a Paraguayan business
- register a company or act as a company officer
- prove income for permanent residency — the 2026 rules under Res. 407/2026 make a RUC with a filing history strong evidence for several categories
- request a Paraguayan tax residency certificate
- satisfy a bank that wants to see your tax registration

You probably don't need one immediately if you are a retiree living on a foreign pension with no Paraguayan income and no need for a tax residency certificate. Discuss it with an accountant (contador) anyway, because a RUC is easier to set up early than to explain later.

**Registering.** Registration is done with DNIT, largely through its online system (Marangatu), once you have your cédula. You will declare an economic activity, an address and contact details. Choosing the activity matters: it decides which taxes and filings apply to you. That is a conversation for a Paraguayan accountant, not a guess on a form.

**What to bring (or have scanned):**

- [ ] Cédula
- [ ] Proof of address in Paraguay (a utility bill or lease)
- [ ] Email address and Paraguayan phone number
- [ ] Description of the activity you will carry out
- [ ] Your accountant's details, if they will file for you

**After registration.** A RUC comes with filing obligations, even for months when you earn nothing. Missed filings produce fines and block certificates. Most foreign residents with a RUC use an accountant for the monthly and annual filings. Build that cost into your budget.

## The tax residency certificate

DNIT issues a certificate of tax residency. It is issued <Fact k="tax.residency_certificate" />. In practice DNIT also looks at your record of entries and exits (the constancia de movimiento migratorio from DNM) for the fiscal year. The certificate is issued per fiscal year.

**Who needs it.** People who need to show a foreign tax authority or bank that they are tax resident in Paraguay — for example, to rely on a change of tax residence at home or to update a bank's tax-residence records. **Whether a Paraguayan certificate is enough for your home country is a question for your home-country adviser.** Many countries apply their own tests regardless of what another country certifies. Chapter 8 goes further.

**Myths we hear.**

- "The certificate makes me non-resident at home." It doesn't. Your home country decides that under its own rules.
- "Just spend a set number of days here and it is automatic." It isn't automatic. DNIT applies its own documentary requirements, and your home country applies its own tests.
- "Anyone with a residency card can get one." You need the cédula, an active RUC and the supporting evidence.

## Order of operations

| Step | Needs | Gives you |
|---|---|---|
| Residency approved | DNM file | Resident card and resolution |
| Cédula | Resident card, civil documents, fresh certificates | Paraguayan ID number |
| RUC | Cédula, address, activity | Tax number and filing obligations |
| Bank account | Cédula, often RUC or income evidence | Local banking (chapter 7) |
| Tax residency certificate | Cédula, active RUC, migration record | Per-year certificate for foreign use |

## Collecting through a representative

If you can't stay in Paraguay until the cédula is ready, ask in advance whether someone else can collect it for you. Where Identificaciones accepts a representative, it usually expects a power of attorney signed before a Paraguayan notary (escribano), or one signed abroad, apostilled and translated. Practice changes, so confirm the current requirement before you leave the country rather than after.

A power of attorney is a serious document. Limit it to the specific task — collecting the cédula, or registering the RUC — and give it only to someone you trust or a provider accountable to you in writing.

## Common cédula problems

- **Data mismatch.** The form uses the passport's name order and the birth certificate uses another. Resolve it at filing, not when the card arrives.
- **Certificates out of date.** Fresh police or Interpol certificates needed; see above.
- **Missing translation.** A document was translated for DNM but the translation wasn't brought to Identificaciones.
- **Lost receipt.** Without the file number, tracking your cédula is slow. Photograph the receipt.
- **Waiting with no news.** Ask whoever follows your file to check status; don't assume silence means progress.
