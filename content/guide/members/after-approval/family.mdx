A household can plan together without assuming every person has the same application. Each family member brings their own identity, nationality, civil records and practical needs. Our aim is to make those differences visible early enough that the household can choose a workable sequence.

This chapter covers the coordination that an individual checklist misses. Chapter 3, Documents, apostilles and translations, by nationality, explains the document chain; chapter 2, The routes compared, explains the route decision. Here, we connect those decisions across the family.

## Describe the household before choosing the filing plan

List everyone who may apply, their citizenships, current place of residence and relationship to the other applicants. Include different surnames, previous marriages, adoption, guardianship or other circumstances that affect how relationships are documented.

Ask the adviser to confirm the basis for each person's application. Do not assume that a spouse automatically follows the lead applicant under identical conditions or that an adult relative qualifies as a dependent. Household planning and individual eligibility need to be reconciled explicitly.

**It suits you to prepare together if:** the relationships and routes have been reviewed and shared timing helps everyone. **It does not suit you to force a joint schedule if:** an unresolved case would make the whole household travel before it is ready.

## Give each person a file

Keep separate records of identity documents, required certificates, authentication, translation and outstanding questions. Add a shared section for relationship evidence, but mark which individual files depend on it.

A marriage certificate may be relevant to several tasks, yet that does not mean the same physical original can be in different offices at once. Ask how many accepted copies are needed and who retains the original. Confirm the document handling before dispatching it.

Use the person's name on each task rather than a household-wide tick. "Documents complete" can hide that a child's record is still being corrected while the adults' files are ready. Ask for a readiness review by person, then a household review of what can proceed together.

## Spouses: show the relationship accurately

Check how the marriage is recorded and how the names connect to current passports. If a document reflects a former surname, ask what evidence bridges the change. If a previous marriage affects the current records, disclose it before the checklist is finalised.

Do not assume that all relationship formats are treated identically under the proposed route. Describe your actual circumstances and request the applicable evidence requirements. A candid early question is easier to resolve than a certificate ordered for the wrong purpose.

Discuss whether both adults will apply and travel together. If plans differ, ask what that changes for each application, the shared documents and the household's practical arrangements. Keep the answers attached to the individual files.

## Children: resolve authority and attendance early

Where a child is applying, identify who has legal authority to make the relevant decisions and whether another parent's consent or judicial documentation may be needed. Explain custody, guardianship, adoption or an absent parent accurately. Do not treat a routine travel permission as automatically sufficient for residence paperwork.

The [Migraciones Mercosur permanent-residence requirements](https://migraciones.gov.py/residencia-permanente-mercosur/) illustrate why a child's representation and supporting authority need specific attention. Have the adviser identify the requirements for your child's actual route rather than copying a provision from another category.

Ask who must attend, which documents must be original and what can be arranged before travel. If a parent cannot attend, obtain the exact proposed solution for review before signing or authenticating anything. A broadly worded authorisation may not cover the act the receiving office requires.

## Older dependents need an explicit route answer

For adult children, parents or other relatives, ask for an individual eligibility assessment. Financial dependence in everyday life does not itself establish the immigration category. If someone needs a separate route, include its documents, attendance and cost in the household plan.

Also distinguish immigration support from care needs. If a relative depends on medication, accessibility or regular clinical support, verify the practical arrangements before choosing a move date. Chapter 1, Why Paraguay (and why not), provides the broader fit test; this is where it affects your family's schedule.

Do not let the easiest application dictate a timetable that the person with the greatest support needs cannot manage.

## Coordinate money, travel and the handover

Ask which professional work is shared and which is charged per applicant. Our family-service reference is <Fact k="pricing.family" />. Chapter 4, Costs, real ones, helps separate document preparation, travel and later setup from that service scope.

Arrange appointment-day logistics: childcare, transport, access to originals and an adult who can respond if a document issue arises. Avoid placing an essential school or housing commitment on an unconfirmed approval date. Ask whether staged travel is feasible if files progress differently.

After approval, check each person's status and next steps separately. Chapter 6, Cédula and RUC, should be applied according to each person's circumstances, not copied automatically across the household.

**Your finished household plan:** every applicant has an identified route, a document owner, confirmed attendance needs and an individual next step. Shared planning then becomes an advantage instead of a way for missing details to disappear.
