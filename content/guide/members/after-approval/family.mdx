Most families we help underestimate one thing: every person is a separate application. The paperwork multiplies, the fees multiply, and the chance of a name mismatch multiplies with it. The good news is that it doesn't take proportionally longer — if the documents are ready, a family files in the same visit as a single person.

This chapter covers who can come with you, what each person needs, the documents that prove the relationships, the rules for children, and the practical side: schools, health care and daily life.

## Who counts as family for residency

- **Spouse.** A married spouse can apply alongside you, with an apostilled and translated marriage certificate.
- **Unmarried partner.** A registered civil partnership or a legally recognised union from your home country can be used if it is documented. An unregistered relationship is harder: in practice your partner applies in their own right.
- **Minor children.** Children apply with their parents, with birth certificates naming both parents.
- **Adult children and parents.** They usually apply in their own right, on their own route and with their own evidence. At the permanent stage, the 2026 rules include a dependants category (see below); whether an adult relative fits it depends on the evidence.

On the Investor Pass, the investor's spouse and minor children are covered (chapter 10).

## What each person needs

| Document | Main applicant | Spouse | Child |
|---|---|---|---|
| Passport | Yes | Yes | Yes, their own |
| Birth certificate, full version, apostilled and translated | Yes | Yes | Yes, naming both parents |
| Marriage certificate, apostilled and translated | Yes | Yes (same document) | — |
| National police certificate | Yes | Yes | Only from the age set in the DNM checklist |
| Certificates from other countries lived in | Yes | Yes | If applicable by age |
| Paraguayan police and Interpol certificates | Yes | Yes | By age — confirm per child |
| DNM fee | <Fact k="fees.temporary_residency" /> | Same, per person | Same, per person |

Each police certificate must be <Fact k="documents.police_certificate_validity" />. For a family, that means coordinating several certificates so they are all inside the window on the same filing day. Order them together.

## Proving the relationships

DNM needs to see how everyone is connected. The chain is:

1. **Marriage certificate** connects you and your spouse.
2. **Each child's birth certificate** connects the child to both parents, with names exactly matching the parents' passports.
3. **Name-change documents** bridge any difference — a married surname, a divorce, a later change.

Before you apostille anything, write every family member's name exactly as it appears in each document, in a grid. Any cell that differs needs explaining with a document. This is the single most common reason family files get stuck.

**Blended families.** If a child's other parent is not moving with you, you need to show you are entitled to relocate the child. That usually means:

- a notarised, apostilled consent from the other parent authorising residency in Paraguay, **or**
- a court order granting you custody or permission to relocate
- plus the other parent's details as they appear on the child's birth certificate

Without that, don't file for the child. Resolve it at home first.

**Adopted children.** Bring the adoption order, apostilled and translated, together with the birth certificate that names the adoptive parents.

## Travelling with children

Paraguay, like its neighbours, pays close attention to minors travelling across borders. A child travelling with only one parent may be asked for a notarised authorisation from the other. Carry it, apostilled and translated, every time — not just when you file.

## The 2026 rules and families

**Presence.** Each family member holds their own residency and must meet the presence rule individually: <Fact k="temporary.presence_rule" /> while temporary, and <Fact k="permanent.presence_rule" /> once permanent. If one parent works abroad and rarely visits, their residency is the one at risk.

**Proof of means at conversion.** Under DNM Res. 407/2026, permanent residency requires <Fact k="solvency.requirement" />. Family members who don't have their own income usually use the **dependants** category, relying on the main applicant's evidence. That makes the main applicant's income file more important: it has to support the whole household. Plan who will be the main applicant for each family member before you convert.

**Timing.** Convert everyone together. If the main applicant's permanent residency is approved but a dependant's temporary card expires first, the dependant is exposed.

## Costs for a family

Every per-person government fee in chapter 4 multiplies by the number of applicants. The lines that don't multiply are the ones you share: flights for the adults already travelling, accommodation, local transport. Translations multiply by pages, not people, so a single marriage certificate is translated once.

If you want help, family scope is quoted separately: <Fact k="pricing.family" />.

## Schools

Asunción has private bilingual and international schools, plus the public system. What to know:

- **Enrolment documents.** Schools ask for the child's passport, residency status (a filing receipt or resident card), birth certificate, and previous school records.
- **School records from abroad** usually need apostille and translation, and a recognition process (convalidación) with the Ministry of Education (MEC) so your child can be placed in the right grade. Start this before you arrive.
- **The school year.** Paraguay's school year runs roughly with the calendar year, so the southern-hemisphere calendar can mean a half-year offset for families arriving from the north. Ask the school about placement.
- **Language.** Bilingual schools teach in Spanish and English; in the public system, Spanish and Guaraní are both taught.

Visit schools on your scouting trip, ask about waiting lists and fees, and get the enrolment requirements in writing.

## Health care

Most foreign families use private health care in Asunción through a private health plan (medicina prepaga). Plans vary in coverage, waiting periods for pre-existing conditions and age limits. Compare several, and read the exclusions. The public system (and IPS, the social security health service, for those who contribute through formal employment) exists but is not what most newcomers rely on.

For specific needs — a child with a medical condition, a pregnancy, an elderly parent — identify the specialist and hospital before you move, not after.

## Daily life for the whole household

Chapter 1 asked whether each person has a workable daily life. For families that means:

- a trailing spouse with something to do: work that travels, Spanish lessons, community
- a school run that works with Asunción's traffic
- after-school activities in a language the child can follow
- contacts for babysitting, a pediatrician and a dentist

These are not immigration questions, but they decide whether the move lasts.

## Step by step: filing as a family

1. **Decide the main applicant** for each family member, now and at conversion.
2. **Build the name grid** and order bridging documents.
3. **Order civil documents** for everyone, full versions, and apostille them.
4. **Resolve custody and consent** for any child whose other parent isn't moving.
5. **Order police certificates together** for every adult, timed to the same filing date.
6. **Send scans** of everything to the translator; ask for one consistent spelling of every name.
7. **Plan one Interpol visit** for everyone who needs it.
8. **File together at DNM**, one application per person, one fee per person.
9. **Apply for cédulas together** on trip 2, including children.
10. **Convert together** to permanent, with the main applicant's evidence covering dependants.

## Having a baby in Paraguay

A child born in Paraguay is Paraguayan by birth under the Constitution, and is registered with the civil registry (Registro Civil). That child then holds Paraguayan nationality in their own right. Register the birth with your own country's consulate too, so the child has your nationality documented and a passport of their own. Your home country's rules on passing on nationality to a child born abroad apply; check them before the birth.

## Pets

Pets are family too, and bringing them needs its own paperwork. SENACSA, the animal health authority, sets the import requirements: typically a vaccination record, a health certificate from your country's official veterinary service and microchip identification. Airlines add their own rules on crates and temperatures, and Paraguay's summer heat affects when animals can fly in the hold. Start with your vet several months before the move.

## Your next step

Make the name grid first: one row per person, one column per document. Then list the relationship documents you need, including any custody or consent paperwork, and order them together so every police certificate lands in the same window. If both parents are moving but only one will be in Paraguay often, reread the presence rules — that is the residency most likely to lapse.
