These are not rare edge cases — they are the same handful of mistakes, on a rotation, every
month.

**Translating documents before arrival.** Covered in chapter 3, worth repeating: a translation
done outside Paraguay by a translator not registered here generally has to be redone. Wait until
you land.

**Letting a police certificate expire mid-process.** Most offices want a certificate issued
within the last few months. If your other documents take time to authenticate, request the
police certificate last, not first.

**Assuming "permanent" means "no presence requirement."** <Fact k="permanent.presence_rule" />
Confirm what applies to your specific route before you plan a life that depends on rarely being
in the country.

**Hiring based on price alone.** The cheapest filing help is sometimes the most expensive if a
mistake means resubmitting documents or restarting a stalled case. Ask what happens if something
goes wrong, not just what the base fee covers.

**Believing a "guaranteed" timeline.** Nobody controls a government office's queue. Anyone who
promises an exact date, rather than a realistic range, is telling you what you want to hear.

**Skipping the tax conversation.** Chapter 8 exists because "territorial tax" gets oversimplified
into "no tax" constantly. Confirm your own situation before you make a financial decision based
on a misunderstanding.
