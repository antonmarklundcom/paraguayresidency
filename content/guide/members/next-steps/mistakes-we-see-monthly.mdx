We see the same mistakes month after month. Not because applicants are careless — most are meticulous people — but because the process punishes things nobody tells you in advance. This chapter lists them in the order you would meet them, with the fix for each. If you read only one chapter twice, make it this one.

## Before you file

**1. Ordering the police certificate too early.** The certificate must be <Fact k="documents.police_certificate_validity" />. People order it first because it feels like the most important document, then spend weeks on apostilles and flights, and arrive with a certificate that has expired for filing purposes. **Fix:** order it last, timed backwards from your filing date (chapter 5).

**2. The wrong police certificate.** A state, provincial or local police check instead of the national one. A name-only check where the national service is fingerprint-based. **Fix:** use the national certificate listed for your country in chapter 3.

**3. Forgetting other countries you lived in.** DNM wants certificates from every country of residence in the relevant period, not just your nationality. **Fix:** list every country you have lived in and check each one against the rule.

**4. Short-form birth certificates.** Without parents' names, it doesn't prove what DNM needs, and for children it doesn't connect them to you. **Fix:** order the full or long-form version.

**5. Apostilles on the wrong thing.** On a photocopy you certified locally, on the wrong document, or from the wrong authority (the state you live in rather than the state that issued the record, for US documents). **Fix:** follow the apostille authority column in chapter 3's table.

**6. Name mismatches.** A middle name missing on one document, a married surname on the passport and a birth surname on the certificate, an accent dropped. **Fix:** lay every document side by side before you apostille anything, and get bridging documents for every difference.

**7. Translations done abroad.** Or done in Paraguay from bad scans, or spelling your name differently from your passport. **Fix:** a Paraguayan registered public translator, clean scans, and a check against your passport before you file.

**8. Booking flights before the documents are reviewed.** It feels productive. It is the most expensive mistake on this list when something turns out to be wrong. **Fix:** have complete scans reviewed, then book.

## At the offices

**9. Arriving without cash.** Some fees are cash only — the Interpol certificate is <Fact k="fees.interpol_certificate" />. **Fix:** withdraw guaraníes on arrival.

**10. Paying unofficial "fees".** Any extra payment to speed something up, without an official receipt, is not an official fee. **Fix:** pay only the official amount, and always get the receipt.

**11. Filing with a missing page.** DNM won't accept an incomplete file. You go back to the queue, sometimes another day. **Fix:** check every item against the checklist the night before (chapter 12).

**12. Bringing only copies.** Officials want originals to compare against copies. **Fix:** originals in a folder, copies in another.

**13. Leaving without the filing receipt.** It proves your application is in process — useful for schools, landlords and border questions. **Fix:** keep it with your passport.

## During review

**14. Not answering DNM.** A query from DNM that sits unanswered stalls your file. **Fix:** make sure the contact details on your file work, and check them.

**15. Planning around a promised date.** Processing is <Fact k="residency.timeline" />. Anyone who promises a specific date is guessing. **Fix:** plan around a range, with slack on both ends.

## After approval

**16. Letting approval sit.** The resident card waits; the cédula clock hasn't started; banks won't open. **Fix:** plan trip 2 as soon as you have approval (chapter 5).

**17. Stale certificates at Identificaciones.** The police and Interpol certificates from filing may be too old for the cédula. **Fix:** ask before you fly and get fresh ones on day one if needed.

**18. Typos on the cédula form.** They follow you into every record. **Fix:** read every letter before you sign.

**19. Assuming the cédula comes in weeks.** The official figure is <Fact k="cedula.timeline" />. **Fix:** plan banking and purchases for later.

**20. Opening a RUC without an accountant.** Then missing monthly filings for months with no income. Fines follow. **Fix:** don't register an activity you won't file for; if you register, use an accountant.

## The 2026 mistakes

These are the new ones, and we expect to see them more over the next two years.

**21. Filing and disappearing.** <Fact k="temporary.presence_rule" />. Since DNM Res. 376/2026, the register is swept twice a year and lapsed residencies are cancelled administratively. **Fix:** put an annual entry in your calendar. If you know you'll be away longer, ask DNM for authorisation before you go.

**22. Assuming permanent status can't be lost.** <Fact k="permanent.presence_rule" />. **Fix:** same calendar entry, less often.

**23. Leaving conversion evidence to the last months.** Under Res. 407/2026, permanent residency needs <Fact k="solvency.requirement" />. A degree alone no longer works. **Fix:** start an income-evidence folder the day your temporary residency is approved.

**24. Missing the conversion window.** The temporary card lasts <Fact k="temporary.duration" />. **Fix:** a reminder several months before the window opens, and a complete file waiting.

**25. Using last year's fee figures.** Fees are <Fact k="fees.basis" />. **Fix:** check the current DNM fee before budgeting (chapter 4).

**26. Paying for a deposit that doesn't exist.** The deposit is <Fact k="solvency.deposit_abolished" />. **Fix:** if anyone asks you to fund a deposit for the standard route, ask them which law requires it.

## Money and tax mistakes

**27. Believing a headline.** Paraguay's personal rate is <Fact k="tax.territorial_rate" />, and <Fact k="tax.foreign_income_treatment" />. That is Paraguay's side. **Fix:** chapter 8's briefing sheet, and your home-country adviser, before you move anything.

**28. Moving a portfolio into a Paraguayan company.** Income that would have stayed outside Paraguayan tax held personally can be pulled into it when held by a Paraguayan entity. **Fix:** advice before structure.

**29. Treating the tax residency certificate as an exit ticket.** It is evidence in Paraguay; it doesn't end your residence at home. **Fix:** ask your adviser what your home country needs.

**30. Moving a large sum without warning the bank.** Compliance holds, questions, delays. **Fix:** tell the bank first, send the documents with the transfer (chapter 7).

## Investor Pass mistakes

**31. Buying a home and calling it an investment.** The real-estate route requires <Fact k="investorpass.route_real_estate_usd" />. **Fix:** confirm qualification in writing before you buy.

**32. Informal jobs on the productive route.** The jobs must be formal. **Fix:** payroll and social security registration from the start.

**33. A thin source-of-funds file.** **Fix:** build it before investing (chapter 10).

## Family mistakes

**34. Missing custody or consent documents** for a child whose other parent isn't moving. **Fix:** resolve at home first (chapter 9).

**35. Police certificates out of sync.** Several adults, several windows. **Fix:** order them together.

**36. Forgetting each person's presence rule.** Every family member holds their own residency. **Fix:** everyone enters the country, not just the main applicant.

## The pattern behind all of these

Almost every mistake above comes from one of three things: **doing steps in the wrong order**, **trusting old information**, or **assuming the card is the end of the process**. Chapter 5 fixes the first, this guide's updates fix the second, and the presence and conversion rules are the answer to the third.

## Mistakes in choosing help

Whether you use us or someone else, these are the ones that cost the most.

**37. No written scope.** A verbal "we handle everything" means something different to each side. **Fix:** a written list of what is included, what is excluded, and who pays for extras.

**38. Paying everything up front.** **Fix:** milestones — for example, part on starting the review, part on filing, part on approval.

**39. Handing over originals without a receipt.** Your passport and apostilled documents are hard to replace. **Fix:** a signed list of every original handed over, and copies of everything you keep.

**40. A power of attorney wider than the job.** **Fix:** limit it to the specific task and a specific period.

**41. No updates.** Silence for weeks is a warning sign, not a sign of progress. **Fix:** agree how and how often you will hear about your file.

## Your next step

Go through the list and tick the ones that could apply to you. For each tick, write the fix into your plan with a date. Then use chapter 12's checklists to make sure you don't meet any of them in practice.
