The Investor Pass deserves consideration when an investment and a residency objective genuinely belong together. It deserves more scrutiny when the investment exists only to obtain a card. This chapter helps you decide whether to investigate it further; the detailed route material belongs to our sibling brand, Paraguay Investor Pass.

Think of the proposition as an investment-linked path to direct permanent residency, subject to current programme conditions and your eligibility. It is not permission to buy any Paraguayan asset and assume the purchase qualifies. Our threshold reference is <Fact k="investorpass.min_investment_usd" />.

## Ask whether you would want the investment anyway

Write down the investment rationale without mentioning residency. What would you own? How could it produce value? Who would manage it? What could go wrong? If that paragraph is empty, you are paying for an immigration objective through an investment you do not yet understand.

**It suits you if:** you already intend to commit capital in Paraguay and can independently assess both the asset and programme eligibility. **It does not suit you if:** you need that capital for living expenses, cannot tolerate it being unavailable or would overlook a weak asset because the residency promise feels urgent.

Chapter 2, The routes compared, provides the alternative immigration paths. Compare against a route you actually qualify for, not an imagined delay or a sales presentation's worst-case description of temporary residency.

## Screen the route before the asset

Real estate: <Fact k="investorpass.route_real_estate_usd" />. Ask what makes the proposed property and ownership structure eligible. Then commission the appropriate independent review of ownership, title, restrictions and contractual terms. A brochure describing a project as eligible is a claim to verify.

Productive business: <Fact k="investorpass.route_business_usd" />. Understand the business, how your capital enters it, your ownership or rights and who controls decisions. Ask which records establish the investment for immigration purposes and which records show whether the business itself is sound.

Financial instruments: <Fact k="investorpass.route_financial_usd" />. Identify the instrument, issuer, custodian, access restrictions and evidence of ownership. Do not let familiar words such as "deposit" or "fund" replace the actual product terms.

Tourism-sector investment: <Fact k="investorpass.route_tourism_usd" />. Ask how the proposed project qualifies, how your interest is documented and what happens if development or operations change. Sector language alone does not answer those questions.

## Keep legal eligibility and commercial judgment separate

Ask an appropriately qualified adviser to explain the current programme basis and how the proposed investment meets it. Separately, obtain the professional review needed for the transaction itself. The person selling the asset should not be your only source of assurance about either question.

Disclose referral fees or commercial relationships when comparing recommendations. Ask who represents you, who represents the seller and which services the fee covers. A useful recommendation remains understandable after those interests are made visible.

You should receive a list of unresolved conditions before committing. If a condition cannot be confirmed until later, understand exactly what money or contractual obligation would already be at risk at that point.

## Work out the sequence of money and evidence

Ask when the investment must be made, what evidence is required and how that fits with the application. Do not infer the sequence from a generic diagram. Your contract, bank transfer and immigration file need a consistent account of the transaction.

Prepare the source-of-funds evidence before attempting to move the capital. Chapter 7, Banking, explains the importance of a traceable explanation and advance discussion with the institution. An immigration representative's confidence does not bind a bank's review.

Read the payment and refund terms closely with your adviser. Ask what happens if the asset fails to qualify, the application is refused, the seller cannot complete or you withdraw. Do not assume an immigration outcome automatically reverses an investment contract.

## Compare time saved with commitments accepted

The processing reference is <Fact k="investorpass.timeline" />. Direct permanent residency and fast completion are not interchangeable claims. Investment preparation, document review and authority processing can each affect the calendar.

Keep status validity separate from processing: <Fact k="investorpass.validity_years" />. Ask about ongoing conditions, evidence to retain and what an eventual sale or change in the investment could mean. Get those answers before assuming the asset can be sold whenever convenient.

Use chapter 4, Costs, real ones, to separate professional fees, transaction expenses and capital committed. Ask your tax advisers to review the investment as well as the residency move; chapter 8, Taxes for residents, explains how to prepare that briefing.

## Decide whether to proceed to due diligence

Bring a short proposal to the next discussion: your residency objective, the investment you are considering, the reason you want it and the capital you can responsibly commit. Include your unanswered questions about eligibility, ownership, timing and exit.

The next step is a reviewed proposal, not a transfer prompted by urgency. For detailed programme material, continue with paraguayinvestorpass.com and confirm the current conditions for your case.

**Your decision:** proceed only when the residency benefit and the investment rationale each withstand scrutiny. If either remains unclear, the standard routes remain worth comparing.
