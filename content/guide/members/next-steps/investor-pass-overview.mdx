The Investor Pass is the newest door into Paraguayan residency, and the one with the most confused information around it. Launched in 2026, it is <Fact k="investorpass.legal_instrument" />. In plain terms: make a qualifying investment, get a certificate from the Ministry of Industry and Commerce (MIC), and use it to apply for permanent residency directly — without spending two years on temporary residency first.

This chapter explains the four routes, the process, what it costs you beyond the investment, and — most importantly — when it is worth it and when the standard route in chapter 2 is the better choice.

## The basics

- **Legal basis:** <Fact k="investorpass.legal_instrument" />.
- **Launched:** <Fact k="investorpass.launch_date" />.
- **Minimum investment:** <Fact k="investorpass.min_investment_usd" />.
- **What you get:** <Fact k="investorpass.validity_years" />.
- **Who is covered:** the investor, plus spouse and minor children.
- **Who issues what:** MIC issues the Foreign Investor Certificate (Constancia de Inversionista Extranjero, CIE), through the SUACE single window. DNM grants the residency.

## The four routes

| Route | Minimum | Key conditions | Suits |
|---|---|---|---|
| Productive business | <Fact k="investorpass.route_business_usd" /> | Business plan, jobs created, investment actually executed | Entrepreneurs building an operating business in industry, commerce or services |
| Tourism project | <Fact k="investorpass.route_tourism_usd" /> | Business plan and technical follow-up | Hotels, lodges, tourism operators |
| Real estate | <Fact k="investorpass.route_real_estate_usd" /> | Property must be used for an economic activity | Investors in commercial or rental property |
| Financial instruments | <Fact k="investorpass.route_financial_usd" /> | Qualifying instruments, held for the required period | Passive investors with liquid capital |

**On the productive route**, the older SUACE programme lives on: it is <Fact k="suace.status" />. The jobs must be formal — registered employees, not informal help — and the investment must actually be spent on the business, not just deposited.

**On the real-estate route**, the rule people break most often is the one about use. A house or flat you live in does not qualify. The property must generate economic activity: commercial premises, rental property, agricultural or industrial land in use.

**On the financial route**, the list of qualifying instruments comes from the resolution and its implementing rules. Don't buy anything until you have confirmed in writing that the specific instrument qualifies, and plan to hold it for the full period.

**On the tourism route**, expect technical review of the project, and plan for the business plan to be read critically.

## How the process runs

1. **Choose the route and structure the investment.** Decide whether you invest personally or through a Paraguayan company. That choice has tax consequences (chapter 8) — including the rule that brings some foreign income into tax when held by a Paraguayan entity.
2. **Prepare the source-of-funds file.** Where the money comes from, documented: sale contracts, payslips, investment statements, inheritance papers. Foreign documents apostilled and translated.
3. **Make the investment** and document it: property title and use, company registration and payroll, instrument purchase confirmations, bank transfers from your own account.
4. **Apply to MIC for the CIE** through SUACE, with the business plan where your route requires it.
5. **Apply to DNM for permanent residency** with the CIE, plus the personal documents from chapter 3: passport, apostilled civil documents, police certificates inside the window, Paraguayan police and Interpol certificates, sworn translations.
6. **Pay DNM fees:** <Fact k="fees.permanent_residency" />, per person.
7. **After approval:** cédula (chapter 6), bank (chapter 7), and ongoing compliance with the route's conditions.

**Timeline:** <Fact k="investorpass.timeline" />. That excludes the time it takes you to find, negotiate and complete the investment itself, which is often the longest part.

## What it costs beyond the investment

- DNM fees per person, and the certificate and translation costs from chapter 4.
- Legal and notarial work on the investment: property conveyancing, company formation, contracts.
- A business plan written to MIC's expectations for the productive and tourism routes.
- For companies: accountant, payroll and social security for the employees.
- Transaction costs on property and financial instruments, and currency conversion (at <Fact k="fx.reference_rate" />).

## Investor Pass or standard route?

| | Standard route (chapter 2) | Investor Pass |
|---|---|---|
| Investment | None | <Fact k="investorpass.min_investment_usd" /> |
| First status | Temporary: <Fact k="temporary.duration" /> | Permanent directly |
| Proof of means at permanent stage | Under Res. 407/2026 categories | The investment and its documentation |
| Presence rule, first years | <Fact k="temporary.presence_rule" /> | <Fact k="permanent.presence_rule" /> |
| Citizenship clock | Starts after the later permanent resolution | Starts at the permanent resolution |
| Main risk | Missing the conversion window | An investment that doesn't qualify or isn't maintained |

**It is worth it when:**

- you are going to make an investment of this kind anyway, for its own sake
- you value starting as a permanent resident, with the more forgiving presence rule, because you will not be in Paraguay every year
- you want the citizenship clock — <Fact k="citizenship.years" /> — to start as soon as possible
- your income profile would be awkward to fit into the 2026 proof-of-means categories, but your capital is easy to document

**It is not worth it when:**

- the investment only exists to get a card the standard route would give you anyway
- you would be buying a home to live in and calling it an investment
- you can't document the source of funds cleanly
- you haven't looked at the tax consequences of the structure

## Red flags in Investor Pass offers

- **"The minimum investment, no jobs needed."** The lowest threshold belongs to the productive route, which comes with formal jobs and a business plan.
- **"Buy this apartment and get residency."** Your own home doesn't qualify; a property sold for this purpose needs to be used for economic activity.
- **"A residency that expires after a fixed number of years."** Permanent residency has no expiry; it is the card that is renewed.
- **A seller of the investment who is also your only adviser on whether it qualifies.** Get an independent view.
- **Pressure to pay before the structure is confirmed in writing.**

## After you have it

Permanent residency through the Investor Pass is still subject to the presence rule: <Fact k="permanent.presence_rule" />. The card is renewed periodically: <Fact k="fees.permanent_card_renewal" />. And where your route has continuing conditions — jobs maintained, instruments held, property in use — keep the evidence, because DNM and MIC can ask.

Our sibling site, [paraguayinvestorpass.com](https://paraguayinvestorpass.com), goes deeper into each route.

## Route by route: what to prepare

**Productive business**

- A business plan showing the activity, the investment schedule and the jobs to be created
- Company formation documents and the company's RUC
- Evidence the investment was actually executed: equipment invoices, premises lease or title, capital transfers
- Payroll and social security registration for the formal employees
- Your source-of-funds file

**Tourism project**

- A business plan for the tourism project, detailed enough for technical review
- Land or premises title or lease, and the permits the project needs
- Evidence of the investment made and its schedule
- Your source-of-funds file

**Real estate**

- The property title in your name (or your company's), registered
- Evidence the property is used for an economic activity: a rental contract, a commercial lease, an operating business on the premises
- Evidence of the purchase price paid and how it was paid
- Your source-of-funds file

**Financial instruments**

- Written confirmation that the specific instrument qualifies
- Purchase confirmations and custody statements in your name
- A plan to hold the instrument for the full required period, and statements showing it
- Your source-of-funds file

## Questions to ask before you invest

1. Which route am I using, and which document proves I meet each condition?
2. Who confirms in writing that this specific investment qualifies?
3. If the investment is a property, what economic activity will it carry, and how will I evidence it?
4. If it is a business, who are the employees, and when are they registered?
5. What must I keep doing after approval, and for how long?
6. What happens to my residency if the investment is sold or the business closes?
7. What are the tax consequences of holding this personally or through a company?

If you can't answer all seven, you aren't ready to transfer money.

## Your next step

Write down which route fits the investment you would make anyway. If none of them does, go back to chapter 2 — the standard route will serve you better. If one does, start the source-of-funds file this week; it is the part that takes longest to get right.
