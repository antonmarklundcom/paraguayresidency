Use this chapter as a working control beside your case-specific instructions. A tick should mean that the relevant evidence exists, not that you have read about a task. The earlier chapters explain the decisions; this chapter helps you track them without losing the handoffs.

Keep columns for status, owner, evidence and next action. Useful statuses are "not started", "awaiting confirmation", "in progress" and "complete". If an item does not apply, record who confirmed that and why. An unexplained blank is too easy to mistake for completion later.

## Before collecting documents

- Write your reason for applying and the practical outcome you need. Check it against chapter 1, Why Paraguay (and why not), including any essential household requirements.
- Confirm the proposed route and the basis for eligibility. Record unresolved conditions from chapter 2, The routes compared, before treating the route as settled.
- List citizenships, residence history and relevant family circumstances for the reviewer. Do not use a generic nationality checklist as a substitute for that information.
- Obtain the current document list for your case. Identify the source of the instructions and who will answer questions about them.
- Compare the service scope with your intended finish line. Use chapter 4, Costs, real ones, to mark included work, exclusions and unanswered cost questions.

## Document preparation: common checks

- Check passport details and the validity requirement confirmed for your route and travel arrangements. Avoid using a remembered minimum from an old checklist.
- Identify the required civil-record versions and issuing authorities. Record differences in names and the evidence connecting those names before authentication.
- Confirm which police or background certificates are required for your history. Record the accepted issuer and any applicable freshness criteria.
- Check the authentication and translation plan for each document with chapter 3, Documents, apostilles and translations, by nationality, open beside the list.
- Obtain a review of complete scans before travelling. Keep the reviewed version and the list of outstanding corrections together.

## Nationality and issuing-country branches

**Where an apostille route applies:**

- Confirm applicability for the document and destination, the competent issuing authority and any prior certification needed.
- Check that the authenticated document is the version the receiving office wants. Do not confuse authenticating a signature with confirming the content meets the application requirement.

**Where consular legalisation is required:**

- Write down the complete chain supplied by the relevant authorities, including delivery and return arrangements.
- Identify who holds each original at each stage. Allow the confirmed chain to shape your filing plan rather than compressing it to fit a booked flight.

**Where nationality may affect the residence category:**

- Confirm the applicable route and its specific list. <Fact k="mercosur.residency_route" />
- Keep the nationality question separate from the authentication of records issued elsewhere. Explain multiple citizenships before documents are ordered under a particular route.

## Route-specific checks

**Temporary residency:**

- Confirm the initial filing requirements and record the later application you must plan for. <Fact k="temporary.duration" />
- Ask what evidence to retain and which future steps sit outside the present service scope.

**Permanent residency:**

- Record the basis for your application, including any prior status required for your category.
- Confirm how ongoing conditions fit your travel plan. <Fact k="permanent.presence_rule" /> Keep the answer with your status documents.

**Investor Pass:**

- Complete the eligibility and investment screening in chapter 10, Investor Pass overview, before committing funds.
- Obtain a reviewed sequence for investment evidence, source-of-funds documents and filing. Understand contractual outcomes if eligibility or the application fails.
- Identify any ongoing investment conditions and who will advise you before a sale or material change.

## Household checks

- Maintain an individual document and eligibility record for each applicant. Use chapter 9, Family, to identify shared evidence and separate dependencies.
- Resolve authority, consent and representation questions for children before signing or authenticating documents.
- Confirm attendance requirements by person. Record whether files can proceed separately if a household member is delayed.
- Arrange appointment-day care, transport and access to originals. Avoid assuming that the lead applicant's readiness means everyone is ready.

## Travel and submission

- Confirm document readiness and the attendance arrangements described in chapter 5, Timeline, week by week. Distinguish a booked appointment from an approval estimate.
- Prepare the reviewed originals, requested copies and contact details. Understand what remains to be completed locally.
- After filing, retain the acknowledgement, reference and copy of the submitted set. Record who receives and responds to authority queries.
- Ask for an explanation of your current permission to stay, work and travel as applicable. Do not infer it from payment or attendance alone.

## After approval and ongoing use

- Check the approval details and obtain the next-step instructions. Follow chapter 6, Cédula and RUC, for identity-document preparation and the accountant's registration assessment.
- Check completed records for errors and arrange access to your own administrative accounts. Keep recovery information secure.
- Prepare the bank application using chapter 7, Banking. Confirm account terms and functionality before relying on it for essential payments.
- Complete the advice and recordkeeping plan in chapter 8, Taxes for residents. Immigration approval should not be the trigger for assuming a tax outcome.
- Calendar confirmed future actions and record what changes require review. Keep the printable resource in Members → Resources alongside your case-specific notes, checking for revisions.

**Completion rule:** close an item only when you can locate its evidence and explain what happens next. If a new instruction conflicts with this list, resolve the difference with the responsible adviser before proceeding.
