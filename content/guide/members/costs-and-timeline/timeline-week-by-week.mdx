A residency timeline has two kinds of time in it. There is **your** time — ordering documents, getting apostilles, booking flights — which you control. And there is **the authorities'** time — DNM reviewing your file, Identificaciones printing your cédula — which you don't. Most delays come from the first kind being done out of order, so that the second kind starts late.

This chapter lays the whole thing out as a calendar. Week numbers count from your filing day in Asunción, which we call Week 0. Negative weeks are preparation at home.

## The big picture

| Phase | Where | What happens | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|
| Preparation | Home | Documents, apostilles, scans reviewed | Depends on your country — plan backwards from filing |
| Trip 1 | Asunción | Translations, Interpol, police certificate, DNM filing | About a working week if everything is ready |
| DNM review | Anywhere | DNM checks your file and your records | <Fact k="residency.timeline" /> |
| Trip 2 | Asunción | Collect resident card, apply for cédula | A few working days |
| Cédula | Anywhere | Identificaciones processes and prints the card | <Fact k="cedula.timeline" /> |
| RUC and bank | Asunción or online | Tax number, then bank account | See chapters 6 and 7 |

## Week by week

## Where it stalls, and how to avoid it

**Before you travel: the police certificate.** This is the single biggest cause of wasted trips. It has to be national-level, apostilled and still inside the recency window on the day you file. Countries with fingerprint-based checks and slow federal apostilles (the US and South Africa especially) need the most planning. Work backwards: filing date, minus a margin, minus apostille time, minus issue time. That gives you the day to apply.

**On arrival: translations and names.** If the translator has scans in advance, translations are ready when you land. If a name is spelled differently between passport and certificate, the translator can't fix it — you need a bridging document, and that means going back to chapter 3.

**At DNM: incomplete files.** DNM will not accept a file with a missing piece. It is quicker to check every item against the checklist the night before than to discover the gap at the counter.

**During review: record checks.** Since late 2025, Interpol and DNM verify records directly with the country of origin. For some nationalities this adds time nobody can predict. There is nothing to do but make sure your contact details are correct and respond to any query at once.

**After approval: the cédula.** The official timeline is <Fact k="cedula.timeline" />. In 2026 that has been optimistic. Don't plan a bank account or a property purchase that depends on having the cédula on a particular date.

## How long in Asunción?

For trip 1, we plan around a working week, assuming documents are reviewed and translations are waiting. Arriving on a weekend and filing midweek leaves a buffer for a second Interpol visit or a correction. Anything shorter works only when every document is already perfect.

Trip 2 is shorter: a few working days to collect the resident card and file for the cédula. If your travel is expensive, ask whoever follows your file whether the card will be ready before you book — not after.

## The long view: two years and beyond

The first card is only the start. Put these dates in your calendar the day your resident card is issued:

1. **Every year:** an entry into Paraguay. For temporary residents the rule is <Fact k="temporary.presence_rule" />. Enforcement is now twice-yearly under DNM Res. 376/2026.
2. **From the day of approval:** start a folder of income or asset evidence for the Res. 407/2026 categories (chapter 2).
3. **Before the temporary card expires:** the conversion window. Temporary residency lasts <Fact k="temporary.duration" />. Set a reminder several months before the window opens so your evidence is ready.
4. **After permanent approval:** <Fact k="permanent.presence_rule" />. And the naturalisation clock: <Fact k="citizenship.years" />.

## What this means for your plans

- **Employer or client commitments:** tell them you need a flexible week for trip 1 and a few days for trip 2, with dates confirmed only after documents are reviewed.
- **Leases:** don't end your current lease on the assumption that residency will be done by a certain date.
- **Children's schooling:** start school enquiries early; the school may accept the filing receipt while the cédula is pending, but ask.
- **Big purchases:** property, cars and bank-dependent transactions are easier after the cédula. Plan them for later.

## Three planning scenarios

**Everything ready.** Documents reviewed, translations waiting, certificates fresh. Trip 1 is a single working week, trip 2 a few days, and the long waits are simply the authorities' time. This is the scenario the table above describes.

**The slow police certificate.** Your country's national check takes a long time to issue, and the apostille adds more. Work backwards from the day you expect it to arrive, not from the day you would like to fly. If it arrives late, move the flight; never file with a certificate that will already be outside the window. In this scenario the civil documents can be apostilled and translated early, so that the police certificate is the last piece, not one of many.

**The family.** Several adults, several police certificates, several sets of translations. Order all police certificates together so they land in the same window, and plan the Interpol visit for everyone on the same day. Children below the checklist age skip some steps; confirm which before you arrive. Allow an extra day in Asunción for the sheer volume of copies and signatures.

## If you can't take a full week off work

Many of our clients work remotely through trip 1. It is possible if you plan for it:

- Keep mornings free. Most offices work best early, and queues are shortest then.
- Schedule calls for the afternoon and evening, and don't book anything important on filing day.
- Choose accommodation with reliable internet and a backup (a mobile data plan on a local SIM).
- Keep one day entirely free as a buffer. It is usually needed for something.
