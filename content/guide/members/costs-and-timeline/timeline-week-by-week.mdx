**Weeks 1–2, before you travel:** gather the core document set (chapter 3), start any apostille
or legalisation process, and book your travel once you have a filing appointment.

**Week 3, arrival and filing:** biometrics, the initial application, and — for most routes — the
point at which you are legally allowed to remain while the case is processed.

**Weeks 4–8, processing:** this is where cases wait on the government, not on you. It moves
faster with a complete file and slower with a missing or mistranslated document — the single
biggest lever you control is submitting everything correctly the first time.

**Where it actually stalls:** almost never at the government end once a file is complete. It
stalls when a translation was done before arrival and has to be redone locally, when a police
certificate expires while other paperwork is still moving, or when a document needed
legalisation instead of an apostille and nobody caught it until submission. Start the
document with the longest lead time — usually the police certificate or a legalisation, not the
apostille — in week 1, not week 3, and the rest of the timeline holds.

**After approval:** chapter 6 covers the cédula and RUC, which follow approval rather than
running in parallel with it.
