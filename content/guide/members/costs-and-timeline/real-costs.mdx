"How much does it cost?" is the first question everyone asks us, and the honest answer is: the government part is small and fixed, the document part depends on your country, and the part that really moves the total is how many trips and how many mistakes you make. This chapter gives you the fixed numbers, a worksheet for the variable ones, and a straight comparison between doing it yourself and having it done.

## How Paraguayan government fees work

DNM fees are not set in guaraníes. They are <Fact k="fees.basis" />. Two consequences:

- **Every July, the fees rise** with the minimum wage. A figure you read last year is probably too low.
- **The dollar equivalent moves with the exchange rate.** At <Fact k="fx.reference_rate" />, the guaraní is much stronger than it was a year or two ago, which is why older pages quote lower dollar amounts for the same fee.

Pay fees in guaraníes. Some offices take cash only — the Interpol certificate is one — so arrive with local currency and keep every official receipt. If anyone asks for an extra "processing" payment without an official receipt, don't pay it.

## The costs outside Paraguay

Here the numbers depend on your country, so we give you the structure and you fill in the figures from your own quotes.

**Official documents.** Replacement birth and marriage certificates, the national police certificate and any fingerprinting fee. Some countries charge more for faster issue; given the police-certificate window, the faster option is sometimes worth it.

**Apostilles.** Charged per document by the apostille authority in the issuing country (chapter 3 lists the authorities). Count every document, including the police certificate. If a document needs a notary or solicitor certification before the apostille, add that line too.

**Couriers.** Sending originals to the apostille authority and back, and in some cases to Paraguay ahead of you. Use tracked services; a lost police certificate can cost you the whole window.

**Flights and accommodation.** At least one trip to Asunción to file, and usually a return trip to collect your resident card and apply for your cédula (chapter 5). Price the length of stay the timeline actually needs, not the cheapest flight.

## The costs inside Paraguay

**Sworn translations.** Priced per page by a Paraguayan registered public translator. Count every page, including the apostille pages. Spanish-language documents are free of this line.

**Copies, certified copies and notary work.** Small individually, but they add up across a family.

**Local transport and time.** Taxis or ride-hailing between offices, and the days you are not working because you are in a queue.

**Professional help.** If you use it. See the comparison below.

## DIY or done for you?

Doing it yourself, the all-in figure per adult is <Fact k="costs.diy_total" />. That excludes flights, accommodation and the value of your time. Here is where the difference really lies.

| | Doing it yourself | Having it done |
|---|---|---|
| Government fees | Same | Same — they don't change with who files |
| Document checking before you travel | You, against the checklist | Reviewed from scans before you book flights |
| Translations | You find and brief a translator | Arranged, names checked against passport |
| Interpol and police certificates | You navigate the offices in Spanish | Accompanied, or prepared in advance |
| DNM filing | You queue and answer questions | Accompanied filing |
| Following the file | You chase DNM yourself | Followed up for you |
| Cédula and RUC | Separate process you handle | Can be included in scope |
| Risk of an extra trip | Higher if something is wrong on arrival | Lower, because problems are found from scans |
| Professional fee | None | For temporary residency: <Fact k="pricing.temporary" /> |

DIY is realistic if you speak Spanish, have flexible time in Asunción and your documents are simple. It gets expensive when a problem appears on filing day and you have to fly home to fix it — at that point one extra round trip usually costs more than the help would have.

If you get help, insist on four things in writing: what the fee covers, what it excludes, who pays when an extra document is required, and what happens if the file is delayed. A fixed fee with a clear scope is worth more than a cheaper quote with vague edges.

## Payment timing

Write a payment calendar. Many costs land before you have any result: documents, apostilles, translations, flights. The DNM fee is paid at filing. Professional fees are often split into milestones. Knowing when each payment falls avoids the unpleasant surprise of a total you can afford arriving at a moment you can't.

## Which lines multiply for a couple or a family

When you move from one applicant to several, not everything doubles.

| Line | Multiplies by person? |
|---|---|
| DNM fee, Interpol, police certificates | Yes — each applicant pays |
| National police certificates from home | Yes, for every applicant old enough to need one |
| Birth certificates | Yes, one per person |
| Marriage certificate | No — one document covers the couple |
| Apostilles | Per document, so they follow the document count |
| Translations | Per page, so shared documents are translated once |
| Flights and accommodation | Per traveller, but accommodation is often shared |
| Professional help | Quoted per household or per person — ask which |

## How to read a quote — ours included

Whoever you ask, put these questions to them and get the answers in writing:

1. Is the government fee included, or paid separately by me at the counter?
2. Are translations included? At what page count?
3. Are Paraguayan police and Interpol certificates included?
4. Does the fee cover one trip or two? Accompaniment at every office, or only DNM?
5. What happens, and what does it cost, if DNM asks for an extra document?
6. Is the cédula included? The RUC? The later conversion to permanent?
7. When is each part of the fee due?

A quote that answers all seven clearly is comparable. One that doesn't is a starting point for a conversation, not a price.
