"Paraguay citizenship by investment" is a common search, and it has a short answer: there isn't one. Paraguay does not sell citizenship, and no amount of investment turns into a passport on its own. What investment does buy is faster permanent residency, through the Investor Pass. Citizenship is a separate, later step, open to residents who meet the constitutional conditions. This piece explains the real route, where the Investor Pass fits in it, and what to make of anyone offering a shortcut.

## What the law says about naturalization

Paraguayan nationality by naturalization comes from the Constitution, which sets four conditions: <Fact k="citizenship.constitutional_requirements" />. In practice the residence is counted as permanent residency: <Fact k="citizenship.years" />.

The application goes to the Supreme Court (Corte Suprema de Justicia), which reviews the file and, if it is satisfied, issues the naturalization certificate, the carta de naturalización. It is a judicial process, not an administrative one, and it has no published processing time. There is no investment category in it and no fee that replaces its conditions.

## Where the Investor Pass fits

The Investor Pass changes the first half of the journey, not the second. It lets you skip temporary residency and receive permanent residency directly, on the strength of <Fact k="investorpass.cie_scope" />. That can start your qualifying period sooner. It does not shorten the period.

| Stage | Standard route | With the Investor Pass |
|---|---|---|
| Temporary residency | <Fact k="temporary.duration" /> | Skipped |
| Permanent residency | A change of category at the end of the temporary period | Direct: <Fact k="investorpass.timeline" /> |
| Qualifying residence | <Fact k="citizenship.years" /> | The same |
| Naturalization | Application to the Supreme Court | The same |

So the honest pitch of the Investor Pass for someone who wants citizenship is this: it can bring the start of the citizenship clock forward by the length of the temporary stage, at the price of committing capital to a qualifying investment. Whether that trade is worth it depends on why you are investing in the first place.

## Why people think Paraguay sells citizenship

The confusion has a few sources, and it helps to recognize them when you read an offer.

- **"Golden visa" language.** Marketing that calls the Investor Pass a golden visa invites comparison with countries whose programs lead to passports. Paraguay's leads to permanent residency.
- **Residency and citizenship used as synonyms.** Some pages call permanent residency "Paraguayan status" or show the cédula, the identity card every resident receives, as if it were a citizen's document. A foreign resident's cédula is not a passport and does not make you a national.
- **Old deposit schemes.** For years, Paraguayan residency was sold on the back of a fixed bank deposit, sometimes bundled with talk of an eventual passport. That deposit is <Fact k="solvency.deposit_abolished" />, and it never bought citizenship.
- **Speed claims.** "Permanent residency in months" is true of the Investor Pass. It is sometimes stretched into "citizenship in months", which is not.

If an offer uses any of these, ask the provider to name the legal instrument behind each step. For residency, it will be the migration law or <Fact k="investorpass.legal_instrument" />. For citizenship, it can only be the Constitution and the Supreme Court.

## Residence means living here

This is where many investor plans fall down. The permanent card has a light presence rule: <Fact k="permanent.presence_rule" />. That keeps the card valid. It does not show that you live in Paraguay or exercise a profession, trade or business here, which is what the constitutional conditions describe.

An investor who runs a productive business in Paraguay, lives here for much of the year and pays tax here will find those conditions far easier to document than one who holds a passive investment and visits occasionally. How the court weighs each case is its own judgment, which is exactly why the plan has to start years before the application, not the month before.

## Dual nationality

Before you set your heart on a Paraguayan passport, check whether you can keep the one you have. Paraguay's rule is that <Fact k="citizenship.dual_nationality" />. Your own country's law matters as much: some countries allow dual nationality freely, some restrict it, and some remove your nationality if you naturalize elsewhere. Get both sides confirmed by lawyers in each country before you file anything.

## What a Paraguayan passport offers

For those who do qualify, the passport has real uses. Paraguayan citizens can visit the Schengen area without a visa for short stays, and as nationals of a Mercosur member they can use the Mercosur residence arrangements to live in other member countries. Citizenship also removes the residency presence rule. It does not change your tax position on its own: Paraguay taxes on a territorial basis whatever your nationality.

## Red flags

Be wary of anyone who offers:

- a Paraguayan passport "in months", or a guaranteed citizenship date,
- citizenship "packages" priced as a single fee,
- a route that does not require you to live in Paraguay,
- an arrangement that skips the Supreme Court.

None of these match the law. At best they are residency sold under a misleading name; at worst they put your money and your record at risk.

If what you want is a secure right to live in Paraguay, permanent residency already gives you that, and the Investor Pass is the direct route to it for qualifying investors.
