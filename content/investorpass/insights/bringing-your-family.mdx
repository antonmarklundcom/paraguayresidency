Most people asking about the Investor Pass are not asking for themselves
alone. They are asking whether a spouse can come too, whether a teenager still
in school counts as a dependent, whether an aging parent living with them can
be included. The short answer is usually yes, with some paperwork attached.
The longer answer is worth walking through, because the family side of an
application is where most of the delays actually happen — not the investment
review itself.

## Who can generally be included

A spouse is included as a matter of course. Dependent minor children are
included as a matter of course too. Beyond that, it gets less fixed. Dependent
adult children — a 22-year-old still financially supported and unmarried, say
— are sometimes included, and sometimes a caseworker wants more documentation
of the dependency before agreeing. The same goes for a parent who lives with
you and relies on you financially.

We are hedging here on purpose. The rules around who counts as a dependent
under the Investor Pass are new, and they are still being applied case by
case rather than off a fixed checklist. What worked for one family last month
is a reasonable guide, not a guarantee. If your household has anyone beyond a
spouse and minor children, treat it as a question to raise early rather than
an assumption to build a plan around.

## What each dependent needs beyond the primary applicant

The primary applicant carries the investment paperwork. Everyone else added
to the case carries proof of the relationship instead. In practice that
means:

- A spouse needs a marriage certificate, apostilled or legalized and
  translated into Spanish if it was issued elsewhere.
- A minor child needs a birth certificate showing the relationship to the
  primary applicant, plus consent from the other parent if that parent is not
  part of the application.
- An adult child or parent being included as a dependent needs whatever
  proves both the relationship and the financial dependency — this is the
  part that varies most, and it is worth confirming exactly what will be
  accepted before you start collecting documents rather than after.

Every dependent also needs their own police clearance and, once in country,
their own medical exam — the same personal documents the primary applicant
needs, just multiplied by however many people are on the case.

## Does the investment amount change per person

No. This is worth stating plainly because people often assume it scales with
family size, the way some other countries' programs do. It does not. The
qualifying investment currently starts <Fact k="investorpass.min_investment_usd" />,
and that figure is assessed once, against the case as a whole. Adding a
spouse and three children does not raise it, and adding nobody does not lower
it. What changes with family size is the volume of paperwork and the number
of individual cédula processes at the end, not the capital requirement.

## When the family situation is not straightforward

Blended families come up often enough that it is not really an edge case.  A
child from a previous relationship, a stepchild who has never been formally
adopted, a spouse from a marriage that is not yet finalized on paper in every
country involved — none of these rule you out, but each one needs its own
piece of documentation to close the gap between what the file shows and what
is actually true about your household.

An unmarried partner is the situation we get asked about most. The program is
built around marriage and legal dependency, so a partner without either
generally cannot ride on the primary applicant's case the way a spouse can.
There are sometimes other routes for that person to apply on their own
timeline, which is a conversation worth having directly rather than guessing
at from a web page — see our [contact page](/contact) if that describes your
situation.

None of this is disqualifying on its own. It just means the documentation
list is longer than "marriage certificate, birth certificates, done," and
that list is easier to plan for four months out than four weeks out.

## The card and cédula timeline is per person

Once the case as a whole is approved, each family member still goes through
their own identity document process locally. The <Fact k="cedula.timeline" />
applies to each person individually — your spouse's cédula does not arrive
the moment yours does, and a child's does not arrive the moment a parent's
does. If you are planning around a school year, a lease, or a job start date
for any one family member, plan around their own timeline rather than the
household's approval date.

The residency itself, once granted, carries the same
<Fact k="investorpass.validity_years" /> for every person on the case. It is
the administrative step of turning that approval into a physical card that
happens one person at a time.

## Building the family list before you file

The practical order that causes the fewest delays: settle who is being
included before the investment paperwork is finalized, not after. Gathering
an apostilled marriage certificate or a consent letter from an absent parent
can take longer than the investment side of the case, especially if any
document needs to come from a country other than Paraguay. Our
[requirements page](/investor-pass/requirements) lays out the base document
list; treat the family additions above as what gets layered on top of it for
your specific household.

If your situation has any wrinkle at all — a stepchild, a dependent parent, a
partner you have not yet married — it is worth raising on a call before you
start collecting paperwork, so nothing gets gathered twice.
