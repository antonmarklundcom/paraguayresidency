People ask about taxes before they ask almost anything else about the Investor
Pass. That makes sense. A residency card is only useful if you understand what
it does and does not change about your money. So here is the plain version,
with the hedges left in on purpose, because a real tax question deserves a
real accountant, not a paragraph on a residency site.

## Territorial tax, in plain terms

Paraguay runs a territorial tax system. That means the country generally
taxes income earned inside Paraguay, at <Fact k="tax.territorial_rate" />, and
treats income earned outside Paraguay differently. Specifically,
<Fact k="tax.foreign_income_treatment" />.

That is not the same as saying Paraguay is a tax-free country. It isn't, and
we will not tell you it is. It taxes what happens inside its borders. What
happens outside its borders is, broadly, outside its system. Whether that
distinction helps you, and by how much, depends entirely on where your income
actually comes from and how your own country's tax code is written.

## Holding residency is not the same as being a tax resident

This is the point people skip past, so we will say it directly. Getting the
Investor Pass gives you permanent residency status under immigration law. It
does not, by itself, make you a Paraguayan tax resident. Tax residency has its
own test, usually built around things like how many days you spend in the
country and where your center of economic interest sits. You can hold the
card and never trigger tax residency. You can also hold the card and become a
tax resident, if your circumstances fit the test.

These are two different systems answering two different questions. Your
immigration file answers "can you live here." Your tax status answers "who
taxes your income, and on what." Conflating the two is the most common
mistake we see, and it is an easy one to make, because both processes involve
the same government and the same paperwork drawer.

It also matters in the other direction. Some people assume that because they
plan to spend very little time in Paraguay, tax residency simply will not
apply to them, and they stop thinking about it there. That may well be true.
It also might not be, depending on how the days-in-country test and the
center-of-economic-interest test actually read in your case, and depending on
choices you make later, like buying a home here or shifting business
activity into the country. We are not going to guess at your outcome in a
residency article. That determination belongs to someone qualified to make
it, working from your actual facts.

## The RUC, and when you actually need one

A RUC is Paraguay's taxpayer registration number — the equivalent of a tax ID
that lets you appear in the tax authority's system, invoice, open certain
accounts, or run a business locally. Not every Investor Pass holder needs
one. If your investment and your life stay entirely outside Paraguay's tax
system — no local business activity, no local income, no local invoicing —
you may never need to register for one. If you buy property to rent out
locally, start a business, or otherwise generate Paraguay-sourced income,
you will likely need a RUC to handle that correctly.

The decision of whether and when to get one is specific to your situation. We
can point you toward the requirements around residency itself — see our
[Investor Pass requirements page](/investor-pass/requirements) — but the RUC
question sits on the tax side of the line, and that is where your accountant
takes over from us.

## Your home country's rules do not disappear

This is the part we want to be unmistakable about. Nothing in this article,
and nothing about the Investor Pass, changes what your home country expects
from you. If you come from a country with:

- citizenship-based taxation, where tax obligations follow your passport
  rather than where you live,
- an exit tax triggered by giving up residency or citizenship,
- controlled foreign company (CFC) rules that tax you on income earned
  through a foreign company you control,
- or foreign asset and account reporting requirements,

those rules keep applying on their own terms. Paraguay granting you
residency, and Paraguay's territorial system leaving certain income outside
its own tax net, has no bearing on any of that. Your home country decides
when you stop being its tax resident, using its own tests, not Paraguay's.
Some countries make that easy. Some make it genuinely hard, with years of
lag and specific filing steps you cannot skip. We do not know which category
your country falls into, and we are not the ones who should tell you.

## What this service does and does not cover

We handle the residency filing. We help you understand what the Investor
Pass requires, what a RUC is for, and how Paraguay's territorial system
works at a general level. What we do not do is advise you on your personal

None of this article is tax advice. Treat it as background, not a plan. If
you are weighing whether the Investor Pass changes your tax bill anywhere,
that conversation belongs with your own accountant before you file anything,
not after.
