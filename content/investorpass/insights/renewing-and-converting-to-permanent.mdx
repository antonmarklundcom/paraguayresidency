The Investor Pass gets marketed around its headline: a fast route to residency
tied to an investment. What gets talked about less is what happens once the
initial term is actually up. It is not automatic, and it is not the same
question for everyone — some people renew on the same basis they started
with, and some people convert to Paraguay's standard permanent residency
track instead. Which one applies to you depends on your case, and it is worth
understanding the shape of both paths well before the date arrives.

## The term is not indefinite

The Investor Pass carries <Fact k="investorpass.validity_years" />. That
sounds like a long runway, and for most people it is, but "long" is not the
same as "does not end." Treating the expiry date as something to think about
later is the single most avoidable mistake here — the people who handle this
smoothly are the ones who started asking questions a year or more out, not
the ones who noticed the date on a card and called that week.

## Two different paths, not one

**Renewal** keeps you on the Investor Pass basis. In broad terms this means
confirming that whatever qualified you originally — the investment, the
route you filed under — is still in place, or handling what changed. If you
sold the underlying investment, moved it, or restructured it, that is
something to disclose and work through rather than something that quietly
sorts itself out.

**Converting to standard permanent residency** moves you off the
investment-linked basis entirely and onto Paraguay's ordinary permanent
residency framework. People do this for a few different reasons: they no
longer hold the qualifying investment, they would rather not have their
residency status tied to an asset going forward, or converting simply fits
their long-term plans better than renewing on the original terms. Converting
is not a downgrade — it is a different legal basis for the same underlying
right to live in Paraguay, and for some people it is the more durable choice
precisely because it stops depending on an investment you may want the
freedom to sell.

## What triggers each path

There is no single rule that decides this for you, and we are not going to
pretend there is. In practice, the decision tends to follow a few real-world
questions:

- **Do you still hold the qualifying investment, in a form that still
  qualifies?** If yes, renewal is usually the more straightforward path. If
  you have sold, restructured, or wound down the investment, conversion is
  worth a serious look.
- **Do you want your residency status tied to an asset going forward?** Some
  investors are comfortable with that link indefinitely. Others reach the end
  of the initial term and decide they would rather not renew something that
  depends on continuing to hold a specific investment.
- **Has your presence pattern in Paraguay changed?** Permanent residency
  generally comes with its own presence expectations, distinct from whatever
  applied during the Investor Pass term. Worth confirming how your actual
  travel pattern fits either path before choosing one.

None of these questions has a universally right answer. They are the ones
worth working through with your case manager as the term approaches, not
assumptions to make on your own from a web page.

## What to prepare in advance, regardless of path

Whichever direction you end up going, the preparation overlaps more than it
differs:

- **Proof of your current investment status**, if you are renewing — updated
  documentation showing the investment still qualifies, or an explanation of
  what changed if it does not.
- **An updated police clearance and any other personal documents** that carry
  a recency window, the same category of paperwork you gathered the first
  time around. These do not stay valid indefinitely, and starting late on
  them is a common, easily avoidable delay.
- **A current read on your presence history** — how much time you have
  actually spent in Paraguay since the original approval, since this can
  matter for permanent status in a way it may not have during the initial
  term.
- **A decision, made early, about which path you are aiming for** — even a
  provisional one. Renewal and conversion are not filed the same way, and
  deciding late narrows your options rather than keeping them open.

## Family members on the same case

If your family was added as dependents under the original Investor Pass
case, their status generally follows the primary applicant's, but each
person's paperwork still needs its own attention at renewal or conversion
time — recency-limited documents in particular do not update themselves
just because the household is renewing together. See our piece on
[bringing your family](/investorpass/insights/bringing-your-family) for how
dependents were handled the first time around; most of the same document
categories resurface here.

## The honest summary

Renewal and conversion are both normal, both manageable, and neither is
something to leave until the last few months. The term length gives you real
room to plan, but only if you start using that room well before the date on
the card becomes the deadline. If you are not sure yet which path fits your
situation, that is a completely reasonable thing to bring to a call rather
than something you need to have already decided.
