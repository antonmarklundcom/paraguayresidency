Real estate gets most of the attention among the Investor Pass routes, and we cover it in [our real estate deep dive](/insights/real-estate-route-deep-dive). The other two routes that don't require a factory or a payroll are financial instruments and tourism. They could hardly be more different. One is a passive holding with a lock-in; the other is an operating business you build and report on. This piece takes each in turn, straight from Resolution 0283/2026.

## The two routes side by side

| | Financial instruments | Tourism |
|---|---|---|
| Minimum | <Fact k="investorpass.route_financial_usd" /> | <Fact k="investorpass.route_tourism_usd" /> |
| Business plan | No | Yes, previously approved |
| Jobs | No | No minimum set by the resolution |
| Main evidence | A certificate from an authorised securities entity | The business plan, an investment breakdown and solvency evidence |
| After approval | A minimum holding term and regular reporting | Progress reports and verification against the plan |
| Liquidity | Locked for the minimum term | Tied up in the project |
| Main risk | Issuer and market | Operating and execution |

## The financial instruments route

**What it asks.** <Fact k="investorpass.financial_conditions" />. The resolution describes these as relevant financial investments contributing capital to the country, with no obligation to run a business or create jobs.

**What qualifies.** The resolution does not publish a list of eligible products. It admits any economically demonstrable investment whose nature can be verified with documents, and it puts the weight on the certificate: who issues it, for how long, and for what value. That means the practical question is not "does this bond qualify?" but "can a regulated Paraguayan intermediary certify this holding, for this term, in the form the resolution requires?" Ask that question in writing before you move money.

**Timing and currency.** <Fact k="investorpass.investment_status" />. Keep the exchange rate in mind if you invest in guaraníes: the minimum is tested in dollars on the day you apply.

**After approval.** The holding term and the reporting continue, and SUACE's verification directorate follows up on the requirements. What happens to your residency if you sell before the term ends, or if the instrument matures and you roll it over, should be answered in writing before you invest. Permanent residency itself carries the standard condition: <Fact k="permanent.presence_rule" />.

**Who it suits.** Investors who want the qualifying capital to stay in financial form, managed by a regulated institution, with no business to run. The trade-off is a lock-in, and issuer and market risk on the holding itself.

## The tourism route

**What it asks.** <Fact k="investorpass.tourism_conditions" />. Qualifying projects are those that create, buy, expand, modernize or run assets, infrastructure, services or activities directly linked to tourism, under a business plan approved in advance.

**The business plan.** The resolution sets out what it must contain: the company (new or existing, with shareholder or beneficial-ownership records if you are not named in the statutes), the location, the type of tourism service under the tourism authority's classification of providers, an itemized investment table and an execution schedule. Investment is counted in property, equipment, vehicles used in the activity, furniture, building works and installations. Rent, salaries, utilities and recurring administrative costs are not counted.

**Solvency.** You show that you can fund the plan, with recent bank statements (yours or your company's), investment titles, or valuations of property, vehicles or machinery you own. Foreign documents have to be <Fact k="investorpass.foreign_documents" />.

**After approval.** Progress on the plan is reported to SUACE, and its verification directorate checks the project against what you filed. A tourism plan that exists on paper and not on the ground is the kind of file verification is designed to catch.

**Who it suits.** Investors who actually want to run or own a tourism operation in Paraguay, such as a lodge, an estancia, a hotel or a tour business, and who would make the investment with or without the residency. It suits badly anyone looking for a passive route; that is what the financial route and the real estate route are for.

## Pitfalls we would warn you about

- **Choosing the product before the certificate.** On the financial route, confirm that an authorised entity will issue the right certificate before you buy anything.
- **Counting the wrong costs.** On the tourism route, a budget heavy on rent and salaries can fall short of the minimum however much you spend.
- **Ignoring the obligations after approval.** Both routes report after approval. Put the dates in your calendar, not your adviser's.
- **Assuming family members are covered by the certificate.** The certificate is issued to the investor alone. Family members are handled through the migration office, as our [family guide](/insights/bringing-your-family) explains.
