Investors tend to assume that money smooths every path. In Paraguay's Investor Pass, it smooths the route but not the checks. This page is for investors whose record is not clean and who want to know where they stand before committing capital.

## What the investor certificate does

The certificate is issued through SUACE and its purpose is narrow: <Fact k="investorpass.cie_scope" />. It confirms your investment. It does not decide whether you are admitted to residency.

## What still applies

Residency remains the migration office's decision, and the law includes a ground for refusal: <Fact k="residency.criminal_record_refusal" />. Four points:

- It is a power to refuse, not a duty. Outcomes depend on the offence, timing and the rest of the file.
- Prosecutions count, not only convictions.
- The offence is measured against Paraguayan law.
- Repeat offending is a separate ground.

## What the file will carry

Your home police certificate must be <Fact k="documents.police_certificate_validity" />. Paraguayan checks follow in the process. Our [document checklist](/insights/investor-pass-document-checklist) lists everything else in the file.

## Why the order matters

An investor who commits capital, files and is then refused has paid for information that was available beforehand. A better sequence:

1. Request your certificate first.
2. If it shows anything, collect the court paperwork.
3. Take advice, ours or a lawyer's, before buying an asset or paying fees.
4. Choose the route and commit only when the risk is clear.

## Do not hide it

An official certificate cannot be edited, and a residency obtained with false or misleading documents can be cancelled at any time. That risk sits on top of an asset you have already bought.

## When "not yet" is the right answer

A case that is still open, a very recent conviction or several offences usually calls for legal advice before anything else. Sometimes the honest answer is to wait or choose a different jurisdiction; better to hear it early.
