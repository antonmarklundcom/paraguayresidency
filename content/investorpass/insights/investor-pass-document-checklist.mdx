A well-prepared Investor Pass file is boring: every document is present, current and in the right form. A poorly prepared one loses months to a missing apostille or an expired statement. This checklist puts the pieces in layers so you can see what depends on what. It summarises Resolution 0283/2026; the resolution's own text governs, and we read it against your case.

## Layer 1: the certificate application

The application to the Ministry of Industry and Commerce is made online through SUACE. The resolution lists what it needs: <Fact k="investorpass.cie_documents" />.

Once the file is complete, the certificate is issued <Fact k="investorpass.cie_issuing_term" />. That clock stops while you answer any request for more information, which is why a complete first submission matters.

## Layer 2: identity and entry

- Your passport or national ID.
- Proof of entry to Paraguay, and a consular visa if your nationality needs one. The basics of the programme are in [what the Investor Pass is](/insights/what-the-investor-pass-is).
- Your family's documents if they will be included; the family rules are in [bringing your family](/insights/bringing-your-family).

## Foreign documents

Anything issued abroad must be handled a particular way: <Fact k="investorpass.foreign_documents" />. Budget time for this; apostilles and sworn translations are where files stall. Our list of [common delays](/insights/common-delays-and-mistakes) covers the usual culprits.

## Order of work

1. Choose the route before buying anything.
2. List every document and its age limit.
3. Order the shortest-lived documents last.
4. Apostille and translate as one batch.
5. Have the file read by someone who knows the resolution before you submit.
