People with savings, a pension pot or a UK property ask the same question before they move: what happens to all of it? The short answer is that the UK keeps a claim on most of it, Paraguay mostly doesn't, and there is no treaty between the two. This page sets out the questions for your UK tax adviser. It is not advice, and we are not UK tax advisers, so we flag each point rather than settle it.

## First question: have you actually left the UK?

Everything below assumes you have stopped being UK resident for tax. That is decided by HMRC's Statutory Residence Test, based on the days you spend in the UK and the ties you keep, not by the date of your flight or your Paraguayan residency. Our [guide to UK tax when you move](/guides/taxes/uk-tax-when-moving-to-paraguay) explains the test, split-year treatment and form P85.

One more UK rule is worth knowing before you draw anything large: if you return to the UK after a short period abroad, some income and gains taken while you were away can be taxed when you come back. Ask your adviser how the temporary non-residence rules apply to your plans.

## No treaty between the UK and Paraguay

Most countries British movers consider have a double taxation agreement with the UK. Paraguay doesn't: <Fact k="uk.paraguay_tax_treaty" />. Treaties often decide which country may tax a pension, and settle cases where both countries claim you as resident. Without one, UK domestic rules decide what the UK taxes, and any relief for tax paid twice depends on each country's own rules.

In practice, because Paraguay generally doesn't tax foreign-source income, double taxation is less of a risk than the missing treaty suggests. The missing treaty mostly means there is no treaty route to take UK income out of the UK tax net.

## At a glance

| Income or asset | UK position once non-resident (confirm with your adviser) | Paraguay's position |
|---|---|---|
| UK State Pension | Can stay taxable in the UK; paid in Paraguay but frozen | Foreign-source income |
| Workplace and personal pensions | Generally taxable in the UK; providers usually deduct UK tax | Foreign-source income |
| ISA | Keep it, with UK tax relief; no new money | Foreign-source income |
| Rent from UK property | Non-Resident Landlord Scheme; UK return | Foreign-source income |
| Selling UK property | Within UK capital gains tax; short reporting deadline | Foreign-source gain |
| Income from Paraguayan sources | Generally outside UK tax once you are non-resident | Taxed in Paraguay |

## Pensions

Your **UK State Pension** can be paid to you in Paraguay. It is <Fact k="uk.state_pension_paraguay" />. It can remain taxable in the UK, and British citizens living abroad can usually still claim the UK personal allowance against it. Our [State Pension guide](/guides/living-in-paraguay/uk-state-pension-in-paraguay) covers claiming it, National Insurance top-ups and healthcare.

**Workplace and personal pensions** paid by UK providers generally stay taxable in the UK, and the provider normally deducts tax before paying you. Without a treaty there is no treaty claim to be paid without UK tax. Decisions about lump sums, drawdown timing and whether to draw before or after you leave can change what you pay, so make them with an adviser and your figures in front of you. Transferring a UK pension to an overseas scheme is a specialist area with its own charges; take regulated advice before considering it.

On the Paraguayan side, a pension from the UK is foreign-source income, and <Fact k="tax.foreign_income_treatment" />.

## ISAs

You can keep an ISA when you move abroad. GOV.UK says it keeps its UK tax relief, but you cannot pay new money into it while you are not UK resident, unless you are a Crown employee posted overseas or their spouse or civil partner. Tell your ISA provider as soon as you stop being UK resident. If you return, you can pay in again within the normal annual allowance.

Some ISA providers restrict what non-resident customers can do, such as switching funds or opening new accounts. Check yours before you leave.

## Renting out your UK home

If you let out a UK property while living in Paraguay, HMRC treats you as a non-resident landlord. Under the Non-Resident Landlord Scheme, your letting agent, or your tenant if there is no agent, deducts basic-rate tax from the rent after allowable expenses, unless HMRC approves you to receive the rent in full. You apply for that approval with HMRC's form for non-resident landlords. Either way, you normally still declare the rent on a UK Self Assessment return, which non-residents file by post or through commercial software rather than HMRC's online service.

## Selling UK property

Non-residents are within UK capital gains tax on UK land and property, and a sale has to be reported to HMRC within a short deadline, even if no tax is due. How much is due depends on how long you owned and lived in the property and when you left the UK. Get advice before you exchange contracts, not after completion.

## Paraguay's side, in brief

Paraguay taxes on a territorial basis: <Fact k="tax.territorial_rate" />. Income from Paraguayan sources, such as rent from a flat in Asunción or interest from a Paraguayan bank, is taxed there; for capital income the rule is <Fact k="tax.capital_income_rate" />. Foreign income is generally outside it. None of this makes Paraguay "tax-free", and we don't describe it that way. Our [territorial tax explainer](/guides/taxes/territorial-tax-explained) and [RUC and tax residency guide](/guides/taxes/ruc-and-tax-residency) cover the Paraguayan side in full.

## Questions to take to your UK tax adviser

1. On my expected dates and ties, when do I stop being UK resident, and does split-year treatment apply?
2. How will each of my pensions be taxed once I live in Paraguay, and should I take any lump sum before or after I leave?
3. Do the temporary non-residence rules affect anything I plan to draw or sell?
4. Should I apply to receive UK rent without tax deducted, and what will I still owe?
5. If I sell my UK home, what will I report, by when, and how much is likely to be due?
6. Does the lack of a UK–Paraguay treaty change anything for my specific income?
