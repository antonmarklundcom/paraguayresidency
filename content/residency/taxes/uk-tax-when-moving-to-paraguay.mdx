Most of what people ask us about UK tax when moving to Paraguay isn't really a Paraguay question at all — it's a "have I actually left the UK, in HMRC's eyes" question, and that's decided in London, not Asunción. This page sets out what the UK side actually involves, in plain terms, without guessing at figures or outcomes that depend on your own facts.

## Leaving the UK is a test, not a date

A common assumption is that UK tax residence ends the day you board a flight, or the day your Paraguay residency is approved. It doesn't work that way. HMRC's Statutory Residence Test decides UK tax residence for a given tax year based on a combination of the number of days you spend in the UK and the number of "connecting ties" you retain — family, accommodation, work, and time spent in the UK in previous years, among others. Two people who move to Paraguay on the same day can land on different sides of that test depending on how many ties they keep and how much time they still spend in the UK afterwards.

This is not a test you can shortcut by reading about it. It applies your actual facts — days, ties, history — against a defined structure, and the outcome genuinely varies person to person. If UK tax residence matters to your decision (and for most people moving here, it does), work through the Statutory Residence Test with a UK tax adviser before you assume you've left it behind, not after.

## Split-year treatment: sometimes, not automatically

In the tax year you actually leave, HMRC's rules can, in certain circumstances, apply split-year treatment — treating part of that year as UK-resident and part as non-resident, rather than one status for the whole year. Whether split-year treatment applies to you, and which of HMRC's specific cases covers your situation, depends on facts like whether you have full-time work abroad, where your only home is, and the exact sequence of your move. This is one of the more commonly misunderstood parts of leaving the UK, and it is squarely a UK tax adviser's job to confirm it for your dates, not a general article's.

## Form P85 and telling HMRC you've gone

Form P85 is how you formally notify HMRC that you are leaving the UK, and it can affect things like a repayment of tax already paid in the current year. Filing it is a sensible administrative step for most people who are genuinely leaving, but it is not, on its own, a determination of your tax residence status — that's still governed by the Statutory Residence Test above. Ask your adviser whether P85 is the right form for your situation, and what else it should be paired with (for example, if you have rental income or other ongoing UK filing obligations).

## UK-source income doesn't leave when you do

This is the point people miss most often: becoming non-UK-resident does not automatically remove UK tax on income that is actually sourced in the UK. Rental income from a UK property, certain UK pensions, and some other UK-source income can remain taxable in the UK regardless of where you live, subject to the specific rules that apply to that income type and any relevant treaty position. Whether and how much continues to be taxable, and what reliefs might apply, is a case-by-case question. This is not something to estimate from a residency website — confirm the treatment of each of your specific UK income sources with your adviser before you move, so there are no surprises in your first UK tax return as a non-resident.

Pensions in particular deserve a dedicated conversation. UK pension income, when and how it's paid, and any decisions about drawing it before or after your move can all interact with both UK rules and your new circumstances in Paraguay. We are not going to generalise about pensions here — get that reviewed specifically, with your actual pension arrangements in front of the adviser.

## Getting the two sides talking to each other

The mistake we see most is treating the UK exit and the Paraguay residency as two unrelated errands running on separate timelines. In practice, when you leave, how many ties you keep in the UK, and how your income is structured all interact with both the Statutory Residence Test and how straightforward your Paraguay filing turns out to be. Bringing your UK tax adviser and your Paraguay residency file into the same conversation early — rather than sorting one out and discovering a problem with the other later — is the single most useful thing we can recommend on this page.
