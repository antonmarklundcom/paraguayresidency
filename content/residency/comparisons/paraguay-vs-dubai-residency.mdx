Dubai and Paraguay come up together for one reason: people leaving the UK who want to pay less tax. They are very different places to actually live, and the residency behind each works differently too. This is a straight comparison. We are a Paraguay residency team, so we won't pretend to be UAE experts, and we don't quote UAE thresholds, which the UAE changes often. Check those with an adviser in the UAE.

## The two side by side

| | Paraguay | Dubai (UAE) |
|---|---|---|
| How you get residency | Temporary residency on documents: <Fact k="temporary.duration" /> | Usually through an employer, your own company, a freelance permit or a qualifying property or investment |
| Tied to a sponsor? | No, it is personal | Usually, yes: an employer or company |
| Personal income tax | Territorial: Paraguayan-source income only | None on salaries or personal investment income |
| Tax treaty with the UK | None | Yes |
| Staying away too long | <Fact k="temporary.presence_rule" /> | Most residence visas lapse after a long absence; some long-term visas are exempt |

| Cost of living | Low for a capital city | Among the highest in the region |
| Path to citizenship | <Fact k="citizenship.years" />, then a court application | Only by nomination in exceptional cases |

## Tax: zero, territorial, and the UK in the middle

The UAE does not tax individuals on salaries or personal investment income. Paraguay does tax income, but only income from Paraguayan sources: <Fact k="tax.territorial_rate" />, and <Fact k="tax.foreign_income_treatment" />. For someone living on foreign income, both can end up light, and that is why they share a shortlist.

The part that matters more is the UK. Moving to either country does not by itself end UK tax residence; HMRC's Statutory Residence Test decides that, on your days in the UK and the ties you keep. UK-source income, such as rent from a UK property or a UK pension, can stay taxable in the UK wherever you live.

There is one structural difference. The UK has a double taxation treaty with the UAE, and <Fact k="uk.paraguay_tax_treaty" />. A treaty can help settle questions such as where you are resident if both countries claim you, and how some income is taxed. Without one, you rely on UK domestic rules alone. Our [UK tax guide](/guides/taxes/uk-tax-when-moving-to-paraguay) sets out the questions, and [our explainer on Paraguay's territorial system](/guides/taxes/territorial-tax-explained) covers the Paraguayan side. Take both to a UK tax adviser before tax decides the country.

## Stability: sponsored visa or personal residency

This is the difference people underrate. In the UAE, most residents hold a visa sponsored by an employer or by their own company. If the job ends or the company closes, the visa is generally cancelled and you have a short window to find a new sponsor or leave. Long-term visas linked to property or investment exist, but they depend on keeping the qualifying asset.

Paraguayan residency is personal. Once granted, it does not depend on an employer, a company or an asset. You hold temporary residency, then move to [permanent residency](/residency/permanent-residency), which keeps going as long as you meet the presence rule: <Fact k="permanent.presence_rule" />. For people who want a residency they can hold in reserve while living elsewhere, that is a large part of the appeal.

## Cost of living

Dubai is expensive: rent in the areas most Britons choose is high, and schools and healthcare follow. Paraguay is one of the cheaper capitals in South America. ## Lifestyle

Dubai is a global hub: direct flights everywhere, including the UK, English everywhere, a huge British community and a fast, polished city. Summers are extremely hot. Paraguay is quieter and further away, with no direct flights to the UK. Life runs in Spanish, it is green and humid, and the foreign community is small. People tend to know within one visit which of the two they could live in.

## Who tends to choose which

**Dubai suits** people with a job or business there, people who want a big international city and short flights, and those for whom a high cost of living is an acceptable price for no income tax.

**Paraguay suits** people living on foreign income who want low costs, people who want a residency not tied to an employer, and those looking for a second residency to hold alongside life elsewhere.
