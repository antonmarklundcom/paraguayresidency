Panama and Paraguay show up on a lot of the same shortlists, usually because
both are known for straightforward-sounding paths to residency and for
territorial tax systems. Once you look closer, though, they're built for
somewhat different people. This isn't an argument that one is better than
the other — Panama has real advantages Paraguay doesn't try to match, and
vice versa. It's a look at what each one actually asks of you.

## Reputation and residency-by-investment

Panama has spent decades building an international reputation as a
banking and business hub, and its residency system reflects that. It offers
several distinct routes, including its own residency-by-investment options
that ask for a qualifying investment in real estate, a fixed-term deposit,
or certain government securities, alongside routes tied to specific
nationalities and to smaller-scale friendly-nations criteria. The exact
qualifying amounts and terms for these routes are set by Panamanian
regulation and have shifted over the years, so we won't state a figure for
Panama here — if you're seriously considering that route, Panamanian
immigration counsel is who should confirm the current numbers, not a
Paraguay-based residency site.

Paraguay's standard route doesn't ask for an investment threshold at all.
It runs on the same document set for nearly every applicant — birth
certificate, police clearance, proof of means, apostilled and translated —
filed in Asunción, moving through <Fact k="temporary.duration" /> before you
apply for permanent status. Paraguay does have its own newer
investment-linked pass for people who want a faster route to permanent
status rather than starting at the temporary stage, but for most applicants
the appeal of Paraguay's system is precisely that they don't need an
investment route at all — proof of stable means is enough. If you want to
understand where that fits against the standard track, our
[permanent residency](/residency/permanent-residency) page walks through
both.

## Banking and business environment

This is where Panama's advantage is real and worth naming directly. Panama
has a long-established international banking sector, a dollarized economy,
and a business registration system built around decades of use by
international companies. If you need sophisticated banking relationships,
an established corporate services industry, or a jurisdiction with a long
track record servicing international clients, Panama has infrastructure
Paraguay hasn't built to the same scale — yet.

Paraguay's banking sector is smaller and more domestically oriented,
though it has been opening up to foreign residents and international
business steadily. What Paraguay trades for that smaller scale is lower
friction elsewhere: simpler company formation for smaller operations, a
tax authority (the RUC system) that's more approachable for an individual
or small business than Panama's more built-out corporate apparatus, and
generally less paperwork overhead for someone not running an international
holding structure. If your business needs are modest — you're not funneling
international capital through the jurisdiction — Paraguay's simplicity can
be a genuine advantage rather than a limitation.

## Tax approach

Both countries lean territorial, which is part of why they get compared so
often. Paraguay's version is direct: <Fact k="tax.territorial_rate" />, and
<Fact k="tax.foreign_income_treatment" />. It's a small system with a
consistent rule, not a patchwork of exceptions.

Panama also taxes territorially in principle, but its system carries more
history and more nuance around what counts as Panama-sourced versus
foreign-sourced income, particularly for anyone running an active business
through a Panamanian entity rather than simply holding residency. We're not
going to state a Panamanian tax rate or threshold here — those are set by
Panamanian law, they've been revised over time, and getting them right
matters too much to guess at from outside the country. If tax treatment is
the deciding factor for you, get a review from an accountant licensed in
whichever country you're actually going to file in. We go through how
Paraguay's own system works, plainly, in
[our territorial tax explainer](/guides/taxes/territorial-tax-explained).

## Cost and lifestyle

Panama City runs a genuinely international cost structure in its business
and financial districts — closer to what you'd pay in a mid-tier US or
European city than to the regional average, though costs ease considerably
outside the capital and in the interior. It also has the Panama Canal
economy behind it, English spoken widely in business contexts, and a
skyline that reflects decades of foreign capital.

Asunción, by comparison, is consistently cited as one of the more
affordable capitals in South America — lower rents, lower everyday costs,
and a noticeably slower pace of daily life. Guaraní is spoken alongside
Spanish, and the city is smaller and less internationally visible than
Panama City. Neither trait is automatically better: some people relocating
want the international infrastructure and networking that comes with a hub
like Panama; others are specifically trying to get away from that pace and
cost, and Asunción suits them better. If cost of living is central to your
decision, [our detailed look at living costs in Asunción](/guides/living-in-paraguay/cost-of-living-in-asuncion)
lays out the categories that actually move a household budget.

## Who tends to prefer which

Investors and business owners who want an internationally recognized
jurisdiction, an established banking sector, and are comfortable with a
larger qualifying investment tend to lean toward Panama. It suits people for
whom the residency itself is one piece of a broader international business
or asset-holding strategy.

People who want a simpler standard route without an investment threshold,
a genuinely lower cost of living, and territorial tax treatment without
needing a complex corporate structure to benefit from it tend to lean toward
Paraguay. It suits people whose priority is a working, affordable residency
rather than a flagship international address.
