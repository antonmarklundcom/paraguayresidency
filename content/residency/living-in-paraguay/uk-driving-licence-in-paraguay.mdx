Driving is one of the first practical questions British movers ask, and one of the easiest to get wrong because the answer changes when you stop being a visitor. This short guide covers driving on a UK licence as a visitor, getting a Paraguayan licence once you live here, and keeping your UK licence in good order.

## As a visitor: your UK photocard licence works

The FCDO's travel advice for Paraguay is clear on the basics: <Fact k="driving.uk_licence_visitors" />. If you still hold an old paper licence, check the FCDO's advice on international driving permits (IDPs) before you travel, because a paper licence alone may not be enough.

Police checks on the roads are a normal part of driving in Paraguay, so keep your licence, passport and the vehicle's papers in the car rather than at the hotel. If you are hiring a car, check that the insurance covers you as a foreign licence holder.

## Once you live here: a Paraguayan licence

Visitor rules are for visitors. If you are moving to Paraguay, GOV.UK's own advice is not to rely on an IDP but to exchange your UK licence or apply for a licence in the country you move to. In Paraguay the current position is <Fact k="driving.resident_licence" />.

Two things surprise British movers here:

- **Licences are municipal.** There is no national equivalent of the DVLA handling your application. You apply to the municipality where you live, such as the Municipalidad de Asunción if you live in the capital, and requirements and fees can differ between municipalities.
- **The cédula comes first.** In practice, municipalities ask for your Paraguayan cédula, and the cédula is only issued after your residency is approved: <Fact k="cedula.timeline" />. That leaves a gap between arriving as a new resident and holding a local licence, and it is worth planning how you will get around during it. Ask us about the current position for your dates.

## What the exchange usually involves

Municipalities publish their own requirements, and Asunción's are posted as images on its website rather than as text, so check the current list with your municipality before you go. Applicants are typically asked for:

- your cédula,
- your valid UK licence, authenticated and translated into Spanish,
- a blood group certificate from a Paraguayan laboratory,
- any medical or other checks the municipality requires, and the municipal fee.

The authentication step catches people out. A UK photocard licence is not the kind of document the FCDO can apostille on its own, so the usual route is a copy certified by a UK solicitor or notary, then apostilled by the FCDO, then translated in Paraguay. If you are already ordering apostilles for your residency documents, add the licence to the same batch. Our [guide to apostilling UK documents](/guides/documents/apostille-uk-documents-for-paraguay) explains the FCDO process.

## What is different on the road

For a British driver, the obvious change is the side of the road: Paraguay drives on the right, and most cars for sale or hire are automatics or left-hand-drive manuals. Beyond that, a few things take adjusting to. Motorbikes are everywhere and weave through traffic, so check your mirrors more often than you would at home. Heavy summer storms can flood low-lying streets in Asunción within minutes; locals simply wait for the water to drain, and so should you. Outside the cities, main roads are generally good, but lighting, signage and road markings can be sparse, and livestock on rural roads is not unusual. Many people who move here drive less than they expected, because ride-hailing apps and taxis are cheap by UK standards.

## Keep your UK licence in good order

Don't let your UK licence lapse. It is the document you exchange, and you may want it again if you return to the UK. The DVLA deals with licences for people who live in Great Britain, so renewing from an overseas address may not be possible: check with the DVLA before you leave, and renew early if your photocard is close to expiry.

## A short checklist

1. Check your UK licence is a photocard and in date, and renew it before you move if it is close to expiry.
2. If you hold a paper licence, read the FCDO's IDP advice before your first trip.
3. Add a certified, apostilled copy of your licence to your residency document batch.
4. Once your cédula is issued, apply to your municipality for the Paraguayan licence.
