Almost every British mover wants to keep at least one UK bank account, and for good reasons: a UK pension, rent from a flat, HMRC, direct debits and the odd UK purchase all run through it. Whether you can keep it is up to your bank. This page covers what British banks tend to do when a customer moves abroad, how to handle it honestly, and how it fits with opening an account in Paraguay.

## Can you keep it? It depends on the bank

There is no single UK rule. Each bank decides how it treats customers who live abroad, and the answer can differ by country and by product. In broad terms you will meet three responses:

- **The account carries on as it is**, sometimes with a few products (new savings accounts, credit cards, overdrafts) closed to non-residents.
- **You are moved to an international or expat account**, often run from Jersey, Guernsey or the Isle of Man, which may have eligibility rules, a minimum balance or a monthly fee.
- **The bank gives notice and closes the account.**

Policies change, so the only reliable answer is your own bank's, in writing, before you move. If your main bank will close the account, it is far easier to open a second UK account while you still live at a UK address than after you have left.

## Tell your bank, and tell it the truth

Banks ask where you live and where you are resident for tax because the law requires them to. When you move, update your address and your country of tax residence. The tempting shortcut, keeping a parent's or friend's address as if it were yours, can breach your account terms and leaves you exposed if the bank later checks. If you need someone in the UK to handle post, ask the bank how it deals with a correspondence address for customers living abroad.

Whether you have actually stopped being UK resident for tax is a separate question, decided by HMRC's Statutory Residence Test rather than by your bank. Our [guide to UK tax when you move](/guides/taxes/uk-tax-when-moving-to-paraguay) explains it.

## Keep your UK phone number

The practical thing that locks people out of UK banking is not the bank's policy but a text message. Most UK banking apps still send security codes by SMS to a UK mobile number. Keep that number working after you leave, on a cheap SIM-only plan or an eSIM, and check that you can receive texts in Paraguay before you need to.

## What to keep running through a UK account

A UK account is the natural home for UK money:

- **Your State Pension**, if you draw one. GOV.UK lets you choose a UK account or one in the country you live in. The pension is <Fact k="uk.state_pension_paraguay" />, which our [State Pension guide](/guides/living-in-paraguay/uk-state-pension-in-paraguay) explains.
- **UK rental income** and any letting agent payments.
- **HMRC payments or refunds**, and direct debits you keep, such as a UK mobile plan.
- **An ISA.** You can keep an existing ISA when you move abroad but you cannot pay new money into it, as GOV.UK explains. Our guide to [UK pensions, ISAs and property after the move](/guides/taxes/uk-pensions-isas-and-property-after-moving-to-paraguay) covers the tax side.

## Moving money to Paraguay

You will move money from the UK to Paraguay regularly, so compare the total cost of a transfer, meaning the exchange rate as well as the fee. Specialist transfer services are often cheaper than a high-street bank's international payment. Paraguayan banks commonly offer accounts in both guaraníes and US dollars, which gives you some choice about when you convert.

## Opening an account in Paraguay

A fully working Paraguayan bank account, with cards and transfers, generally waits on your cédula, not just on your residency approval. The cédula timeline is <Fact k="cedula.timeline" />, so plan for months of running your UK account alone before the Paraguayan one exists. Our guide to [opening a bank account in Paraguay](/guides/living-in-paraguay/opening-a-bank-account) covers what Paraguayan banks ask for.

## A checklist before you leave

1. Ask your bank, in writing, what happens to your account when you live in Paraguay.
2. If it will close the account, open a second UK account while you still have a UK address.
3. Keep your UK mobile number and test that texts reach you abroad.
4. Update your address and tax residence honestly, and set up a correspondence arrangement if you need one.
5. Tell your ISA provider that you are no longer UK resident.
6. Decide where your State Pension and any UK rent will be paid.
7. Set up a transfer service before you need to send money in a hurry.
