A German certificate can be carefully issued, multilingual and perfectly useful at home without being ready for a Paraguay residency file. The problem is usually not the quality of the document. It is the assumption that a familiar certification or an existing Spanish text settles every question the receiving office will ask.

For German applicants, it helps to separate the record, its authentication and its translation. They do different jobs. Work out who issued the record, confirm the apostille route for that issuer, and only then settle the local translation package. That sequence gives you something concrete to check instead of a vague assurance that the document is “international”.

## Start with the civil record you actually need

Ask the relevant civil registry which birth or marriage record it can issue for the intended foreign use. Tell us which version you are considering before ordering. A register extract, a certificate and a multilingual version may present information differently; the useful question is whether the requested version contains the information your Paraguay file needs.

An existing document can be a good reference for that conversation. Send a legible scan for review, including endorsements and the reverse if it contains text. Do not assume a shorter format is preferable because it is easier to translate, or that a more elaborate format must be necessary simply because it exists.

The place of registration also matters. A German passport does not establish that your birth or marriage record is German-issued. If the underlying event was registered abroad, identify the authority that issued the document you intend to use. Its country of origin may determine a separate authentication path.

## Germany's federal structure affects the route

Germany and Paraguay are members of the Hague Apostille Convention. For covered documents, the apostille provides the authentication route instead of consular legalisation. It confirms the relevant official signature or seal, not the accuracy of every entry or the document's suitability for a particular residency application.

Germany has federal and regional issuing structures. The competent apostille body can depend on whether a document comes from a federal authority or from an authority within a particular Land, as well as the type of document. Do not assume the office handling a civil record will also handle a federal criminal-record certificate.

The [German guidance on apostilles](https://uk.diplo.de/uk-en/02/legalisation/apostilles-2613188) recommends asking the original issuing authority when the competent office is unclear. That is a sensible starting point for your checklist. Confirm any required preliminary certification with that authority and the intended apostille body before posting the original.

A chain described by a friend in another region may be accurate for their document and wrong for yours. Your present address is not a substitute for identifying the issuer. The practical aim is to attach the correct authentication to the correct public record, without adding unneeded notarisation or assuming a regional document must always pass through a federal office.

## The criminal-record certificate is its own document

For a German applicant, the likely document to discuss is an official certificate of conduct or criminal-record certificate from the competent federal records authority. The request may be handled through a local application channel or an available remote procedure. Confirm the actual issuer, request category and delivery arrangement for your circumstances; the place where you apply is not necessarily the issuing authority.

Expect a formal record identifying the applicant and stating the relevant result of the criminal-record enquiry. Different purposes can call for different forms or disclosure arrangements. Do not choose a category simply because an employer previously asked for it, and do not assume a certificate sent directly to a domestic authority is the same product you can submit abroad.

Before requesting it, ask how the accepted form reaches you, whether it can follow the intended apostille route, and what Paraguay needs to see. If fingerprinting or another identity step is suggested by a provider, have its necessity confirmed for your actual request rather than importing instructions from another nationality's checklist.

## Multilingual does not automatically mean filing-ready

The local rule explained in our [apostille and translation guide](/guides/documents/apostilles-and-translations) remains the starting point: use a certified or sworn translator recognised in Paraguay. A German sworn translator's qualification does not automatically satisfy the receiving office's local recognition requirement.

Apply that rule to the complete German set, including the criminal-record certificate and any civil records. If a multilingual certificate contains Spanish, ask for a review rather than declaring the translation unnecessary. The apostille, marginal notes or accompanying endorsements may need attention even where part of the original is already understandable in Spanish.

Normally, arrange local translation after authentication. Ask the translator to preserve the meaning of names, place names and administrative terms rather than silently making the documents look alike. Differences involving umlauts, transliteration or a married surname should be flagged against the passport before filing. A translation should expose the record clearly, not conceal a discrepancy.

## Resolve the exceptions before dispatch

Keep copies for reference and establish where originals will be returned. Check that attachments remain with their records and that every document arriving for translation is the version reviewed for the file. If a registry issues a replacement, have the authentication path checked again rather than assuming the old package transfers.

The useful result is not a promise that German paperwork will be effortless. It is knowing what each document still needs before you send it to the next person.
