Two different things get called "the Interpol check" in conversations about Paraguay residency, and mixing them up is one of the more common sources of confusion in a document file. Separating them clearly, before you request anything, saves a step later.

## The check Paraguay runs, and the certificate you provide

The first is internal to the Paraguayan process: immigration authorities screen applicants against international law-enforcement records as part of reviewing a residency application. You do not request this, arrange it, or receive a document for it — it happens on their side, using the identity information already in your file.

The second is the document you are actually asked to provide: a police or background certificate issued by the competent authority in your own country of nationality (or, in some cases, your country of recent residence). This is the "police certificate" referenced throughout our nationality-specific document guides. Confusion arises because some countries' issuing process for that certificate is administered through their national Interpol bureau as the office that handles international-use requests — Interpol is the *channel* their government uses to issue the paper, not a separate certificate Paraguay is asking for on top of it.

## Why the naming varies by country

Whether your national certificate is called a "police clearance certificate," a "certificate of good conduct," a "criminal record extract," or informally "the Interpol certificate" depends entirely on which authority in your country issues it and how that country's own guidance describes it. <Fact k="documents.police_certificate_validity" site="residency" /> applies regardless of what the certificate is called locally — the recency window matters more than the label on the document.

Do not assume the presence or absence of the word "Interpol" on a certificate changes whether Paraguay accepts it. What matters is:

- **Issuer.** Confirm the certificate comes from the authority your country's own guidance names as competent for a criminal-record check "for use abroad" or "for immigration purposes" — not a private background-check company, and not a certificate produced for an unrelated purpose such as domestic employment.
- **Scope.** A local or municipal check is not always the same as a national one. If you have lived in more than one country as an adult, more than one certificate may be required — this comes up often for applicants who moved countries mid-career.
- **Form.** The certificate needs to be the kind of official document that can carry an apostille (see our [apostille and translation guide](/guides/documents/apostilles-and-translations)), not a printout or an online verification page with no signature or seal.

## Where this fits in your filing sequence

The police certificate is one part of the wider document set — alongside your birth certificate and, where applicable, your marriage certificate — that gets apostilled and then translated for the Paraguayan immigration office. The [Document Checklist tool](/documents/checklist) walks through the full set for your route and lets you flag which certificate applies to your case; our nationality guides (American, British, Canadian, Australian, German and South African applicants) go into the issuer-by-country detail this page deliberately keeps general.

## What to do if you are not sure which certificate you need

Tell us your nationality, your country of current residence if different, and which countries you have lived in as an adult. We confirm which certificate (or certificates) your case actually needs, the issuing office, and the current validity window — in writing, before you request anything or pay a fee to an intermediary. Getting this wrong is one of the more expensive mistakes to fix late, because a certificate can expire again before a reissued one arrives.
