An Australian document set usually spans more than one issuing authority. Your birth or marriage certificate comes from a state or territory registry, your criminal history check from a federal police service, and your passport confirms none of the details on either. Treating the whole folder as "Australian paperwork" that one office can process is where avoidable delays start.

The more useful approach is to separate each document by who issued it and what it actually is, then work out the authentication path for that specific record. Once that is clear, the rest of the checklist is mostly a matter of sequencing.

## Birth and marriage records are a state or territory matter

Australian births, deaths and marriages are recorded by the registry of the relevant state or territory, not by a single national office. Check which registry issued your certificate, and ask that registry which certified version is suitable for use overseas. A certificate held for years, a family copy, and a fresh certified extract are not necessarily interchangeable for this purpose, so confirm the required form before assuming what you already have will do.

If the birth or marriage took place outside Australia, note that separately. Australian citizenship does not bring a foreign civil record into the Australian apostille system. The issuing country's own authentication process may apply instead, so identify it before adding that document to an otherwise all-Australian checklist.

## DFAT issues the apostille, but the document still has to be right

Australia and Paraguay are both members of the Hague Apostille Convention. For documents covered by the Convention, the apostille replaces the consular legalisation chain that used to apply. In Australia, apostilles are issued by the [Department of Foreign Affairs and Trade](https://www.dfat.gov.au/) (DFAT), which also publishes guidance through its consular and Smartraveller channels on what a document needs before an apostille can be attached.

An apostille authenticates the relevant official signature or seal. It does not decide whether the underlying certificate is the version Paraguay expects, and it does not add missing information to a document that was the wrong one to begin with. Ask DFAT's current guidance, or the issuing registry, whether your specific certificate needs a preliminary certified-copy step before an apostille can be applied, rather than assuming every document goes through the same short process.

Do not add an extra certification step out of caution if nothing requires it, and do not skip one because a different document in your file did not need it. Each record's path depends on what it is and who issued it.

## The AFP National Police Check is a specific product

For an Australian applicant, the likely document for the criminal-history section of the file is a National Police Check issued by the [Australian Federal Police](https://www.afp.gov.au/) (AFP), or through an accredited body on the AFP's behalf. Confirm the purpose category you request it under, since a check obtained for employment, volunteering or a licence application may not be framed the way an immigration or residency application expects.

The finished document should be an official certificate identifying you and stating the outcome of the relevant national police history check. Review the stated purpose where the certificate shows one, along with the issuing details, before treating it as ready to send for authentication. Ask which version can proceed through the DFAT apostille route and, separately, whether it is the form Paraguay accepts; confirming one does not confirm the other.

If an accredited agency handled your application, keep its role as an intermediary separate from the AFP as the actual issuer. The apostille attaches to the certificate you receive, not to the convenience of whichever service helped you apply for it.

An apostille added afterwards does not reset the date on the underlying record.

Work out how you will request, receive and authenticate the accepted certificate before you apply for it. Requesting a check under the wrong purpose category first, then discovering it needs to be reissued, tends to cost more time than confirming the category beforehand.

## Translate the complete package locally in Paraguay

The rule set out in our [apostille and translation guide](/guides/documents/apostilles-and-translations) applies to Australian documents as well: use a certified or sworn translator recognised locally in Paraguay. A NAATI-certified translation obtained in Australia does not, on its own, meet that local requirement.

Plan the translation around the final birth or marriage certificate and the National Police Check, once each is authenticated. Give the translator the complete package, including the apostille and any attached endorsements, so the Spanish version matches what is actually being filed. Authentication normally comes before translation for that reason.

Check names before that handoff. If a certificate uses a maiden name, a name recorded with a different spelling, or a name changed since the document was issued, flag it with supporting evidence rather than expecting the translation to reconcile the difference.

## Leave with a checked folder, not a hopeful one

Before sending originals anywhere, keep legible reference scans and a note of who holds each document. When it returns, check that the apostille belongs to that specific document and that no attachment has gone missing. Review the complete set together before treating delivery as the end of the task.
