An American document folder can contain records from unrelated issuing systems. Your birth certificate may come from a state or local registry, your background record from a federal authority, and your marriage evidence from somewhere else again. Sending the whole bundle to whichever apostille service is nearest is not a reliable way to make it ready for Paraguay.

The useful starting point is jurisdiction. Identify who issued each record, then confirm who can authenticate that issuer's signature. Your current state of residence does not necessarily answer either question. Once you separate those routes, the paperwork becomes easier to manage without pretending it all moves through the same office.

## Build the checklist around issuers

List the birth record, police or background certificate, and any marriage or name-change evidence your case requires. Beside each item, write the issuing jurisdiction and the form you hold: official certified copy, original record, electronic result or informal scan. These descriptions matter more than the folder label “US documents”.

For civil records, ask the relevant registry which version is suitable for foreign use and authentication. A hospital keepsake, a scanned family copy and a certified registry record may describe the same event, but they do not necessarily carry the same evidential status. We would check the requested form against your Paraguay checklist before you pay for a replacement.

If the event occurred outside the US, raise that separately. American nationality does not mean every record in your file was issued by an American authority. A foreign-issued certificate may need a different country's authentication process, even if you have always used it alongside your US passport.

## State and federal apostilles are different routes

The US and Paraguay are members of the Hague Apostille Convention. The apostille route generally replaces consular legalisation for covered public documents. It does not turn every American record into a federal document or make the same authentication office competent for every signature.

The [official US apostille guidance](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/replace-certify-docs/authenticate-your-document/apostille-requirements.html) distinguishes federal documents from state documents. A federal record generally follows the designated federal authentication route; state or local records generally follow the competent route in the relevant state. Confirm any preliminary certification for the exact record rather than adding it automatically.

This distinction is especially important if a service proposes to notarise a copy. Ask whether the resulting apostille would authenticate the public record itself or a notarial statement about a copy. Do not assume these are equivalent for Paraguay. A genuine apostille can still sit on a document that does not meet the receiving office's requirements.

There is no benefit in building a longer chain merely because it sounds more official. Nor should you skip a required certification because another state's registry issued an apparently similar certificate without it. Work from the issuing record and its applicable instructions.

## Choose the background record before choosing the provider

For a US applicant, the likely starting point is a national criminal-history record issued through the federal records authority. Depending on the request, fingerprint collection or an authorised intermediary may be involved. Confirm the accepted issuer and form for your residency route rather than relying on a provider's description of its product as an immigration check.

The result may be an official criminal-history response identifying you and reporting the outcome of the records search. A commercial screening report, state clearance or local police letter can be a different product with different coverage. None should be substituted simply because it is convenient to obtain. Ask what record the filing team needs, then work backwards to the correct application process.

Keep the distinction between collector and issuer clear. A business that takes fingerprints or forwards a request is not necessarily the authority issuing the final record. The apostille path follows the document you receive, not the location of the business that helped you request it.

## Coordinate readiness without inventing a deadline

For the background certificate, <Fact k="documents.police_certificate_validity" />. Establish the applicable acceptance conditions before deciding when to request it. An authentication stamp does not erase the underlying issue date, and a delivery confirmation does not establish that the certificate is acceptable at filing.

Civil records and police records may be prepared through separate channels, but they still need to meet in a coherent file. Track what is pending with each issuer, what is awaiting authentication and what is ready for translation. If something changes, review the affected document instead of assuming the whole bundle remains equally ready.

For the broader application, plan around <Fact k="residency.timeline" />. A service's estimate for processing an apostille is not an estimate for the Paraguayan residency decision. Keeping those separate makes travel planning more honest and makes it easier to see which part of the chain actually needs attention.

## Apply the Paraguayan translation rule to the finished set

The rule in our [apostille and translation guide](/guides/documents/apostilles-and-translations) applies to American documents too: translations need a certified or sworn translator recognised locally in Paraguay. Certification held by a translator in the US is not enough on its own. Do not commission an English-to-Spanish version at home on the assumption that any certified translation will travel with the original.

Normally, authenticate the requested record and then send the complete package for local translation. Include the apostille, endorsements and any relevant reverse side. A translator working from an incomplete scan may not be able to produce a translation corresponding to the final filing set.

Review suffixes, middle names and name changes before this stage. Tell us if a civil record and passport use different forms of your name. The appropriate response may involve supporting evidence or a correction through the issuer; it should not be an improvised change in translation.

## Make the handoff explicit

Before dispatching originals, establish the return destination and keep reference copies. When documents come back, match every authentication to its underlying record and check the attachments. Mark a document as ready only after its content, authentication and translation plan have been reviewed together.

Use the [document checklist](/guides/documents/what-you-need-to-apply) to frame your initial review, then bring us the issuing details rather than a guess about which office ought to handle them. The most useful outcome is a separate, confirmed path for each record, with clear responsibility for the point where those paths meet in Paraguay.
