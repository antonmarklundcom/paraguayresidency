"How much does Paraguay residency cost?" has two answers, and people usually mix them up. One is what the Paraguayan government charges. The other is everything you pay on the way to the counter. This page separates them for a British applicant, line by line, so you can budget before you commit to anyone.

## How the government fees are set

The fees are not a fixed amount in guaraní that changes once a year. They are <Fact k="fees.basis" />. Two consequences: any figure you read has a shelf life, and a pound figure is only as good as the exchange rate used. Ours uses <Fact k="fx.reference_rate" />; convert with the live rate on the day.

## The UK side

This is where budgets get vague, so here is what to count rather than a made-up total:

- **The ACRO Police Certificate**, which has to be <Fact k="documents.police_certificate_validity" />. Our [ACRO guide](/guides/documents/uk-police-certificate-acro-for-paraguay) shows how to request it.
- **Apostilles** on the certificate and on any birth or marriage certificates. See [apostilling UK documents](/guides/documents/apostille-uk-documents-for-paraguay).
- **Translations** into Spanish where required.

Each is paid to a different body, and none is in the government table above.

## The all-in figure

Doing it yourself, <Fact k="costs.diy_total" />. That includes government fees, translations and apostilles, and excludes flights, accommodation and any service fee. The most expensive mistake is not overpaying for a document; it is ordering the wrong one, or ordering it too early and letting it expire before your appointment.

## Agent or DIY?

The government fees are identical either way. What differs is your time and your risk of re-ordering documents.
