If you are preparing to leave South Africa, document collection can look like something to finish between packing and arranging travel. That is where the difficulty starts. A request receipt is not a police clearance, a collected certificate is not necessarily ready for an apostille, and a courier delivering a bundle does not mean anyone has checked its contents.

Build your Paraguay document plan around those handoffs. Know who requests the record, who checks it, who sends it for authentication and where it goes afterwards. This matters particularly if you expect to be outside South Africa while part of the work is still underway. Distance makes an unclear instruction harder to repair.

## Check civil record formats before requesting replacements

Start with your birth certificate and any marriage or name-change evidence relevant to your application. Ask the civil registration authority which available format contains the information required for foreign use, then have that version checked against your Paraguay checklist. Do not assume that a shortened record and a fuller record will be treated as interchangeable.

That is a question about the document's contents, not an instruction to order every version offered. A scan of what you already hold can help establish whether a replacement is needed. Include any reverse-side text and endorsements so that the review is based on the complete record rather than the attractive part of the page.

If you were born or married abroad, identify where the certificate was issued. South African citizenship does not change a foreign document's origin. An overseas record may need authentication through its own issuing country, and that should be visible on the checklist before someone agrees to collect your South African documents together.

## Use the apostille route for the relevant public record

South Africa and Paraguay are members of the Hague Apostille Convention. For covered public documents, the apostille route replaces consular legalisation. You should not add a consular step merely because an older relocation checklist describes one. Confirm the applicable path for your actual document instead.

The practical starting point for the usual official civil and police records is a national issuing and authentication framework, with the competent apostille route checked by document type. This should not be treated as the US-style division between federal records and state civil records. Nor does it mean every South African document goes straight to the same desk without preliminary checks.

The [South African legalisation guidance](https://www.gov.za/services/services-residents/travel-outside-sa/legalising-official-documents-and-end-user) notes that the preparation can depend on the documentation involved. Ask whether your particular original or certified record needs a prior signature or certification, and which designated apostille body can authenticate it. Avoid inserting an extra copy-certification step simply because a collection agent routinely offers that service.

An apostille authenticates the official signature or seal in the chain. It does not supply missing civil information or guarantee acceptance of the record for residency. If the underlying version is wrong, the authentication can be genuine and the file can still need a replacement.

## Distinguish police clearance from collection assistance

For a South African applicant, the likely starting point is an official police clearance certificate issued through the national police records authority. Fingerprint collection may form part of that request. Confirm the accepted request procedure, identification requirements and certificate form before arranging collection, especially if you are already abroad.

The finished document should be an official record identifying you and stating the result of the relevant criminal-record enquiry. A private screening report or a letter confirming that fingerprints were submitted is not the same thing. Ask to see which actual document the service intends to deliver and who issues it.

A collection agent may help with logistics without being the issuer or deciding what Paraguay accepts. Keep those roles separate when reading an offer. If somebody promises a complete “clearance package”, ask whether that means the official certificate, its authentication, or only assistance with the application. Clear scope is more useful than a reassuring product name.

For the police certificate, <Fact k="documents.police_certificate_validity" />. Establish those conditions alongside the filing plan before deciding when to request the record. The apostille does not refresh the underlying certificate, and the date a courier collects it does not answer the receiving authority's recency question.

Your residence history may also matter. If you have lived outside South Africa, tell us which countries are involved so that any additional clearance requirement can be checked. Do not assume that nationality alone determines the entire police-record section of the file.

If you plan to leave before collection is complete, confirm the practical arrangements while you can still change them. Who can receive the document? What authorisation, if any, does the issuer require for collection? Can a correction be requested from abroad? These are questions for the relevant issuing process, not promises a Paraguay filing team should invent on its behalf.

## Translate the completed package locally

English originals still follow the rule in our [apostille and translation guide](/guides/documents/apostilles-and-translations): use a certified or sworn translator recognised in Paraguay. A South African translator's certification does not, by itself, meet that local requirement. The same point applies if part of your document set is in another South African language.

Normally, finish authentication before commissioning the filing translation. Supply the complete police certificate, civil records, apostilles and attached endorsements for review. Do not detach pages to make scanning easier or send only the certificate while leaving the authentication with a collection agent.

Look closely at surnames and personal details before translation. If a marriage or name change explains a difference from your passport, flag the supporting record. If the difference is an apparent issuing error, ask how it can be corrected. The translator should not be asked to choose whichever spelling makes the bundle look consistent.

## Make departure a handoff you can explain

Keep reference scans and a simple status record showing where every original is held and what happens next. When a package arrives, check its contents before forwarding it. That inspection is the opportunity to catch a missing endorsement or an unexpected certificate format while the route back to the issuer is still clear.

Use our [core document guide](/guides/documents/what-you-need-to-apply) to prepare the initial list, then have the South African collection and authentication arrangements checked against it. You do not need to pretend the work is quick. You need to know that the people handling it are passing the right documents to each other.
