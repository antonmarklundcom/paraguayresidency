Your British passport is a useful starting point for a Paraguay residency checklist. It does not tell us where your birth was registered, which police certificate you already hold, or whether the name on your marriage certificate matches the name you travel under. Those details decide what happens to your documents before they can join your application.

The avoidable mistake is to treat everything in the folder as equally ready. A certificate can look official and still be the wrong version for authentication or the wrong kind of evidence for immigration. Start by checking the actual documents, not by booking every service that has “legalisation” in its name.

## Separate nationality from the origin of each record

If your birth and marriage were registered in the UK, begin with the relevant civil registration authority and confirm the certified record needed for your file. Registration arrangements differ across the UK, so we would check the issuing authority on the certificate rather than assume your current address identifies it. A family photocopy is useful for an initial review, but should not be treated as the filing document without confirmation.

If you became British after being born elsewhere, your birth certificate may follow the authentication system of that other country. The British passport does not move a foreign civil record into the UK apostille system. The same issue can arise with a marriage celebrated abroad. Put the issuing country beside each document on your checklist before requesting replacements.

That small distinction prevents a frustrating loop: sending a document to a perfectly legitimate office that cannot authenticate the signature on it. It also helps us identify which questions can be settled together and which need separate instructions.

## The UK apostille route is broadly national

The UK and Paraguay are members of the Hague Apostille Convention. For documents covered by that route, an apostille replaces the consular legalisation chain. It authenticates the relevant official signature or seal; it does not decide whether Paraguay accepts that particular certificate for your application.

For a UK-issued document, the working route is generally from the issuing authority to the UK's designated apostille body, with any required certification checked beforehand. This is broadly a national legalisation structure, even though the underlying records may come from different registration systems. It is not a reason to assume every document can be submitted in the same form.

The [UK legalisation guidance](https://www.gov.uk/get-document-legalised) distinguishes documents that can be submitted directly from those needing certification first. Use that distinction to ask about your exact certificate. Do not arrange a solicitor's copy simply because someone else needed one for a different document. An added certification changes which signature is being authenticated and may not produce the evidence your file needs.

## Ask for the police certificate suited to the purpose

For British applicants, the likely starting point is a police certificate intended for use overseas, obtained through the relevant police certificate issuing service. Confirm the service and certificate category for your circumstances before applying. A domestic employment disclosure, a personal records request and an overseas police certificate should not be treated as interchangeable just because they all concern criminal history.

Expect an official certificate or record identifying you and setting out the result of the relevant records check. Review the personal details, the stated purpose where applicable, and the issuing signature or certification. Ask which form can proceed through the apostille route and which form Paraguay will accept. Those are related questions, but neither answer supplies the other automatically.

An employer having accepted a previous check tells you little about its suitability here. Likewise, a provider saying it can obtain an apostille does not settle whether the underlying check covers what the residency file requires. We would rather resolve that before you order than explain a rejection after the document has crossed borders.

Ask what event the receiving authority uses when assessing your document, and whether a change in the filing plan calls for another review. Do not assume the apostille refreshes the police certificate or creates a new acceptance window.

Your residence history matters alongside your nationality. If you have lived outside the UK, tell us where before treating the British clearance as the whole criminal-record part of the file. Additional evidence may be requested depending on your history and route. We need the history to check that possibility; we cannot infer it from the passport cover.

The useful preparation is to establish how you will request, receive and authenticate the accepted certificate. Ordering first and investigating acceptance afterwards can leave you with a finished document that never belonged on the checklist.

## English originals still need the local translation route

Our [apostille and translation guide](/guides/documents/apostilles-and-translations) explains the rule we apply here: use a certified or sworn translator recognised locally in Paraguay. A translator being certified in the UK does not, by itself, satisfy that requirement. English documents are not an exception simply because someone on the filing team can read them.

For your British set, plan the translation around the final birth record, police certificate and any marriage or name-change evidence requested. Have the translator review the apostille and attached endorsements as part of the complete package. Authentication normally comes before translation, so that the Spanish version corresponds to the document actually being filed.

Check names before that handoff. If your birth record uses a previous surname, identify the evidence connecting it to your passport. If a middle name appears inconsistently, flag it rather than editing a scan or expecting the translator to make the records agree. Translation conveys the record; it does not repair it.

## Leave with a checked folder, not a hopeful one

Before sending originals anywhere, keep legible reference scans and record who holds each document. When it returns, check the apostille belongs to that document and that attachments remain intact. Ask for a review of the complete set before treating delivery as completion.

Bring the questions that remain unresolved to us through the [contact page](/contact): the issuer, the certificate category, any name discrepancy and any document from outside the UK. We can then build the collection order around your actual file. The goal is straightforward: every document has a clear purpose, an appropriate authentication path and a local translation plan before it reaches the counter.
