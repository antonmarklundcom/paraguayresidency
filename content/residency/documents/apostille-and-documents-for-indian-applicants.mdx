Indian citizens can live in Paraguay on the same residency routes as everyone else. The difference is at the very start: unlike British or European applicants, most Indians need a visa before they can enter Paraguay at all. After that, the file looks much like any other nationality's: civil records, a police clearance and proof of means, apostilled at home and translated in Paraguay. This page takes the steps in order.

## Step one: the entry visa

<Fact k="visa.india_consular" />. Paraguay has an embassy in New Delhi, and India has one in Asunción. Apply for the visa well before you intend to travel, and confirm the current requirements, fees and processing time with the embassy rather than a visa agency's summary. Some agency pages still claim visa-free entry for Indian passports; don't book flights on that basis.

Residency itself is not applied for at the embassy. The standard route is to enter Paraguay on the visa and then file for [temporary residency](/residency/temporary-residency) in person in Asunción. Temporary residency lasts <Fact k="temporary.duration" />. Keep the passport page with the visa and your entry stamp safe: proof of lawful entry is part of the file.

## The documents you will need

The core set is the same as for any applicant, and our [document checklist guide](/guides/documents/what-you-need-to-apply) covers it in full:

- a **birth certificate**, and a **marriage certificate** if your spouse is applying with you or your name has changed,
- a **police clearance certificate** from India, and from any other country you have lived in recently,
- **proof of means**, explained below,
- your **passport**, valid for the whole process.

Everything issued in India needs an apostille, and everything not in Spanish needs a Spanish translation made in Paraguay.

## The police clearance certificate

For use abroad, Indian applicants usually request a police clearance certificate (PCC) through Passport Seva, at a Passport Seva Kendra or regional passport office. A PCC issued that way is generally the most straightforward to apostille. A certificate from a local police station follows a longer authentication route, so ask before you choose it.

The recency rule matters here: the certificate must be <Fact k="documents.police_certificate_validity" />. Many Indian applicants have worked in the Gulf, the UK or elsewhere before moving on, and each of those countries' certificates is part of the file too. Request them in the order that lets them all arrive inside the window. If anything appears on any certificate, read our guide to [residency with a criminal record](/guides/documents/paraguay-residency-with-a-criminal-record) before you go further.

## Apostilles from the Ministry of External Affairs

India is a member of the Hague Apostille Convention, as is Paraguay: <Fact k="apostille.since" />. The apostille issued by the Ministry of External Affairs (MEA) replaces embassy legalisation, so you don't need the Paraguayan embassy to legalise your documents.

What varies is the step before the MEA. Documents issued centrally, such as a Passport Seva PCC, usually go to the MEA directly. Civil records such as birth and marriage certificates, issued by municipal or state authorities, usually need authentication by the state government first. The MEA handles apostille requests through authorised collection agencies; use the MEA's own list rather than an agent who claims special access. Check the route for each document before you send it anywhere, because a missing state-level step means starting that document again.

## Names, dates and spellings

Indian documents often record names differently from a passport: a single given name, a father's name used as a surname, initials, or spellings that changed between records. Paraguayan officials compare every document against your passport. Before anything is apostilled, line up the name and date of birth on each document with your passport, and gather the supporting evidence (a gazette notification, an affidavit or a marriage certificate) for any difference you can't correct at source. It is far easier to fix at the start than after the translations are done.

## Translation in Paraguay

Translate after the apostille, never before, and use a sworn translator recognised in Paraguay rather than one in India. Our [apostille and translation guide](/guides/documents/apostilles-and-translations) explains why the order matters. Most Indian certificates carry English text, which Paraguayan translators work from routinely. If any document is only in Hindi or another Indian language, tell us early, because a translator for that language pair may take longer to arrange.

## Proof of means

Proof of means is <Fact k="solvency.requirement" />, so the evidence depends on your situation: salary slips and an employment letter, business records, income tax returns, pension papers or bank statements. Documents issued in India need the same apostille and translation treatment as your civil records. Tell us which category you fall into before you gather anything, so the evidence matches it.
